# The panel tabs

The collapsible tabs of the panel: how they are laid out, Completeness, Record trees, Error ranking and Trends. The entries of the Legend tab are described in [The map](mappa.md#rendering).

## Layout

The collapsible tabs of the panel (Legend, Error ranking, Trends, Completeness, Record trees) open one at a time. The closed tabs sit in two columns of equal width, two per row; when their number is odd, the last one is alone on its row and takes the whole row, and the open tab is below them, full width. Opening a closed tab sends the open one back to its place in the grid; tapping the open tab closes it, and all go back to the grid. The same applies to the monumental trees map, which has Legend, Completeness and Record trees. The legend list, the ranking list, the Completeness tab and the Record trees tab are as tall as the space left in the window (recalculated when a tab opens, the window is resized or the box on top changes): if the entries do not fit, only the list scrolls. If the panel is still taller than the window, the panel scrolls and not the page

## Completeness

The "Completeness" tab ([issue #31](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/31)): how many trees have the main data. The checks report wrong values, this tab the missing ones. It counts the trees in view, the same set as the "Trees in view" count: the trees inside the map on screen, at the current zoom (below zoom 10 only trees with errors and, from zoom 9, monumental trees: a note says so) and with the genus/species and error filters applied; stumps are not counted. It is recalculated when the map moves or a filter changes, and only while the tab is open. One bar from 0 to 100% per entry, with the percentage and the absolute numbers ("38% · 1,204 of 3,170"), in two groups: "Basics" (Species or genus `species=*` or `genus=*`, [issue #73](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/73): a tree with only the genus counts too; Leaf type `leaf_type=*`, Leaf cycle `leaf_cycle=*`) and "Measurements and photo", marked as extra (Height `height=*`, Circumference `circumference=*`, Crown diameter `diameter_crown=*`, Photo). Below them, the "Dioecious trees" group ([issue #58](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/58)) has the Sex entry (`sex=*`), which counts only the trees in view of dioecious taxa, with male and female flowers on separate trees: those whose `genus=*`, or the first word of `species=*`, is one of the genera Broussonetia, Cephalotaxus, Cercidiphyllum, Chamaerops, Ginkgo, Gymnocladus, Hippophae, Ilex, Laurus, Maclura, Phellodendron, Phoenix, Pistacia, Podocarpus, Populus, Salix, Taxus, Torreya, Trachycarpus, and those whose `species=*` is (also followed by an author or a cultivar) Acer negundo, Ailanthus altissima, Araucaria araucana, Ceratonia siliqua, Diospyros lotus, Diospyros virginiana, Juniperus communis, Juniperus oxycedrus, Juniperus virginiana, Phytolacca dioica or Schinus molle (`DIO_GENERA` and `DIO_SPECIES` in `index.html`). The group appears only when there is at least one of these trees in view, and its highlight marks only them ("Highlighted: dioecious, no sex=* ×"); it is in the monumental trees map too, below "Optional". A tree counts when the tag is present, whatever its value; for Circumference a tree with `diameter=*` counts too, because the trunk is drawn from it. Photo counts with at least one of `wikimedia_commons` (file or category), `panoramax`, `image:fotoshi`, `image` (an http address) or the photo (P18) of the linked Wikidata item that the card shows. The ⓘ next to each entry (mouse over or tap) says which tag to use, with the link to the OSM wiki, and how the value is surveyed (for example the circumference is measured 1.30 m above the ground), and adds that values should be surveyed on the ground or taken from a source, not estimated. Tapping a row highlights on the map in magenta (following the crown shape from zoom 16) the trees that lack that value; the row turns magenta and a magenta notice appears in the box on top ("Highlighted: no height ×"), also when the tab is closed: tapping the notice, or the row again, removes the highlight. One highlight at a time. With no region chosen, while the trees are loading or with no trees in view, the tab says so instead of showing the bars. The texts are in `cp` in the `I18N` object of `index.html`, the entries in `COMP`

## Record trees

The "Record trees" tab ([issue #15](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/15)): for eight records (six on the monumental trees map, without Highest elevation and Most isolated; Highest elevation only where the trees have an elevation), the tree with the best value and, on request, the top ten ([issue #48](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/48)). With a tie all the tied trees are shown (the first 10, then "and N more tied").

* **Tallest**: the highest value of `height=*`
* **Thickest trunk**: the largest trunk circumference, from `circumference=*`; if that tag is missing, from `diameter=*` multiplied by π (`diameter` without a unit is in millimetres, whatever its value). When the value comes from `diameter`, the row shows the diameter (e.g. "⌀ 1,200 mm"). The values of `multi_stemmed=*` are not used
* **Most slender** and **Stockiest**: the highest and the lowest ratio between the height and the trunk diameter (`diameter=*`, or `circumference=*` divided by π), only for trees with both measurements and at least 2 m tall. The row shows the ratio (e.g. "85×") and, below, the two measurements
* **Widest crown**: the highest value of `diameter_crown=*`
* **Oldest**: the oldest date of `start_date=*` or, for trees without it, of `planted_date=*` ([issue #81](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/81); with both tags `start_date` counts). The forms read are a year of three or four digits, `YYYY-MM`, `YYYY-MM-DD`, a decade (`1850s`), a century (`C16`, also `early`, `mid` or `late C16`), years BC (`500 BC`, `500 BCE`), each also preceded by `~`, `before` or `after`, and ranges (`1500..1600`). Approximate dates count with the oldest year they indicate: `~1500` and `before 1500` count as 1500, `1850s` as 1850, `C16` as 1500 (`mid C16` 1533, `late C16` 1566), `1500..1600` as 1500. Dates of the same year are tied (`1850-05-01`, `1850`, `~1850` and `1850s` are one position). The row shows the date as written on OSM. Other forms (`c. 1850`, `1850;1900`, `12.05.1900`) are not read
* **Highest elevation**: the highest elevation above sea level of the tree's position (tree stumps are not ranked). It is not a tag: it comes from the Elevation API of [overpass.ch](https://overpass.ch), during the update of the region, only for the trees that have one, see [Elevation](dati.md#elevation). Outside Switzerland the model is Copernicus GLO-30, a 30 m grid of the surface that includes canopies and buildings, so in a wood the value can be a few tens of metres above the ground; in Switzerland swissALTI3D, the ground on a 2 m grid. The row shows the elevation rounded to the metre. It follows the filters like the other records. In the regions without elevations, and at a level with none of them, the record is not shown
* **Most isolated**: the largest distance from the nearest of the other trees (`natural=tree`), woods (`natural=wood`, `landuse=forest`) and tree rows (`natural=tree_row`) on OpenStreetMap, also outside the region (a tree near the border can have its nearest tree in the next region). Tree stumps are not ranked. The row shows the distance (in metres below 1 km) and, below, what is nearest: a tree, a wood, a forest or a tree row. When nothing is within 50 km the row shows "over 50 km". This record is not calculated from the tags of the tree but with [QLever](https://qlever.dev/osm-planet), on QLever's copy of OpenStreetMap, which can be a few days behind the region's data; it is computed locally after the update of the region, because QLever refuses the requests of the GitHub Actions, see [Data and updates](dati.md#most-isolated). It is always the same list, also with a region loaded: it does not follow the genus/species and error filters, and a note says so when a filter is active. A region for which it has not been computed yet shows "Not computed yet for this region". The species pages (`species/`) have the same record for the trees of their species, still measured from trees of any species

### Top ten

To the right of the name of each record a small triangle points right; pressed, it points down and the top ten list opens below the record. Opening one closes the one opened before, like the tabs; when the region or the filters change, the open record stays open and its list is recalculated. The list shows whole positions until it reaches ten trees; if a tied position does not fit in the room left, as many trees as fit are shown, followed by "and N more tied" (1 tree + 9 tied = 10, and the list ends there; 1 + 15 tied = 1 + 9 and "and 6 more tied"). From the second position on, each row starts with its position; tied trees have the same one. Order inside a tie: first the values reported by a check (⚠); for Oldest, then the most precise date (day, month, year, then `~`/`before`/`after`/range, decade, century) and, with the same precision, the oldest; finally the node id, so the order is always the same. Values are tied when they are equal in the first 9 significant digits (`recTop` in `index.html`, `record_order` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`: the two must stay the same).

### Top three compared

For Tallest, Thickest trunk and Widest crown. For Tallest, between the name and the triangle, the same icon that opens "Trunk comparison" on the card opens that window with the first three trees of the list, in list order, to scale and side by side, each numbered, with height and trunk (two trees if only two have a height; with one the icon is not there). A tree without a mapped circumference has the trunk estimated for the map (see [Height bands for the estimated trunk](dati.md#height-bands-for-the-estimated-trunk)), drawn lighter with a dotted outline and marked "⌀ ~"; if there is no estimate either (for example a height above the limit), only the height is drawn, as a dashed line marked "⌀ ?". A note below the drawing explains both. Values reported by a check have a ⚠, also when they dwarf the others (`height=300`). The references (person, house, Tower of Pisa, and the width reference behind the thickest trunk) follow the rules of the comparison on the card, and the trunks have the colour of the species. For Thickest trunk and Widest crown the icon has two concentric circles ([issue #60](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/60)) and the first three trees are drawn seen from above, to scale, one inside the other on the same centre, each numbered on its edge and with its own outline (solid, dashed, dotted): trunks as circles with the colour of the species, crowns with the outline used on the map (lobed for broadleaved, star-shaped for needleleaved, with fronds for palms, from `leaf_type` or estimated from the species; a circle if the type is not known), with the same area as the circle of `diameter_crown`. Next to them, the same reference seen from above as in "Trunk from above" on the card, chosen on the first tree, with a sentence comparing its diameter with the length of the reference. Values reported by a check have a ⚠.

### What happened that year

For Oldest only. Each row of Oldest has a calendar button that shows, below the row, a sentence about the year ("In 1969, humans land on the Moon"), with a serif "W" that links to its source on Wikipedia (for a year without its own sentence, the sentence of its decade); the button does not open the card. The sentence is about the year, not about the tree: `start_date` does not necessarily say when the tree was planted. The year is that of the date used by Oldest (`start_date` or `planted_date`). The sentences are in `scripts/year_events.json`, one entry per year (not per tree), in Italian and English; approximate dates use the entry of the year they name (`~1850`, `before 1850`, `1850..1900` → `1850`), of the decade (`1850s`) or of the century (`C16`, also for `early`/`mid`/`late`), years BC their own (`480 BC`). If the year has no entry the button is not there. The events are known worldwide and the same for every country: discoveries, inventions, feats, culture, wars and other historical events. An entry can also be written for a single country (`JP:1159`, the birth of Minamoto no Yoshitsune) or a single region (`IT-21:C16`, the Shroud of Turin moved to Turin, for Piedmont): for the trees of that region the region's entry comes first, then the country's, then the entry for every country, both for the year and for the decade. The region is the tree's region, so the monumental trees map, which has no regions, shows only the entries for every country. The summary of "Update tree data" lists the years in the top ten of Oldest of some region that have no entry yet, see [Data and updates](dati.md). The species pages (`species/`) have the same button in their Oldest box, next to the first tree and to each tree of the top five, with the sentence below the tree (one sentence open at a time); the monumental trees outside the regions get only the entries for every country. The sentences are copied into the page when it is made, so a new entry appears there after the page is made again.

### Values and checks

Values with a unit (`150 cm`, `30 ft`, `7'4"`) are converted and shown in the site's unit (metres; millimetres for `diameter`). Values that are not readable, zero or negative do not count. Values reported by a check count too: the row then has a ⚠, and hovering over it with the mouse (or tapping it) names the check. The checks considered are those on the value used: "Height > 116.22 m" and the species height limit for the height; "Circumference > 43 m", the species circumference limit, "circumference and diameter do not match" (or, when the value comes from `diameter`, "diameter without unit too small for millimetres") for the trunk; the three checks on the height/circumference ratio for height, trunk and ratio; "Crown diameter > 60.4 m" for the crown; and, for the tags used, "Decimal comma", "Unit written in a non-standard form" and "Tag written in the value (=)". Hovering over the name of a record (or tapping it) explains how it is calculated: the tags the value comes from and, for the two ratios, the 2 m threshold.

### Rows

Each row has the name of the tree (as in the card title: `name`, or the common name of the species, or the species), the value and, when the records cover more than one region, the region and the country. A click on a row opens the card of the tree and centres the map on it at zoom 19, like the Z key; if the tree is in another region, that region is loaded first.

### Levels

In the QA tool the tab follows the open level, and the subtitle says which one, with the date of the latest data update (two dates when the regions were updated on different days). With no country chosen: all the regions loaded on the site. With a country open: its regions. With a region loaded: all the trees of the region, not only those in view. The records cover only the regions loaded on the site, and the subtitle says so. Without a loaded region the records come from `data/records.json`, see [Data and updates](dati.md), requested the first time the tab is opened; with a region loaded they are calculated from the trees of the region, and follow the genus/species and error filters: choosing a species shows the records of that species in the region. Without a loaded region the two filter menus are greyed out, so there the records are always those of all the trees. The top ten of a country or of all the regions is built from the top ten of each region: a region whose data was written before the top ten existed has only its first position, and a note says for how many regions the list is not complete yet (it arrives with their next update). On the monumental trees map the records and the lists cover the trees in view.

In the monumental trees map the records cover the trees in view, like the Completeness tab, with the genus/species filter, and are recalculated when the map moves, only while the tab is open. Below zoom 7, where the map shows only some of the trees, the tab shows a notice that invites to zoom in instead of the list. The trees of the MASAF list not yet on OSM are not counted; the members of [homogeneous groups](mappa.md#homogeneous-groups-of-the-masaf-list) of the MASAF list are.

When a filter is active, a yellow notice in the tab names it ("Active filter: Quercus robur"). If no tree has the measurement of a record, or the data is still loading, the tab says so. The texts are in `rc` in the `I18N` object of `index.html`, the rules in `recValues` (`index.html`) and `record_values` (`scripts/fetch_trees.py`), which must stay the same

## Error ranking

The "Error ranking" tab: countries in order of share of trees with at least one error and, inside each country, the regions; tapping a region opens it. The counts are in the `errors` field of `data/index.json` and are filled in when the data is updated. The `%` and `#` buttons next to the title, visible only when the tab is open, sort by percentage (the default) or by number of trees with errors; the choice is saved in the browser. Numbers are coloured according to position, with the same scale as the map (from green, last place, to orange up to red, first place): countries among themselves and the regions of each country among themselves; rows without counts are grey. Next to each row, the position compared with 7 days before the latest date in `data/history.json` (for each region the last value recorded up to that day; if the history is shorter, the oldest date; `RANK_DAYS` in `index.html`), so a movement stays visible for a week even if in the meantime the data is updated again without changing: red ▲ if it goes up towards first place (more errors than the others), green ▼ if it goes down, grey = if it stays the same; nothing if the region has no previous value. Positions are compared only among rows that have both the current and the previous value: a newly added country or region does not push down those below it. The position can also change without the region's data changing, when the others are updated

## Trends

The "Trends" tab: two charts over time, for the loaded region, for the open country or for all regions (the subtitle says which). The title of the closed tab is "Trends"; when it is opened it becomes "Error trend", and the ⇄ icon next to the title, visible only when the tab is open, switches to "Tree trend" and back. Reopening the tab always starts from "Error trend".

* **Error trend**: the share of trees with at least one error. When a check is added the share goes up even if the data has not changed
* **Tree trend**: the number of trees (stumps included), useful for example to spot imports. For a country or for all regions, the chart starts from the first date whose number of trees is at least 5% of the highest one: the earlier dates, with few regions, would be drawn at zero

The values come from `data/history.json`; for a country or for all regions, at each date the last known value of each region is summed. The vertical axis narrows around the values (for example from 0.1% to 0.3% if the share is 0.2%). For a country or for all regions, a dashed vertical line marks the dates on which regions were added (their names are in the tooltip of the line): from that date their trees are added to the sum. The box appears only with at least two dates
