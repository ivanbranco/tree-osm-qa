# QA checks

The checks DendrOSM runs on every tree, with their thresholds and where they come from.

## QA checks

Trees with at least one error have a red outline. The checks are grouped as in the error menu of the map.

### Measurements

* Circumference greater than 43 m: the largest recorded, in African baobabs (*Adansonia digitata*), according to [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/living-tree-with-greatest-girth)
* Height greater than 116.22 m: the tallest measured tree, [Hyperion](https://www.monumentaltrees.com/en/usa/california/delnortecounty/4462_redwoodnationalpark/9104/) (*Sequoia sempervirens*, Redwood National Park, California)
* `diameter_crown=*` greater than 60.4 m: the widest crown of a living single-trunk tree, a [Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/635581-widest-crown-on-a-living-tree-single-trunk) record (*Samanea saman* "Chamchuri", Thailand, 2018). Banyans (*Ficus benghalensis*), which have many trunks, can exceed the threshold
* Circumference and height greater than the record of the single species. The records are documented on the wiki page [Tree species records](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/Tree_species_records) and come from monumentaltrees.com, except some taken from MASAF: the heights of *Cedrus atlantica*, *Celtis australis*, *Morus alba*, *Pinus pinea*, *Platanus × hispanica*, *Quercus ilex* and *Quercus pubescens*, and the circumferences of *Cedrus atlantica*, *Magnolia grandiflora*, *Quercus ilex* and *Quercus pubescens*. A species can have only one of the two limits: without a circumference record, or with only a lower bound (*Ficus macrophylla*, "more than 25 m"), only the height limit; *Betula platyphylla* only the circumference limit, because the height record on monumentaltrees.com (15.60 m) is lower than most of the heights mapped in Japan (median 18 m on 67 trees, November 2026). The limits are copied into `scripts/species_limits.json`, where `height_source` or `circumference_source` indicate a source other than monumentaltrees.com, shown on the card; the comparison uses genus and epithet, so a cultivar, an author or a rank after them uses the limits of the species (`Pyrus calleryana 'Chanticleer'` those of `Pyrus calleryana`; not a hybrid written with both parents, such as `Salix alba × fragilis`); the same holds for the synonyms in `scripts/whitelist.json` and the other spellings of their names (`x` instead of `×`), which use the limits of the name in the wiki list (for example `Platanus acerifolia` and `Platanus x hispanica` use those of `Platanus × hispanica`) and applies only if the generic check has not already been triggered
* Exaggerated height/circumference ratio: "tree too slender" if `height` is more than 100 times the circumference (e.g. `height=24`, `circumference=0.01`), "tree too stocky" if the circumference is more than 3 times `height`. The thresholds are deliberately loose, to avoid false positives: the stockiest real trees, such as monumental olive trees, are around 0.5. It does not apply if the circumference (for stocky) or the height (for slender) is already reported as over the limits, nor to stumps. The thresholds are `SLENDER_MAX` and `STOCKY_MIN` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* Tree too slender for its species: for species with a typical height/circumference ratio (`height_circ_ratio` in `scripts/species_limits.json`, the median of the OSM data on the wiki page [Tree species records](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/Tree_species_records), copied only when it comes from at least 30 trees), the tree's ratio exceeds 4 times that value and in any case 40. It applies only to trees at least 10 m tall, because newly planted saplings are thin; it does not apply if the height is already reported or if the generic check already applies. Tested on 59,169 trees, it reported 81. The thresholds are `SLENDER_SP_FACTOR`, `SLENDER_SP_MIN` and `SLENDER_SP_MIN_H` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* For "tree too slender", "tree too slender for its species" and "tree too stocky" the small icon next to the height on the card (two trunks and a person), or the **C** key, opens a scale drawing of the trunk next to the typical trunk of a tree of the same height and to references such as a person and a house ([issue #14](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/14)): see [Trunk comparison](scheda.md#trunk-comparison). The typical trunk uses the species ratio (`height_circ_ratio`) for "tree too slender for its species" and the median of all OSM data with both measurements mapped (12: 11.8 on 368,958 trees of the regions in November 2026, `TYPICAL_HC_RATIO` in `index.html`, the same value as `_default` in `scripts/species_limits.json`) for the other two
* Measurements too large for the date: the circumference (also from `diameter=*`) is more than 4 m plus 0.3 m for each year since `start_date=*` or `planted_date=*`, or `height=*` is more than 20 m plus 2.5 m per year (years = current year minus the year of the date; `start_date=2023` with `circumference=7.8` in 2026: 3 years, limit 4.9 m). The bases allow for large transplanted trees and the growth rates are those of the fastest species (0.3 m of circumference a year is about 9.5 cm of diameter): the thresholds are deliberately loose, to avoid false positives, and in practice the check applies only to recent dates. The older of the two dates counts; decades, centuries and ranges count from their first year. It does not apply to dates with `~` or `before`, to unreadable or future dates (already reported, see [Other tags](#other-tags)), to palms and palm-like plants (transplanted when already large, and their trunk does not widen), to stumps (the date can be the felling date) or to the circumference of olive trees (transplanted even with very thick trunks), nor to a measurement already reported by another check (over the limits, tree too stocky or too slender, comma, unit). Tested in November 2026 on 108,143 trees with a date and a measurement: it reported 12, almost all monumental trees. The thresholds are `YOUNG_CIRC_BASE`, `YOUNG_CIRC_RATE`, `YOUNG_HEIGHT_BASE` and `YOUNG_HEIGHT_RATE` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`. Only with data from the region update after this change
* Very small monumental tree ([issue #34](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/34)): a tree with `denotation=natural_monument`, a monumental source (the same sources as the check on `denotation=natural_monument`, below) or that is a member of a relation with `ref:masaf` (a [homogeneous group](mappa.md#homogeneous-groups-of-the-masaf-list) of the MASAF list) that is less than 1.5 m tall or has a circumference (also from `diameter=*`) under 20 cm, the size of a small plant or a bamboo cane. The thresholds are deliberately low, to avoid false positives: in November 2026, out of 58,855 trees with `denotation=natural_monument`, 14 were under 1.5 m and 28 under 20 cm. A measurement is not reported again if the height/circumference ratio already reports it (the circumference for "tree too slender", the height for "tree too stocky"), and stumps are excluded. Monumental trees that really are this small go in `small_monument` in `scripts/whitelist.json` (node id → note). The thresholds are `SMALL_MON_HEIGHT` and `SMALL_MON_CIRC` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* Stump with a crown: `diameter_crown=*` on a `natural=tree_stump`. A stump is what is left of a felled tree and has no crown; "Fix with JOSM" removes `diameter_crown`. Height is not reported: a tree felled high up is still a stump. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `circumference=*`, `height=*`, `diameter_crown=*` or `diameter=*` with a comma as decimal separator (`2,9` instead of `2.9`, also with a unit: `2,9 m`). Only when the value is a number: lists and other text with commas (`10, 12`, `66,60,51`, `14 x 16,30`) are reported only as values that are not a number (below). "Fix with JOSM" writes the point, except with exactly three digits after the comma and a number from 1 to 999 before it (`1,200`), which can mean 1.2 or 1200: such a value is not used to draw the tree, for the other checks or for the records (`0,250` is used: 0.25)
* `diameter=*` without a unit and less than 10 (e.g. `diameter=0.5`): without a unit the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:diameter) indicates millimetres, and it would be a trunk under 1 cm. If the value is in metres the unit must be written (`diameter=0.5 m`). These values are not used for the circumference; the check applies even if `circumference` is present. The threshold is `DIAM_MM_MIN` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `circumference=*`, `height=*`, `diameter_crown=*` or `diameter=*` with a unit written in a non-standard form: uppercase or spelled out (`2 M`, `8 metres`, `12 mt`), without the space (`150cm`, `2m`), or feet and inches written with letters (`30 ft`, `5 in`, `7 ft 4 in`, `7 feet 4 inches`) or with other quote marks than `'` and `"` (`7′4″`, `7’4”`, `7'4''`), for which `30'`, `5"` and `7'4"` are proposed. This is aligned with [Osmose](https://osmose.openstreetmap.fr/), which also reports `30 ft` (proposing `30'`) and a unit without the space (`2m`, proposing `2 m`). Spaces alone (`7' 4"`) are a hint, not an error (see below). When there is a single [standard form](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Map_features/Units) the card proposes it (`150 cm`, `30'`); for metres, which can also be written without a unit (`8` or `8 m`; for `diameter`, which without a unit is in millimetres, `2000` or `2 m`), the card does not choose between the two forms, and "Fix with JOSM" writes the one without a unit (`circumference=4.8m` → `4.8`, `diameter=2 M` → `2000`). These values are still read and used to draw the tree (`parse_length` and `unit_fixes` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`)
* Overlapping trunks: the distance between two trees is less than the sum of the radii of their trunks. Only measured trunks count (from `circumference` or `diameter`, not estimated ones) that are not already reported as too large; a tree without a measured trunk counts as a point, and is reported (with the other) if it falls inside the trunk of another tree. Stumps and trees at the same point do not count. If they are the trunks of a single tree, only one node with `multi_stemmed=*` should be mapped. This check and "Trees at the same point" (in [Other tags](#other-tags)) compare nearby trees, so they are computed after the single-tree ones (`neighbour_checks` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`)
* `circumference=*` and `diameter=*` both present but not matching: the circumference differs by more than 20% from π × diameter (with `diameter` without a unit in millimetres). The margin lets measurement inaccuracies through, such as non-round trunks. The threshold is `CIRC_DIAM_TOL` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `circumference=*`, `height=*`, `diameter_crown=*` or `diameter=*` with text, ranges, lists, unknown units or values of 0 (or negative). These values are not used to draw the tree. Values with known units (`150 cm`, `7'4"`) are not reported here and are converted to metres; a number that starts with the point (`.75`) is read as 0.75 and gets a hint (see below)

### Species and genus name

* `species=*` possibly misspelled: it is not in the species list, but is very close to it (e.g. `Olea europeea` → `Olea europaea`). Genus and epithet are compared, without the hybrid sign `×` and without cultivar or author: with a one-letter difference it is always reported, with two only if the written genus is not a known genus (so `Myrtus communis` does not become `Pyrus communis`). A species of the list in the same genus with the same letters in another order is proposed before the closest one (`Prunus serratula` → `Prunus serrulata`, not `Prunus serrula`). True species that are not in the list but look similar are excluded in `scripts/whitelist.json` (`species_typo`, e.g. the hybrid `Prunus × incam`, also with a cultivar: `Prunus × incam 'Okame'`); a name that is at least as close to one of them as to any species of the list is not reported either (`Quercus myrsinfolia` is one letter from `Quercus myrsinifolia`, in the whitelist, and two from `Quercus myrtifolia`, in the list). Nor is a name equally close to two species of the list that are not synonyms (`Pirus piraster` is two letters from both `Pyrus pyraster` and `Pinus pinaster`), nor a name whose closest species has an epithet with another initial when a species of the same genus with the same initial is only one letter further (`Quercus ruber` is one letter from `Quercus suber` and two from `Quercus robur` and `Quercus rubra`). A genus followed only by a cultivar (`Magnolia 'Susan'`, `Magnolia x 'Vulcan'`) is not compared: the cultivar is not an epithet. Nor is an uppercase second word of five letters or fewer that differs by two letters from an epithet of the same genus (`Magnolia Susan` does not become `Magnolia yulan`). "Fix with JOSM" writes the proposed name in `species` when `species`, without the author, is only genus and epithet (`Chamaecyparis lawsonianna` → `Chamaecyparis lawsoniana`; not with a cultivar or a rank, which the fix would lose), and adds the missing `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` of that species. With the author too (`Cupréssus sempervìrens L.`) the fix of "Species with the author name" writes the same name (`Cupressus sempervirens`), so the two fixes agree. Only with data from the region update after this change
* Species name in a language (`species:it=*`, `species:pl=*`, `species:en=*`…) without `species=*`, i.e. without the scientific name. 2-3 letter language codes are valid, also with a variant (`species:zh-Hans`): two-letter codes must be ISO 639-1 languages (`species:mp` and `species:at` are not), three-letter ones are all accepted except `ref`, `lat`, `src`, `alt` and `old` (`LANG_2` and `NOT_LANG_3` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`); `species:wikidata` and `species:wikipedia` do not count. If the value is the name of a genus in the same language in the `genus:en` and `genus:fr` columns of the [OSM wiki list of genera](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/List_of_Genus,_Leaf_cycle,_Leaf_type) (case-insensitive; a variant such as `species:en-GB` counts as `en`), the message suggests the genus key instead: `species:fr=Tilleul` → `genus:fr=Tilleul`, plus `genus=Tilia` if `genus` is missing. Only names with no doubt are used: a name-genus pair must also be in `genus_names` in `scripts/whitelist.json`, so a name added to the wiki gives no suggestion until it is added there too. * Name of the taxon in a language (`taxon:en=*`, `taxon:de=*`…) without `taxon=*`: `taxon:xx` goes alongside `taxon`, not instead of it. Language codes as for `species:xx`; `taxon:cultivar` and similar keys are not languages. With the same name tables as for `species:xx`, the message suggests a key for the name: the name of a genus goes in `genus:xx` (with a capital initial, without `sp.`), plus `genus=*` if both `genus` and `species` are missing (`taxon:it=pino` → `genus:it=Pino` and `genus=Pinus`); the name of a species goes in `species:xx`, plus `species=*` if it is missing. No suggestion for a name that is not in the tables, even when `species=*` is present: it can also be the name of a cultivar (`species=Malus domestica` with `taxon:it=Runsè`). No suggestion for a name whose `genus:xx` or `species:xx` already has another value, and none at all if the names point to different genera or species, or to a genus or species other than `genus` and `species`. * Plant that is not a tree: a `natural=tree` whose `species=*` (genus and epithet, also with author or cultivar) or `species:wikidata=*` indicates a plant that is not a tree, now only the century plant (`Agave americana`, `species:wikidata=Q161115`); one of the two tags is enough. The same for a genus in which no species is a tree or a shrub, given by `genus=*` or by the first word of `species=*` (`genus=Agave`, `species=Agave franzosinii`): now *Actinidia*, *Agapanthus*, *Agave*, *Arundo*, *Carpobrotus*, *Echinocactus*, *Hemerocallis*, *Musa*, *Nelumbo* and *Nymphaea*. Plants that are not trees are mapped with `natural=plant`, which "Fix with JOSM" proposes. Shrubs are reported in the same way, with `natural=shrub` in place of `natural=plant`: the species *Juniperus horizontalis* and the genera with shrubs only, *Abelia*, *Cistus*, *Forsythia*, *Nandina*, *Philadelphus*, *Pyracantha*, *Ribes*, *Rosa* and *Rosmarinus* (`species=Rosa canina` → `natural=shrub`). Without `species` and `genus`, the check also uses the species or the genus given by the names in `species:xx` (see "species:xx without species"): `species:it=Rosa canina` or `species:it=Canna domestica` (*Arundo donax*). Only plants with no doubt are listed: genera with even one species that can be a small tree (*Hydrangea*, *Buddleja*, *Cotoneaster*, *Erica*, *Hibiscus*) or that looks like a tree (*Opuntia*, *Euphorbia*, *Yucca*, *Aloe*, *Carnegiea*) are left out, as are *Hedera*, *Vitis* and *Wisteria*, which MASAF lists among the monumental trees. Stumps are excluded. The plants are in `not_trees` in `scripts/whitelist.json` (species → QID), the genera in `not_tree_genera`; the shrubs in `shrubs` and `shrub_genera`. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `species=*` with wrong capitalisation: an uppercase epithet (`Catalpa Bignonioides`, `Taxus Baccata`), an uppercase letter inside the epithet (`Ginkgo biloBa`) or inside the genus after the initial (`GINKGO biloba`, `GinKgo biloba`), or a hybrid sign written as an uppercase `X` between genus and epithet (`Crataegus X prunifolia` → `Crataegus × prunifolia`; a lowercase `x` and an `X` at the start of the name are not reported). The name with only the initial of the genus uppercase is suggested. With an uppercase epithet initial it is genus and epithet as in the species list, and what follows the epithet stays as written (`Pyrus Communis 'Beech Hill'` → `Pyrus communis 'Beech Hill'`); for two-word names with a known genus that are not in the list, it is the same words in lowercase with the genus initial capitalised, only when the second word is the epithet of a species in the list, of any genus (`Prunus Chinensis` → `Prunus chinensis`), when the name is one or two letters away from a species in the list (see "Species possibly misspelled"), or when the whole name is uppercase (`PRUNUS COMUNIS` → `Prunus comunis`); the other two-word names (`Malus Zierform`, `Tilia Greenspire`) are reported as "Non-scientific species", without a suggestion. In the other cases only for names in the species list and two-word names with a known genus: the suggestion fixes genus and epithet and keeps the rest (cultivar) as written. Cultivars in quotes do not count. The suggestion also capitalises the genus initial, so with this error "Species starting lowercase" is not repeated (`quercus Robur`). With an author in the name (`Ginkgo biloBa L.`, `quercus robur L.`) only "Species with the author name" is reported, and its suggestion also fixes the capitalisation (`Ginkgo biloba`, `Quercus robur`), so the two suggestions never disagree. When the name also ends with the hybrid sign and the species list has it as a hybrid, the suggestion puts the sign in its place (`Platanus Acerifolia x` → `Platanus × acerifolia`, without "Hybrid sign at the end of species")
* Hybrid sign at the end of `species=*`: genus and epithet followed by the sign as a separate word, `×`, `x` or `X` (`Platanus acerifolia x`, `Tilia euchlora x`; issue [#100](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/100)). In the name of a hybrid the sign goes between the genus and the epithet (`Platanus × acerifolia`), or before the genus for a hybrid between different genera (`× Chitalpa tashkentensis`). If the species list has that genus and epithet as a hybrid, the card suggests the name of the list, which "Fix with JOSM" writes (`Platanus acerifolia x` → `Platanus × acerifolia`); otherwise (`Salix alba x`) the error has no suggestion. If genus and epithet are also misspelled and the species proposed for them is a hybrid of the list, "Fix with JOSM" writes that species (`Prunus yedonensis x` → `Prunus × yedoensis`, see "Species possibly misspelled"). A sign in the middle of the name (`Salix alba × fragilis`) and names with something after the sign (`Platanus acerifolia x 'Tremonia'`) are not reported. With the suggestion, which already capitalises the genus, "Species starting lowercase" is not repeated (`platanus acerifolia x`)
* `species=*` that does not have the form of a scientific name (Genus epithet) because the second word is uppercase: common names (`Pino Marittimo`, `Olive Tree`, `Black Matipo`) or cultivars without quotes (`Malus Golden Hornet`, `Tilia Greenspire`). Names for which "Species with wrong capitalisation" has a suggestion are reported there instead. If the scientific name is written in parentheses (`Black Matipo (Pittosporum tenuifolium)`) the card suggests it. If the whole value is the name of a species in `species_names` of `scripts/whitelist.json` (see "species:xx without species", in any case and any language of the list, for one species only), the card suggests that species and moves the common name to `species:xx` of its language if that tag is missing: `species=American Chestnut` → `species=Castanea dentata`, `species:en=American Chestnut`; "Fix with JOSM" writes them, with the missing `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` of the species (no suggestion if `genus` is another genus; only with data from the region update after this change). Otherwise, if the whole value is the common name of a genus (see "Non-scientific genus") or a common name whose genus is certain even though it can also mean a species (`Horse Chestnut`: *Aesculus*, list `genus_of_names` in `scripts/whitelist.json`), the card suggests `genus=*` without `species`, and "Fix with JOSM" removes `species` and adds `genus` if it is missing (no fix if `genus` is another genus); a common name of a genus in one language only also moves to `genus:xx` of that language, if missing (not the names of `genus_of_names`). Cultivars in quotes and authors do not count. Also reported, with the same suggestions, are common names with a lowercase second word, when the first word is not a known genus and the value is the name of a species of `species_names` (`Klon jawor`), a word is the common name of a genus (`London plane`, `Micocoulier de Provence`), a word is one that scientific names do not have (`de`, `del`, `of`, `the`, `non`, `tree`…: `Non replantation définitive`) or a word after the first has non-ASCII letters (`Ciprés común`; not the diaeresis, `ë`, `ï`). Not when the second word, without accents, is the epithet of a species in the list (`Betulla pendula`, `Cupréssus sempervìrens L.`: a misspelled genus or the common name of the genus followed by the epithet), nor with the author of the name (`Insieme omogeneo di Fagus sylvatica L.`: "Species with the author name"). Real genera that are missing from the wiki lists (`Embothrium coccineum`, `Grevillea robusta`) are not reported. Only with data from the region update after this change
* Parentheses in `species=*`: no scientific name contains them. Not repeated if the parentheses are already explained by the checks on the author or on the non-scientific name
* Species with the genus abbreviated: `species=*` that starts with the initial of the genus and a dot, followed by a lowercase epithet (`P. avium`, `T. baccata 'Fastigiata'`), or by an epithet with capital letters that is the epithet of a species of the list (`C. Siliqua`). The card suggests the name with the genus written in full and the epithet in lowercase, keeping the rest (`species=Prunus avium`, `species=Ceratonia siliqua`). The genus is that of `genus=*`, if it starts with that letter and is a known genus (or gives a species of the list with the epithet); without `genus`, the only genus of the wiki species list with that initial and a species with that epithet (`Z. serrata` → `Zelkova serrata`); otherwise (`C. japonica`, which has more than one) the error has no suggestion. "Fix with JOSM" writes the name and adds the missing `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` of that species; with a variety or a cultivar it writes the name without them, together with the hint below. With the author of the name, the suggestion is that of the check on the author. "Genus differs from species" is not reported when `genus=*` is the abbreviated genus (`species=T. cordata` with `genus=Tilia`)
* `species=*` indicating an unidentified species (`Pinus sp.`, `Pinus spp`): `genus=Pinus` without `species` should be used. After a known genus the same holds for `spec.` (abbreviation of *species*: `Ficus spec.`), `n. sp.` (`Tilia n. sp.`: in botany *nova species*, but in the data of Île-de-France it is used instead of `sp.`), `species` (`Prunus species`, `Pinus Species Unidentified`) and `unidentified` or `unknown` followed by at most one word (`Crataegus unidentified species`, `Prunus unidentified hybrid`, also cut off: `Chamaecyparis unidentified spe`); with these values "Non-scientific species" and "Species cut off at 30 characters" are not repeated. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `species=*` exactly 30 characters long that ends with the quotes of a cultivar opened and not closed (`Pyrus calleryana 'Chanticclere`) or with a single letter after a word (`Misc group of trees of mixed s`): the value of some imports is cut at 30 characters. A single letter after a number is a unit and does not count (`Latifoglia caducifoglia > 10 m`). No fix is proposed. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `species=*` or `genus=*` that does not indicate a species or a genus: a value without letters (`-`, `.`, `42`) or a word meaning "unknown", "species" or "none" (`Unknown`, `Desconocido`, `Inconnu`, `Essence`, `none`, `sp`), or a phrase describing the tree pit or the group rather than a taxon, found in the data of London (`New Tree Pit`, `Mistaken Tree Pit`, `Unplantable Tree Pit`, `Mixed Species`); the whole value counts, in any case (list `PLACEHOLDER_WORDS` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`). If the taxon is not known, the tag is left out: "Fix with JOSM" removes it. As with `=` and the non-Latin script, the checks on the form of the name are not repeated on that tag and `genus` is not compared with `species`. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `species=*` or `genus=*` meaning "stump" (`genus=Souche`, French; also `Stump`, `Tree stump`, `Ceppo`, `Tocón`, `Baumstumpf`): the whole value counts, in any case (list `STUMP_WORDS` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`). The stump of a felled tree is mapped with `natural=tree_stump`. On a `natural=tree` the card says so, and "Fix with JOSM" removes the tag, changes `natural=tree` to `natural=tree_stump` and removes `diameter_crown`, which a stump does not have; on a `natural=tree_stump` it only removes the tag. As for the previous check, the checks on the form of the name are not repeated on that tag and `genus` is not compared with `species`. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `species=*` with the author of the scientific name (`Phoenix dactylifera L.`, `Camellia sinensis (L.) Kuntze`, `Picea abies (L.) H. Karst.`): the card suggests the name without the author, keeping infraspecific ranks (`var.`, `subsp.`…) and cultivars in quotes. Author names without a dot count as the author when another author, a joining word (`et`, `ex`, `&`) or a rank follows (`Taxus cuspidata Siebold et Zucc.`, `Betula platyphylla Sukaczev var. japonica`), or after a rank in a name that already has an author (`Salix hultenii Flod. var. angustifolia Kimura`). Parentheses with lowercase words are not an author (`Ulmus laevis (Ulmus pedenculata, Ulmus effusa)`: parentheses in `species=*`). Uppercase words in parentheses are an author when they have an abbreviation with a dot or a joining word (`(L.)`, `(Siebold & Zucc.)`) or when another author follows (`Malus toringo (Siebold) Siebold ex de Vriese`); otherwise, at the end of the name, they are not (`Quercus robur (Farnia)`, `Hechtia sp. 10429 (Lunel)`: parentheses in `species=*`). If, without the author, something other than the scientific name is left (`Insieme omogeneo di Fagus sylvatica L.`), the card reports the author without a suggestion. "Fix with JOSM" writes the name without the author and adds the missing `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` of that species (`species=Quercus petraea (Matt.) Liebl.` → `species=Quercus petraea`, `species:wikidata=Q158608`; only with data from the region update after this change); if the name also has a spelling error, it writes the name proposed for it (see "Species possibly misspelled"). It applies only if the second word is lowercase, as in every scientific name, so as not to mistake common names for authors (`Black Matipo (Pittosporum tenuifolium)`); a hybrid sign attached to the epithet does not count (`Malus ×purpurea (E.Barbier) Rehder` → `Malus ×purpurea`)
* Underscore (`_`) in `species=*` or `genus=*` instead of a space
* Multiple values separated by `;` in `species=*` or `genus=*` (`genus=Crataegus;Quercus`): a `natural=tree` node is a single tree, with a single genus and a single species
* Tag written inside a value: `=` in `species`, `genus`, `taxon`, `name`, `start_date`, `planted_date`, `ref:masaf` or `ref:rami` (e.g. `genus=leaf_type=broadleaved`). Links (`image`, `website`, `url`) are not checked, because `=` is normal in addresses. With `=` in `species` or `genus` the checks on the form of the name are not repeated, since they would be triggered by chance
* `species=*` or `genus=*` in a non-Latin script (e.g. `genus=череша`): scientific names are written in the Latin script, the name in the local language goes in `species:xx`. Accented Latin letters are not reported. As with `=`, the checks on the form of the name are not repeated on that tag and `genus` is not compared with `species`
* `species=*` made of a single word. A leading hybrid sign written as a separate word (`×`, `x` or `X`) does not count (`x carnea` is a single word), and a genus followed only by the author or only by the hybrid sign counts as a single word (`Tilia L.`, `Platanus x`). If the word is a genus of the wiki list, in any case (`species=magnolia`), the card says so and proposes `genus=Magnolia` without `species`; not when `genus` is another genus (`genus=Quercus`, `species=Ilex`), because it is not known which of the two tags is wrong: "Genus differs from species" reports the two genera. With a family or an order in `genus` ("Family or order in genus": `genus=Pinaceae`, `species=Cedrus`) the card proposes `genus=Cedrus` without `species`, and the fix replaces the family with that genus. If it is the common name of a genus, in any language of the names used for `species:xx` (see above) and for one genus only (`species=oak`), the card proposes that genus: `genus=Quercus` without `species`. When the name belongs to one language only and `genus:xx` for that language is missing, the common name moves there instead of being removed, with an uppercase initial (`species=Brzoza` → `genus=Betula`, `genus:pl=Brzoza`). In both cases "Fix with JOSM" removes `species`, adds `genus` if it is missing and, for a common name, adds that `genus:xx` (no fix with a different `genus`). If the word is not a genus and `genus` followed by the word, in any case, is a species of the wiki list (`genus=Taxus`, `species=Baccata`), the card proposes that species as written in the list (`species=Taxus baccata`); "Fix with JOSM" writes it in `species` and adds `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` if they are missing, and `genus` stays. In this case `genus` is not compared with `species` (the word is the epithet), nor when the word, lowercase or uppercase, is not a known genus (`genus=Quercus`, `species=Pinea`): only this error is reported. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `genus=*` made of more than one word. The leading hybrid sign does not count: `× Sycoparrotia` (intergeneric hybrid genus) is a single word, and it does not count for the capital letter and for the comparison with `species` either
* `species=*` or `genus=*` starting with a lowercase letter (for `species` only with more than one word: with a single word the previous error is enough). A leading hybrid sign written `x` is not a lowercase initial: `x Chitalpa tashkentensis` is not reported
* `genus=*` that is not a known genus but the common name of a genus, in one of the languages of the names used for `species:xx` (see above) and for one genus only, in any case: `genus=wierzba` (Polish for *Salix*), `genus=Oak`. The card names the language when the name belongs to only one, and proposes the scientific name, which "Fix with JOSM" writes (`genus=Salix`). When the name belongs to one language only and `genus:xx` for that language is missing, the common name moves there with an uppercase initial (`genus=świerk` → `genus=Picea`, `genus:pl=Świerk`). For a genus with a single living species (`MONOTYPIC` in `index.html`, see the hints) and without `species`, the card also proposes that species and "Fix with JOSM" adds it: `genus=Oleandro` → `genus=Nerium`, `genus:it=Oleandro`, `species=Nerium oleander`. With this error "Genus starting lowercase" is not repeated, nor "Genus differs from species" when `species` is of that genus
* `genus=*` possibly misspelled: a single word that is not a known genus (the wiki species and genus lists, and `genera` in `scripts/whitelist.json`) but differs by one letter from one (`Sambuscus` → `Sambucus`, `Thuya` → `Thuja`, `Tilias` → `Tilia`), by two for names of at least 9 letters (`Styphlonobium` → `Styphnolobium`), in any case (`cypressus` → `Cupressus`). Not for names shorter than 4 letters, for names with more than one equally close genus, for names that differ only by the diaeresis (`Hippophaë`), nor, with two letters, for a genus followed by other letters (`Magnoliacé`, the French name of the family). The card proposes the genus, written as in the list, and "Fix with JOSM" writes it. With this error "Genus starting lowercase" is not repeated, nor "Genus differs from species" when `species` is of the proposed genus (`genus=Thuya` with `species=Thuja plicata`). True genera that are missing from the wiki lists and close to a genus that is there go in `genera` in `scripts/whitelist.json` (now `Adenocarpus`, `Chimonanthus`, `Evodia`, `Micromeles` and `Sciadopitys`, which the wiki list writes `Sciadopitus`). Only with data from the region update after this change
* `genus=*` that is the name of a family, ending in `-aceae` (`Cupressaceae`, `juglandaceae`) or one of the alternative family names (`Palmae`, `Leguminosae`, `Compositae`…), or of an order, ending in `-ales` (`Arecales`). A family name misspelled or in another language also counts, with the endings `-acae`, `-acee`, `-aceea`, `-acaea`, `-acées`, `-aceas`, `-áceas`, `-aceæ` (`pinacae`, `Pinacee`, `Pinacées`, `Pináceas`): the proposed name has `-aceae` (`Pinaceae`). The family goes in `taxon:family=*`: the card proposes it if the tag is missing, and "Fix with JOSM" removes `genus` and adds `taxon:family` (with the initial capitalised); if `taxon:family` already has that name it only removes `genus`. No fix for an order or with a `taxon:family` of another family. If `species=*` begins with a known genus (`Cedrus deodara`, or the genus alone: `Cedrus`), it already gives a more precise taxon: the card says the family or order can be removed, and "Fix with JOSM" removes `genus` without adding `taxon:family`; with a single-word `species` that is a genus, the "single-word species" error is reported as well (also with this family or order in `genus`) and its fix puts that genus in `genus` and removes `species`. With this error the other checks on the form of `genus` and the comparison with `species` are not repeated. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `genus=*` different from the first word of `species=*` (the leading hybrid sign, `×` or `x`, is ignored); not checked if `species` is a single lowercase word (e.g. `species=alba`, `species=x carnea`), which is not a genus, if it is a single uppercase word that is not a known genus, also after a hybrid sign (`species=Pinea`, `species=x Sepulcralis`; "Single-word species" is reported), or if `genus` or `species` has more than one value (`genus=Crataegus;Quercus`). When `genus` is not a known genus, the genus of `species` is, and the two differ by at most two letters (`genus=Qeurcus` with `species=Quercus robur`), the card proposes the genus of `species`, which "Fix with JOSM" writes (`genus=Quercus`). Not reported with "Genus possibly misspelled" when the proposed genus is that of `species` (`genus=Styphlonobium` with `species=Styphnolobium japonicum`), nor with "Family or order in genus" or a `genus` or `species` without a name
* `name=*` that is, as a whole, the common name of a species (`name=Roverella`) or of a genus (`name=Faggio`), from the hand-written lists of `scripts/whitelist.json` (the same as for `species:xx`), with none of `species=*`, `genus=*` and `taxon=*`: the card proposes the scientific name, which "Fix with JOSM" adds (`species=Quercus pubescens`, with the `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` that are missing for that species, or `genus=Fagus`) and keeps `name`. Not with more than one value in `name`, nor for a longer name (`name=Quercia delle streghe`). The same holds when `name=*` is the scientific name of a species of the wiki species list, only genus and epithet (`name=Ceratonia siliqua`): `species` is added written as in the list, and `name` stays; also for the hint with `genus` and for the next check. Likewise for the scientific name of a genus of the wiki genus list (`name=Quercus`): "Fix with JOSM" adds `genus=Quercus`. Not for a name that is not in the lists. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `name=*` that is the common name (or the scientific name, see above) of a species or genus, with `species=*` or `genus=*` of another species or genus (`name=Roverella` with `species=Quercus robur` or `genus=Pinus`). Only values with the form of a scientific name are compared: a known genus as `genus` or as the first word of `species`, and genus and epithet for a species (synonyms count as the same species; a cultivar or an author after them do not count). No fix is proposed, because which of the two tags is right is not known
* `taxon=*` that points to another species or genus than `species=*` or `genus=*` (`taxon=Quercus suber` with `species=Quercus robur`, `taxon=Pinus` with `genus=Quercus`). Only values with a known genus are compared: the genera always (the first word of `taxon` and of `species`, or a one-word `genus`), genus and epithet only when both `taxon` and `species` have them; synonyms count as the same species, and a cultivar, a variety or an author after genus and epithet do not count. Not with more than one value. No fix is proposed, because which of the two tags is right is not known. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `name:botanical=*`: the tag is deprecated on the OSM wiki, the scientific name goes in `species=*` or `genus=*`. As for the `taxon=*` hint, the message suggests the tag when the value allows it: a genus of the wiki genus list (`name:botanical=Betula` → `genus=Betula`, also with `sp.` or `spp.` after it: `name:botanical=Betula sp.`), genus and epithet of a species of the wiki species list (written as in the list), or a known genus followed by a lowercase epithet (`name:botanical=Tilia cordata` → `species=Tilia cordata`, a name not in the list: the message asks to check it). The
  author of the name does not count (`name:botanical=Quercus robur L.` → `species=Quercus robur`, `name:botanical=Tilia
  L.` → `genus=Tilia`), unless something else remains besides the name. "Fix with JOSM" removes `name:botanical` and adds `species` (with the `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` that are missing for that species) or `genus`; if `species` or `genus` already has that name (for a genus, also a `species` of that genus) it only removes `name:botanical`. No suggestion and no fix if `species` or `genus` point to something else, or for a value that is not a known genus. Only with data from the region update after this change

### Wikidata

* `species:wikidata=*` inconsistent with `species=*` according to the [OSM wiki species list](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/List_of_Species): a QID different from the one expected for the species, or a QID that belongs to another species. It is not reported if on Wikidata the scientific name (P225) or the basionym ([P566](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P566)) of the item is exactly `species` (the list may use the other name: for example `Mespilus germanica` and `Crataegus germanica`). Other accepted QIDs (for example synonyms) are added, per species, in `scripts/whitelist.json`; a synonym listed there (for example `Camphora officinarum` for `Cinnamomum camphora`) accepts the QID of the name in the wiki list. Not reported either when `species` has a spelling error and the QID belongs to the species proposed for it (`Platanus *hispanica` with the QID of `Platanus × hispanica`): only the spelling error is reported
* `species:wikidata=*` or `genus:wikidata=*` that on Wikidata are not a taxon, i.e. do not have a scientific name ([P225](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P225)), for example an illustration, a fruit or a single monumental tree (deleted items have their own check, below). If it triggers on `species:wikidata`, the previous check on the same key is not repeated
* `wikidata=*` or any `*:wikidata=*` key (`species:wikidata`, `genus:wikidata`, `taxon:variety:wikidata`, `taxon:cultivar:wikidata`…) with a QID that on Wikidata is a redirect: the item was merged into another one. The message gives the target QID; "Fix with JOSM" writes it in place of the old one, keeping the other values of the key (`species:wikidata=Q45417383` → `Q127849`). The other Wikidata checks on the same key already use the data of the target item
* `wikidata=*` or any `*:wikidata=*` key with a QID whose item was deleted from Wikidata. No fix. If it triggers on `species:wikidata`, the first check of this list is not repeated on the same key
* Redirects and deleted items are found with the Wikidata Query Service (`owl:sameAs`, no `schema:version`) and confirmed with the Wikidata API, which has no delay; they are stored in `data/wikidata_redirects.json` and read again at every update. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `wikidata=*` that is a taxon: `wikidata` indicates the individual tree, the species goes in `species:wikidata`
* `wikipedia=*` whose article is linked to a Wikidata item that is a taxon: `wikipedia` indicates the article about the individual tree, the species article goes in `species:wikipedia`, the genus article (an item of genus rank, such as `wikipedia=en:Holly`, *Ilex*) in `genus:wikipedia`. The item of the article is read from the Wikipedia API at every update (`data/wikipedia_qids.json`, as for `species:wikipedia`)
* `species:wikidata=*` that on Wikidata has the rank of genus or higher (section, family…), or `genus:wikidata=*` with the rank of species or lower (property [P105](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P105)). With a single `species:wikidata` of genus rank, no `genus:wikidata` and no `genus` of another genus, the card suggests moving the item to `genus:wikidata`, plus `genus=*` with the item's scientific name when both `genus` and `species` are missing; "Fix with JOSM" does it (`species:wikidata=Q45417383` → `genus:wikidata=Q45417383`, `genus=Tilia`)
* Scientific name on Wikidata ([P225](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P225)) of the item of `species:wikidata=*` or `genus:wikidata=*` different from `species=*` or `genus=*`. It applies to all species, not only those in the wiki list; it is not repeated if the same error is already reported by the list. Genus and epithet are compared, without `×`; the item's basionym (P566) and the QID that the wiki list associates with the species are also accepted; the QIDs in `whitelist.json` are accepted
* Species of the tree on Wikidata (property [P10241](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P10241), "individual of taxon", of the item of `wikidata=*`) different from every species indicated by the tree's tags: `species=*` and `species:wikidata=*` and, without `species`, also `taxon=*` (a species of the wiki list, or genus and epithet), `species:la=*`, the article of `species:wikipedia=*` and `name=*` when it is the name of a species. The synonyms in `scripts/whitelist.json` count as the same species (`Platanus × hispanica` on Wikidata with `species=Platanus acerifolia` is not reported). The message gives both species, to be checked on the ground: without `species`, no hint proposes a value for it and Fix with JOSM does not add it (nor `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` or `leaf_cycle` of the proposed species)
* `wikidata=*` whose Wikidata item has coordinates (P625) more than 1 km from the tree (if it has more than one, the nearest counts; items without coordinates or that are a taxon are not checked). The threshold is `WD_MAX_DIST` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `wikidata=*` whose item has no coordinates, but has an image (P18) with coordinates on Commons more than 500 m from the tree (the subject's position if the file gives one, otherwise the camera position; same `COMMONS_MAX_DIST` threshold as the check on Commons photos). If the same photo is also linked to the tree (`wikimedia_commons` or `image`), only that check examines it

### Links, sources and monumental trees

* Links with an invalid format: `wikidata` other than `Q…`, `wikimedia_commons` that is not a Commons title (`File:…` or `Category:…`: also the address of the page, `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:…`, which "Fix with JOSM" turns into the title, with spaces instead of underscores) or a `File:…` without a file extension (`.jpg`, `.png`…), `panoramax` that is not a UUID, `image` that does not start with `http` (or that is not a single Commons file, `File:…`: see the hints) or that has more than one address separated by `;`
* `website=*` or `url=*` (a single value) that is a Wikipedia article (also on the mobile site, `fr.m.wikipedia.org`): articles have their own tag, `wikipedia=*`, which gives the article about the single tree. Without `wikipedia=*`, the card says that an article about this tree is written `wikipedia=<language>:<title>`, while an article about a place or a list of trees does not go in `wikipedia=*`; there is no "Fix with JOSM", because the choice depends on the article. With `wikipedia=*` of the same article (underscores and the case of the first letter do not count) `website`/`url` can be removed, and "Fix with JOSM" removes it; with a different article the card shows both
* Unreachable links in `image`, `website` and `url` (nonexistent page, 404 or 410, or a site that no longer exists) and `wikimedia_commons=File:…` files that do not exist on Commons. 403, 429, server errors and timeouts are not reported, as they are often blocks against automated tools. Links on the same site are checked one at a time, and each link is rechecked at most every 30 days (`URL_RECHECK_DAYS`). An update of a region checks at most 100 links of the same site (`URL_HOST_MAX`), first those never checked, then the oldest: the others are checked in the next updates and, until then, are not reported
* Linked Commons files (`wikimedia_commons=File:…`, or `image=*` pointing to a file page on Commons or to the file address on upload.wikimedia.org, also of a thumbnail) whose coordinates are more than 500 m from the tree. The subject's position (*object location*) is used if the file gives one, otherwise the camera position (*camera location*); files without coordinates are not checked. Coordinates are read from the Commons API and re-read at most every 30 days, like links. The threshold is `COMMONS_MAX_DIST` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `ref:masaf`, `ref:rami`, `ref:monumentaltrees`, `ref:CRFOP` or `ref:gid` (Poland, [issue #8](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/8)), `ref:usop` (Czechia, code of the ÚSOP register in the [DRUSOP](https://drusop.nature.cz/portal/) database), `heritage:operator=icnf` (Portugal) or `heritage:operator=anf` (Luxembourg), `website`/`url` pointing to monumentaltrees.com, or `wikidata=*` whose item has the monumentaltrees.com ID ([P9704](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P9704)) or is a direct instance (P31) of monumental plant ([Q811534](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q811534)) or has a heritage designation ([P1435](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P1435)), without `denotation=natural_monument` (a monumental tree without a source is not reported). `monument=tree` (also among multiple values) counts as `denotation=natural_monument` everywhere else in the tool ([issue #95](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/95)): such a tree is drawn as a monumental tree and it is on the world map of monumental trees, but it gets this error too, alone or with a monumental source, because `denotation=natural_monument` is missing (also on stumps). All the sources of a tree are listed in a single message. P9704, P31 and P1435 are read from Wikidata at every update, together with the other data of the item. Every designation counts, except those added one by one, on request, to `heritage_not_natural` in `scripts/whitelist.json` (today none) because they do not indicate a monumental tree; an item that also has another designation is reported. On stumps (`natural=tree_stump`) `ref:CRFOP` and `ref:gid` are not reported; on a stump whose `ref:masaf` codes are all removed from the MASAF list (natural death, felling…) no source is reported, Wikidata included, except `monument=tree`. A stump with a code still listed gets the error. Being a member of a relation with `ref:masaf` (a [homogeneous group](mappa.md#homogeneous-groups-of-the-masaf-list) of the MASAF list) is not a source: those trees are mapped as the wiki says, with their own `denotation`. A member with a `ref:masaf` of its own (or another source) on its tags gets this check like any other tree. Relations are not checked: removed or unknown codes on a relation give no error
* Tags of a national register of monumental trees on a tree of a region in another country: `ref:masaf`, `masaf:criteria` and `ref:rami` (Italy), `ref:icnf` and `heritage:operator=icnf` (Portugal), `heritage:operator=anf` (Luxembourg), `ref:CRFOP` and `ref:gid` (Poland), `ref:usop` (Czechia). The country is the one of the region (`iso` in `scripts/regions.json`); stumps are checked too. The list is `NATIONAL_TAGS` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `ref:masaf=*` of a tree that the MASAF list of monumental trees of Italy gives as removed (due to natural death, felling, loss of requirements or downgrading): the card gives the reason, and the recognition row becomes "Formerly listed in the MASAF list of monumental trees of Italy…". It does not apply to stumps (`natural=tree_stump`), for which the code of a removed tree is normal. Codes are compared without spaces and ignoring leading zeros (`03/G114/CN/01` = `003/G114/CN/01`). The list is in `scripts/masaf_list.json`, produced with `scripts/update_masaf.py` from the KML file that MASAF publishes on the [list page](https://www.masaf.gov.it/flex/cm/pages/ServeBLOB.php/L/IT/IDPagina/11260); source: Masaf - Direzione generale dell'economia montana e delle foreste - Difor4 - "dataset AMI - Censimento alberi monumentali d'Italia vs. 11_2024", licence CC BY 4.0
* `ref:masaf=*` with a code that is not in the MASAF list, neither among the listed trees nor among the removed ones. Codes are compared without spaces; the first number can have two or three digits (`02/A002/RM/02` = `002/A002/RM/02`), but a single digit is reported (`2/A002/RM/02`). Stumps are checked too; not on trees of regions in other countries, where the previous check on national registers already reports the tag
* MASAF code of a single tree on several trees ([issue #95](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/95)): the MASAF list gives the code as a single (or isolated) tree (field "tipo" of the list, `t` in `scripts/masaf_trees.json`), but the same `ref:masaf` is on other trees of the region. The card lists the other nodes. Stumps do not count and are not reported; codes are compared as in the previous check. Only trees of the same region are compared (on the world map of monumental trees, all of them)
* MASAF code of a group on one tree only ([issue #95](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/95)): the MASAF list gives the code as a group, a single or double row, an avenue or a homogeneous group, but in the region the `ref:masaf` is only on this tree and on no relation with `ref:masaf`. The [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/IT:Key:ref:masaf) maps a group with a `type=site` relation with `ref:masaf`, `natural=tree_group` and `denotation=natural_monument`, with the trees as members (see [homogeneous groups](mappa.md#homogeneous-groups-of-the-masaf-list)). Italian regions only; stumps do not count
* `masaf:criteria=*` with invalid codes: only `a`–`g`, `a1`, `a2`, `a2.1`, `a2.2`, `a2.3` are allowed, separated by commas without spaces (see the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/IT:Key:ref:masaf#Criteri_di_monumentalit%C3%A0)); spaces, uppercase, `;` and empty parts are also reported

### Other tags

* `tree=*` or `type=*`, tags not documented for trees: check whether they can be replaced with another tag. `type=palm` is not reported, because it needs to be fixed in bulk
* Misspelled key ([issue #32](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/32)): a key that is not one of those used for trees, but differs from one of them only in capitalisation (`Species`, `HEIGHT`), by one letter (`leaftype`, `leaf type`, `species_wikidata`) or by two adjacent letters swapped (`heigth`); for keys of at least 12 letters two differences are allowed (`circumfence` → `circumference`). Keys shorter than 5 letters (`name`, `sex`) are reported only for capitalisation, because one letter makes another word; for `ref:…` keys the part after `ref:` counts, so `ref:gid`, `ref:rami`, `ref:icnf` and `ref:usop` are short keys too (`ref:uid` is another key, `ref:massaf` is reported). Subkeys of known keys (`species:wikipedia:it`) and numbered variants (`image2`, `panoramax:1`) are not reported. Synonyms of `planted_date` are reported too ([issue #90](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/90)): `year_planted`, `date:planted`, `date_planted`, `planted:date` and `planted` (except `planted=yes` and `planted=no`), with `planted_date` as the suggested key. Other keys of the registers of monumental trees are reported the same way ([issue #95](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/95)): `ref:mipaaf` (suggested key `ref:masaf`), `ref:drusop` and `ref:drusop:tree` (suggested key `ref:usop`), also in capitals (`ref:DRUSOP`). The card suggests the known key with the same value; the value is not used to draw the tree. The known keys are `KNOWN_KEYS` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`; none of the 146 keys most used with `natural=tree` on Taginfo is reported
* `fixme=*` (also `FIXME=*`, [issue #85](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/85)): the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:fixme) uses it to describe something to check or correct, and the tag is removed once that is resolved. The card shows the value. Empty values are not reported; stumps are checked too. Only with data from the region update after this change
* Invalid or future date: `start_date=*`, `planted_date=*`, `check_date=*`, `survey:date=*` or `source:date=*` invalid or in the future ([issue #81](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/81) for `planted_date`, checked in the same forms as `start_date`; with both tags invalid the message lists both). The forms of the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:start_date) are accepted: `1850`, `1850-05`, `1850-05-01`, `~1850`, `1850s`, `early`/`mid`/`late 1850s`, `C19`, `early`/`mid`/`late C19`, `before 1850`, `after 1850`, years BC (`500 BC`), dates of the Julian calendar (`j:1918-01-31`, also `j:1918` and `j:1918-01`; a date that does not exist in the Julian calendar is reported, `j:1700-02-29` is accepted) and the ranges `1850..1900` (both ends are checked). A Julian date with the day is converted to the Gregorian calendar for the age on the card, the oldest tree in Record trees and the sentence on the year (`j:1918-01-31` = 13 February 1918). Other forms are reported (`05/03/1990`, `c. 1888`, `2020;2021`, years of one or two digits, months or days of one digit such as `1990-5-3`, more than one prefix such as `before after 1850`, the century with a lowercase `c`), as are ranges that end before they start (`1900..1800`; a decade or a century counts up to its last year, so `1890s..C19` is accepted), dates that do not exist (`2020-02-30`, `2025-06-00`) and dates after the day of the data update (the oldest date indicated counts, also with `before`: a mapped tree already exists, so `before 2030` is reported). `check_date` and `survey:date` (date of the last survey, wiki of [check_date](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:check_date) and [survey:date](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:survey:date)), and the date of a single measurement `check_date:height`, `check_date:circumference`, `check_date:diameter` and `check_date:diameter_crown` ([issue #99](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/99), also without the measurement), and `source:date` (date of the source, [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:source:date); it is not a survey of the tree, so it does not replace the date of a measurement), are accepted only as `2024-09-02`, `2024-09` or `2024`: other forms (`02:09:2024`, `02/09/2024`, `~2024`, `201011`), dates that do not exist (`2024-02-30`) and dates after the day of the data update are reported. With `=` in `start_date` or `planted_date` only the check on the tag written inside a value is reported. Only with data from the region update after this change for `check_date`, `survey:date`, `source:date` and `check_date:*` of the measurements
* Different `start_date` and `planted_date` ([issue #81](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/81)): the tree has both tags with different values (extra spaces aside; `2020` and `2020-05` are different). The [wiki of planted_date](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:planted_date) does not say which of the two is preferred. For the age in the card and for Oldest the site uses `start_date`. Not reported when one of the two dates is invalid or has `=` in the value. Only with data from the region update after this change
* Trees at the same point: two or more `natural=tree` nodes with exactly the same coordinates (stumps do not count). The card lists the other nodes
* `leaf_type=*` or `leaf_cycle=*` (valid) different from those of the species in the species list (also for a name with something after genus and epithet, such as a cultivar, an author or a rank: `Quercus robur 'Fastigiata'` and `Quercus robur L.` count as `Quercus robur`; not for a hybrid written with both parents, `Quercus robur × petraea`) or, if the species is not there, of the genus in the [genus list](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/List_of_Genus,_Leaf_cycle,_Leaf_type). `deciduous` and `semi_deciduous` are considered equivalent, as are `evergreen` and `semi_evergreen`; `leafless` is not compared. For palms (recognised as described in [The map](mappa.md#rendering): `taxon:family=Arecaceae`, a palm genus, etc.) both `palm` and `broadleaved` are accepted. `leaf_type=palm` is also accepted for palm-like plants that are not palms (`PALM_LIKE_GENERA` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`: *Yucca*, *Cordyline*, *Dracaena*, *Cycas*, *Musa*, tree ferns…). For *Tamarix* both `needleleaved` and `broadleaved` are accepted, and for *Cycas* `needleleaved`, `broadleaved` and `palm` (`leaf_types` in `scripts/whitelist.json`). On any other tree that the lists give as `broadleaved` (for example *Ginkgo* or *Morus*), `leaf_type=palm` is reported; for this case only, when the tree has neither `species=*` nor `genus=*`, the genus is taken from the first word of `taxon=*` (`taxon=Ginkgo biloba`).
* `leaf_type=*` other than `broadleaved`, `needleleaved`, `leafless`, `palm`
* `leaf_cycle=*` other than `deciduous`, `semi_deciduous`, `evergreen`, `semi_evergreen`
* `denotation=*` rare or invalid. Each part of a multiple value (`natural_monument;landmark`) is checked on its own, against the number of uses on nodes according to [Taginfo](https://taginfo.openstreetmap.org/keys/denotation#values). It is reported if:
  * it has an invalid format: characters other than lowercase letters, digits and `_` (for example `=`, `:`, `/`, spaces, uppercase) or it does not start with a letter;
  * it has spaces before or after it (`urban; natural_monument`): multiple values are separated by `;` without spaces. "Fix with JOSM" removes the spaces;
  * it is a common value (at least 1000 uses) with other text attached without `_`, for example `urbanheidialhoranta` or `single1953`;
  * it is used at most 10 times.

  The thresholds are at the top of `scripts/fetch_trees.py` (`DEN_RARE_MAX`, `DEN_COMMON_MIN`). The format check always applies; the other two require `scripts/denotation_values.json`.

### General notes

The list-based checks require `scripts/species_list.json`, `scripts/genus_list.json` and `scripts/denotation_values.json`, which are generated by the "Update reference lists" Action; if the files are missing, the checks are not run.

If `circumference=*` is missing but `diameter=*` is present, the circumference is derived as π × diameter. As the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:diameter) states, `diameter` without a unit is in millimetres (unlike `circumference` and `diameter_crown`, in metres); written units (`mm`, `cm`, `m`, `ft`, `in`) are respected. Values without a unit below 10 are not used (see the check above). The derived circumference goes through the same checks and estimates; if both tags are present, `diameter` is ignored (but the checks on the format of numeric values still apply to `diameter` too). One exception: when `circumference` is not a readable number (`circumference=big`), the 43 m limit is checked on `diameter` (`diameter=20000`); the tree is still drawn without a measured trunk.

The checks are computed by `scripts/fetch_trees.py` and saved in the `issues` field of `data/regions/<region>.geojson.gz`.

## Hints

Besides errors, the tree card can show hints: information that could improve the data, not errors to fix. They appear after the errors, preceded by ⓘ, in grey and not bold; they do not colour the tree on the map, are not in the errors menu and do not count in the ranking. Only in the QA tool, not in the monumental trees map. This is also where checks that would give too many false positives as errors live.

* No `species` or `genus`, but the tree's Wikidata item (`wikidata=*`) indicates the species ([P10241](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P10241), not of genus rank or above): `species` could be added with that name, after a survey on the ground. Not shown when the tree's own tags (`species:wikidata`, `species:wikipedia`, `species:la`, `taxon`, `name`) indicate a species: the same one would be a duplicate of their hint, a different one is the error "Species differs from Wikidata"
* `species=*`, but the tree's Wikidata item (`wikidata=*`) only indicates its genus (P10241, for example `Vitis` with `species=Vitis vinifera`): it is not an error on OSM, but the item could be made more precise on Wikidata, citing a source compatible with the Wikidata licence (OSM data is not, so the OSM value cannot be copied there). In this case "Species differs from Wikidata" is not reported
* `species:wikidata=*` (a taxon, not of genus rank or above) without `species`: `species` could be added with the item's scientific name (P225)
* `species` in the wiki species list (also a hybrid written without `×`, see below), without `species:wikidata`: the QID from the list could be added
* `species:wikipedia=*` without `species:wikidata` (when the list does not already give the QID, see above): `species:wikidata` could be added with the QID of the item linked to the Wikipedia article
* `species:wikipedia=*` without `species` (and without the two hints on `species` above): `species` could be added with the scientific name (P225) of the item linked to the article
* For both, the article must have a Wikidata item that is a species (a taxon, not of genus rank or higher). Not the other way round: `species:wikidata` without `species:wikipedia` is not reported
* `species:la=*` (the Latin name) without `species`: `species` could be added with that name, written as in the wiki species list when genus and epithet are there, otherwise as it is. "Fix with JOSM" adds it and keeps `species:la`. Not with more than one value, with a `genus` of another genus, nor on stumps. Only with data from the region update after this change
* The hints that suggest a value for `species` appear only if the scientific name has at least two words: a single word (`Pinus`) is a genus, not a species
* `species:it=*` with a lowercase initial (each value, if there are several separated by `;`): on OSM 98% of the values have a capital (for example `Platano comune`), so a capital is suggested. It is a hint and not an error because it is not a rule written on the wiki
* `name:en=*` with a lowercase initial (`the big oak`): in English a proper name starts with a capital letter, which "Fix with JOSM" writes (`The big oak`)
* `name=*` that is the common name of a species (`name=Roverella`), without `species=*` but with `genus=*` of that species' genus (`genus=Quercus`): `species=Quercus pubescens` could be added. The same for the scientific name of a species of the list (`name=Quercus pubescens`). "Fix with JOSM" adds it and keeps `name` and `genus`. Without any taxon tag, or with one that points to something else, it is an error (see the name group)
* `note=*` (also `NOTE=*`, [issue #85](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/85)): the card shows the note for mappers. Language variants (`note:it`) do not count. It also appears for stumps
* `taxon=*`: the tag does not say which rank the name belongs to, and the message suggests a more precise tag if possible (`species`, `genus`, `taxon:cultivar`…). If the value is genus and epithet of a species in the wiki species list (without a cultivar or anything else), or a genus of the wiki genus list, the card says so and "Fix with JOSM" writes it in `species` or `genus` (with the name as in the list) and removes `taxon`; if `species` or `genus` already has that name, it only removes `taxon`. A value that is not in the list but is a known genus (capital initial) followed by a lowercase epithet, possibly with `×` (`taxon=Citrus sinensis`), has the form of a species name: the card suggests `species` with the same value and asks to check the name (synonyms, hybrid sign), and the fix moves it as written. `sp`, `spp`, `var` and similar words are not epithets. No fix if `species` or `genus` indicate something else (another species, another genus)
* Hybrid sign written with the letter `x` (or `X`) as a separate word in `species=*` or `genus=*`, at the start or inside the name (`x Chitalpa tashkentensis`, `Platanus x hispanica`): the sign `×` is suggested, and "Fix with JOSM" replaces it. Not with multiple values or with an error on the form of the name, which already proposes its own fix. The page computes it from the tags, so it appears without waiting for the region update
* `species=*` that is a hybrid of the wiki species list written without the hybrid sign `×` (`Tilia europaea`, in the list `Tilia × europaea`): the sign only says that the plant is a hybrid, so the name counts as the species of the list in every check (`species:wikidata`, `leaf_type`, `leaf_cycle`, measurement limits), in the statistics and in the species filter, like `x` instead of `×`. Only for genus and epithet, and only when the name without `×` is not another name of the list or a synonym. The card shows how the name is written in the list, and "Fix with JOSM" writes it in `species`. Not with an error on the form of the name
* `species=*` with the form of a species name (two words: genus with a capital initial and lowercase epithet; the hybrid sign `×` does not count) that is not in the wiki species list, also as a synonym, with `x` or without `×` (`Pinus halepensis`): "not in the wiki list of species". If the name is in use, it could be added to the [list](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/List_of_Species); if it is a synonym or a name no longer in use of a species in the list (`Sophora japonica`, in the list `Styphnolobium japonicum`), the second link opens a GitHub issue already filled in with the name and the link to the tree, where the name of the list can be written: the synonym can then be added to `synonyms` in `scripts/whitelist.json`, and the name counts as the species of the list. Not for stumps, with a cultivar, an author or anything else after the epithet, with multiple values, or with an error on the name (`species` errors, "not a tree", tag in the value, not in Latin, underscore, multiple values)
* `species=*` that includes a subspecies, a variety or a cultivar ([issue #68](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/68)); the cases can be combined, also with the genus abbreviated (error "Species with the genus abbreviated", above: the suggested name has the genus in full):
  * a subspecies, `subsp.` or `ssp.` (also without the point) followed by a lowercase epithet (`Pinus nigra ssp nigra`): it could be written as `species=Pinus nigra` with `taxon:subspecies=Pinus nigra subsp. nigra`, the whole name with `subsp.`. Not with an uppercase epithet (`Nelumbo nucifera subsp Lutea`) or without the genus (`nigra subsp. laricio`)
  * a variety, `var.` (also `var` without the point) followed by a lowercase epithet (`Quercus robur var. fastigiata`): it could be written as `species=Quercus robur` with `taxon:variety=fastigiata`. "Fix with JOSM" writes it only if the name is a taxon of variety rank on Wikidata, among those in `data/wikidata_taxa.json` (`Populus alba var. pyramidalis`; property `sp_var_ok`); otherwise the hint has no fix and adds to check that it is a variety and not a cultivar, which goes in `taxon:cultivar`: cultivars are found written with `var.` too (`Pyrus calleryana var. chanticleer`)
  * a cultivar in quotation marks (`Acer palmatum 'Atropurpureum'`, also after `cv.`): `species=Acer palmatum` with `taxon:cultivar=Atropurpureum` (the cultivar without quotation marks)
  * a cultivar without quotation marks after genus and epithet of a species in the wiki species list (`Pyrus calleryana Chanticleer`, `Tilia cordata Green Spire`): only words after the epithet, the first with a capital and at least three letters, none of them an author (with the point, or one of the authors written without it in `AUTHOR_NAMES` of `scripts/fetch_trees.py`: `Betula pendula Roth`), a rank or a hybrid sign. Not when the word after the epithet is lowercase (`Quercus robur fastigiata`)
  * a genus of the wiki genus list with only the cultivar (`Magnolia 'Susan'`): `genus=Magnolia` with `taxon:cultivar=Susan` instead of `species`; the fix removes `species` and adds `genus` if missing. Not with a different `genus`

  "Fix with JOSM" writes these tags and, for the species left in `species`, the `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` that are missing. If `taxon:subspecies`, `taxon:variety` or `taxon:cultivar` (or `variety`, `cultivar`) already has that value, case and quotation marks aside (for the subspecies also the epithet alone, `taxon:subspecies=nigra`, or `ssp.` instead of `subsp.`), the fix only shortens `species`; with a different value there is no hint. Not with more than one subspecies, variety or cultivar, with multiple values, when the whole value is a name of the wiki species list, with the author in `species`, another error on its form or the hybrid sign written `x`, which already propose another `species`
* `ref:masaf=*` without `masaf:criteria`: the criteria that the MASAF list gives for that code could be added (from `scripts/masaf_trees.json`)
* Only in Italian regions, not for stumps: a `ref:masaf=*` code of a listed tree whose coordinates in the MASAF list (`scripts/masaf_trees.json`) are more than 5 km from the tree on OSM (`MASAF_FAR_KM` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`). The card gives the code, the municipality of the list and the distance, for each code with several values separated by `;`. It is a hint and not an error because the coordinates of the list can be imprecise. Codes that are not in the list are already an error, and removed trees have no coordinates in the list
* Only in Italian regions: a tree without `denotation=natural_monument` or `landmark` and without `ref:masaf`, with a circumference (from `circumference`, or from `diameter`) at least equal to the MASAF indicative threshold for its species: it could be a monumental tree. The thresholds are the MASAF "minimum indicative circumferences for the size criterion" (January 2020), transcribed in `scripts/masaf_thresholds.json`, only for the species in the table (for Betula, which has 200 or 300 cm, 300 is used). The hint is not shown if the tree has "Circumference > 43 m", "Circumference > species limit", "tree too slender", "tree too slender for its species" or "tree too stocky" (only the error stays), nor for the members of a relation with `ref:masaf` ([homogeneous groups](mappa.md#homogeneous-groups-of-the-masaf-list)), which already count as monumental. These trees also appear on the map of monumental trees, with a note on the card, unless the circumference has an error
* `source=*` of a tree import listed in `scripts/imports.json` ([issue #2](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/2)): "Tree from an undocumented import" for imports without documentation, "Imported tree" with the link to the wiki page for documented ones. The comparison ignores case, surrounding spaces and the trailing slash; with several values separated by `;` one is enough
* `genus=*` of a genus with a single living species (the others are only fossils), without `species`: `species` could be added with that species (for example `genus=Ginkgo` → `species=Ginkgo biloba`). The genera are in a hand-written list (`MONOTYPIC` in `index.html`): Ginkgo, Metasequoia, Sequoiadendron, Sequoia, Cryptomeria, Sciadopitys, Glyptostrobus, Platycladus, Thujopsis, Pseudolarix, Wollemia, Fitzroya, Davidia, Idesia, Eucommia, Tetracentron, Trochodendron, Oxydendrum, Franklinia, Umbellularia, Nerium, Cydonia. For `genus=Sequoia` in Italy, French-speaking and English-speaking countries (from the region's ISO code: IT, FR, BE, LU, CH, MC, GB, IE, US, CA, AU, NZ) a second hint adds that there the common name "sequoia" is often used for the giant sequoia (*Sequoiadendron giganteum*) too, so the tree should be checked
* A tree without `species=*` at 1800 m of elevation or more (`ELEV_HINT` in `index.html`): the hint lists the most common species above that height in the region, written by hand in `scripts/regions.json` (`elevation.high_species`, split into `needleleaved` and `broadleaved`; for Piedmont *Larix decidua*, *Pinus cembra*, *Picea abies*, *Pinus uncinata*, *Betula pendula*, *Sorbus aucuparia*). With `leaf_type=needleleaved` or `broadleaved` only the species of that type are listed, with `genus=*` only those of that genus; if none is left, or the region has no list, there is no hint. Only on trees with an elevation (see [Elevation](dati.md#elevation)), and not when another hint already suggests a species (from Wikidata, Wikipedia or a genus with a single living species). The elevation comes from a model of the surface, so it is approximate: the hint says "about"
* `circumference=*`, `height=*`, `diameter_crown=*` or `diameter=*` in feet and inches with spaces (`7' 4"`): the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Map_features/Units) writes them without spaces (`7'4"`). It is a hint and not an error because the value is unambiguous and validators such as Osmose accept it; it also appears for stumps
* `circumference=*`, `height=*`, `diameter_crown=*` or `diameter=*` with a number that starts with the point (`circumference=.75`, `height=.5 m`): the value is read as it is (0.75), and the form with the zero in front is suggested (`0.75`, `0.5 m`), which "Fix with JOSM" writes. It is a hint and not an error because the value is unambiguous and it is not a rule written on the wiki. Not for `diameter` without a unit, where the error on a diameter too small for millimetres already applies, nor with a unit in a non-standard form, where that error proposes its own fix. The page computes it from the tags, so it appears without waiting for the region update; it also appears for stumps
* `height=*`, `circumference=*`, `diameter=*` or `diameter_crown=*` dated more than 10 years ago ([issue #94](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/94)): "Measurement unchanged for more than 10 years", with the tags and where each date comes from. The date of a measurement is that of its own `check_date:*` (`check_date:height=*`, `check_date:circumference=*`, `check_date:diameter=*`, `check_date:diameter_crown=*`, [issue #99](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/99)) when it is valid and not in the future, otherwise the date of the last edit of the tree, whatever tag it changed: without `check_date:*`, a measurement on a tree edited recently for another tag gets no hint even if it is older. A `check_date=*` or `survey:date=*` (survey of the whole tree) later than the `check_date:*` of a measurement replaces it, and the last edit counts, which is not earlier; with the two dates in the same year or month (`2012` and `2012-05-01`) the date of the measurement counts. A date with only the year or the month counts as the whole period: `check_date:height=2016` is not more than 10 years old until the year 2016 is. Measurements with the same date are listed together: "height=12, circumference=2.1 (last edit: 2014-05-02); diameter_crown=8 (check_date:diameter_crown=2012)". After surveying them again on the ground, even with the same values, the date of the survey can be written in `check_date=*` (whole tree) or in `check_date:*` (one measurement): it is an edit, so the hint disappears with the next region update. The ten years are counted back from today (`AGE_CUT` in `index.html`). The page computes it from the dates in the data, so with the last edit it appears without waiting for the region update; the `check_date:*` of the measurements arrive with the next update of the region (properties `md_<measurement>`). Not for stumps
* `height=*` under 2 m dated more than 5 years ago: "Tree less than 2 m tall, with the height unchanged for more than 5 years", with the height and where the date comes from (`check_date:height=*` or the last edit, as in the previous hint). After a new survey `height` can be updated or, if the height is the same, the date of the survey written in `check_date:height=*` or `check_date=*`. With this hint `height` is not repeated in the previous one. As there, the five years are counted back from today (`AGE_CUT[0]`, threshold `LOW_H` in `index.html`), and not for stumps
* `diameter_crown=*` that is as wide as the trunk diameter (equal to the centimetre) or narrower: "The crown is as wide as the trunk diameter" or "The crown is narrower than the trunk diameter", with the two tags and the trunk diameter. The trunk is that of `circumference` (divided by π) or, without it, of `diameter`. It is a hint and not an error because real trees can have these measurements; the two measurements could be checked on the ground. Not with "Circumference > 43 m", "Circumference > species limit", "Tree too stocky", "circumference and diameter do not match", "diameter without unit too small for millimetres" or "Crown diameter > 60.4 m", which already report one of the measurements. The page computes it from the tags, so it appears without waiting for the region update; not for stumps
* `diameter_crown=*` more than 3 times `height=*` (`CROWN_WIDE` in `index.html`): "The crown is more than 3 times as wide as the tree is tall", with the two tags. As in the previous hint, it is not an error and the two measurements could be checked on the ground. Not with "Crown diameter > 60.4 m", "Height > 116.22 m" or "Height > species limit". The page computes it from the tags, so it appears without waiting for the region update; not for stumps
* `leaf_type` or `leaf_cycle` missing, with the species or genus in the wiki lists: the values from the lists could be added (wrong ones are already errors). If the species is not in the species list, the values come from the genus and the hint names the genus (e.g. «For Ulmus, …»). `leaf_type=palm` is never proposed: for palms (drawn as palms all the same) the hint and "Fix with JOSM" propose the value of the lists, usually `broadleaved`, and nothing when the palm is in neither list; an existing `leaf_type=palm` on a palm is not reported nor replaced ([issue #55](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/55); only with data from the region update after this change)
* `image=*` pointing to a Wikimedia Commons file (the file page on commons.wikimedia.org, the file address on upload.wikimedia.org, also of a thumbnail, or the file name alone, `image=File:…`), without `wikimedia_commons=*`: `image` could be replaced with `wikimedia_commons=File:…`; "Fix with JOSM" adds `wikimedia_commons` and removes `image` (only if `image` has a single value). It also appears for stumps
* `image=*` (a single value) that is a web page and not an image file: a page of Google Drive (not `drive.google.com/uc`, which gives the file), Google Maps (`maps.app.goo.gl`), `goo.gl`, Mapillary, Flickr, Geograph, Imgur, Wikiloc, Twitter/X, Facebook, Instagram, OneDrive or Wikipedia. Addresses that give the file are not reported (`i.imgur.com`, `live.staticflickr.com`, `images.mapillary.com`), nor Google Photos links (`photos.app.goo.gl`, `photos.google.com`), which are accepted. The card says that a photo can also be uploaded to Wikimedia Commons or Panoramax. For Mapillary the card says that it has its own tag, `mapillary=*`; for the page of a Mapillary photo (`mapillary.com/map/im/<key>` or `mapillary.com/app/?pKey=<id>`), without `mapillary=*`, it suggests `mapillary=<id>` and "Fix with JOSM" writes it and removes `image`. The sites are `IMAGE_PAGE_SITES` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`. It also appears for stumps
* `wikidata=*` without `denotation=natural_monument` or `landmark`: a tree with a Wikidata item is notable, and could have one of the two (it does not appear if an error already says so, for example protection on Wikidata)
* `species=Ginkgo biloba` (or `genus=Ginkgo` without `species`) without `sex=*`, only from April to November (October to May in the southern hemisphere, from the tree's latitude): `sex=female` could be added if the tree is female, with a note on when and how to check ([issue #25](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/25)). The words "foul-smelling seeds" are underlined with a dashed line: a click or tap opens a photo of the seeds ([File:Gingko biloba seeds.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gingko_biloba_seeds.jpg)) with the Commons credits, as for the card photos (loaded only when opened); a second click, a click elsewhere or Esc closes it
* A tree of another dioecious species or genus with known traits, without `sex=*` ([issue #27](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/27)): `sex=female` or `sex=male` could be added if the sex can be recognised; the hint gives the traits of females and males, the same as in the ♂ / ♀ box of the [tree card](scheda.md). It appears only in the months when at least one of the two sexes can be recognised (shifted by six months in the southern hemisphere, from the tree's latitude), and names that period; for the species that can be recognised all year it always appears, without a period. The species is that of `species=*` (also followed by a variety or cultivar), otherwise the genus of `genus=*` or of `species=*`; trees with more than one species (`;`) have no hint. The months, for the northern hemisphere (`SEX_SEASON` in `index.html`): Taxus baccata February–October, Ilex aquifolium May–February, Laurus nobilis March–November, Acer negundo March–December, Ceratonia siliqua August–November, Populus (and Populus nigra, with its own traits) and Salix March–June, Broussonetia April–September, Cephalotaxus March–October, Cercidiphyllum March–December, Chamaerops April–November, Gymnocladus May–March, Hippophae August–April, Maclura May–November, Phellodendron June–January, Pistacia March–November, Torreya April–October, Trachycarpus April–December, Ailanthus altissima June–February, Diospyros lotus and Diospyros virginiana May–December, Juniperus virginiana September–April, Phytolacca dioica May–November; all year for Juniperus communis, Juniperus oxycedrus, Phoenix, Podocarpus, Araucaria araucana and Schinus molle. The page computes it from the tags, so it appears without waiting for the region update

The hints on Wikidata, Wikipedia, `species:it`, `name:en`, `note`, `taxon`, variety and cultivar, MASAF and imports are computed in `hint_props` of `scripts/fetch_trees.py` (properties `hint_sp`, `hint_wg`, `hint_sn`, `hint_sw`, `hint_wp`, `hint_wn`, `hint_la`, `hint_it`, `hint_ne`, `hint_nt`, `hint_tx`, `sp_base` / `sp_gen` / `sp_var` / `sp_cv` / `sp_abbr`, `hint_mc`, `hint_mf`, `hint_ms`, `hint_imp_u` / `hint_imp_d`) and arrive with the next update of the region; the others in the page (`hints` in `index.html`) from the tree's data.
