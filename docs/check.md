# QA checks

The checks DendrOSM runs on every tree, with their thresholds and where they come from.

## QA checks

Trees with at least one error have a red outline. The checks are grouped as in the error menu of the map.

### Measurements

* Circumference greater than 43 m: the largest recorded, in African baobabs (*Adansonia digitata*), according to [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/living-tree-with-greatest-girth)
* Height greater than 116.22 m: the tallest measured tree, [Hyperion](https://www.monumentaltrees.com/en/usa/california/delnortecounty/4462_redwoodnationalpark/9104/) (*Sequoia sempervirens*, Redwood National Park, California)
* `diameter_crown=*` greater than 60.4 m: the widest crown of a living single-trunk tree, a [Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/635581-widest-crown-on-a-living-tree-single-trunk) record (*Samanea saman* "Chamchuri", Thailand, 2018). Banyans (*Ficus benghalensis*), which have many trunks, can exceed the threshold
* Circumference and height greater than the record of the single species. The records are documented on the wiki page [Tree species records](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/Tree_species_records) and come from monumentaltrees.com, except the heights of *Celtis australis* and *Platanus × hispanica* (from MASAF). The limits are copied into `scripts/species_limits.json`, where `height_source` or `circumference_source` indicate a source other than monumentaltrees.com, shown on the card; the comparison uses genus and epithet, so a cultivar, an author or a rank after them uses the limits of the species (`Pyrus calleryana 'Chanticleer'` those of `Pyrus calleryana`; not a hybrid written with both parents, such as `Salix alba × fragilis`); the same holds for the synonyms in `scripts/whitelist.json` and the other spellings of their names (`x` instead of `×`), which use the limits of the name in the wiki list (for example `Platanus acerifolia` and `Platanus x hispanica` use those of `Platanus × hispanica`) and applies only if the generic check has not already been triggered
* Exaggerated height/circumference ratio: "tree too slender" if `height` is more than 100 times the circumference (e.g. `height=24`, `circumference=0.01`), "tree too stocky" if the circumference is more than 3 times `height`. The thresholds are deliberately loose, to avoid false positives: the stockiest real trees, such as monumental olive trees, are around 0.5. It does not apply if the circumference (for stocky) or the height (for slender) is already reported as over the limits, nor to stumps. The thresholds are `SLENDER_MAX` and `STOCKY_MIN` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* Tree too slender for its species: for species with a typical height/circumference ratio (`height_circ_ratio` in `scripts/species_limits.json`, the median of the OSM data on the wiki page [Tree species records](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/Tree_species_records)), the tree's ratio exceeds 4 times that value and in any case 40. It applies only to trees at least 10 m tall, because newly planted saplings are thin; it does not apply if the height is already reported or if the generic check already applies. Tested on 59,169 trees, it reported 81. The thresholds are `SLENDER_SP_FACTOR`, `SLENDER_SP_MIN` and `SLENDER_SP_MIN_H` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* For "tree too slender", "tree too slender for its species" and "tree too stocky" the small icon next to the height on the card (two trunks and a person), or the **C** key, opens a scale drawing of the trunk next to the typical trunk of a tree of the same height and to references such as a person and a house ([issue #14](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/14)): see [Trunk comparison](scheda.md#trunk-comparison). The typical trunk uses the species ratio (`height_circ_ratio`) for "tree too slender for its species" and the median of all OSM data with both measurements mapped (12: 11.8 on 368,958 trees of the regions in November 2026, `TYPICAL_HC_RATIO` in `index.html`, the same value as `_default` in `scripts/species_limits.json`) for the other two
* Very small monumental tree ([issue #34](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/34)): a tree with `denotation=natural_monument`, a monumental source (the same sources as the check on `denotation=natural_monument`, below) or that is a member of a relation with `ref:masaf` (a [homogeneous group](mappa.md#homogeneous-groups-of-the-masaf-list) of the MASAF list) that is less than 1.5 m tall or has a circumference (also from `diameter=*`) under 20 cm, the size of a small plant or a bamboo cane. The thresholds are deliberately low, to avoid false positives: in November 2026, out of 58,855 trees with `denotation=natural_monument`, 14 were under 1.5 m and 28 under 20 cm. A measurement is not reported again if the height/circumference ratio already reports it (the circumference for "tree too slender", the height for "tree too stocky"), and stumps are excluded. Monumental trees that really are this small go in `small_monument` in `scripts/whitelist.json` (node id → note). The thresholds are `SMALL_MON_HEIGHT` and `SMALL_MON_CIRC` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `circumference=*`, `height=*`, `diameter_crown=*` or `diameter=*` with a comma as decimal separator (`2,9` instead of `2.9`, also with a unit: `2,9 m`). Only when the value is a number: lists and other text with commas (`10, 12`, `66,60,51`, `14 x 16,30`) are reported only as values that are not a number (below). "Fix with JOSM" writes the point, except with exactly three digits after the comma (`1,200`), which can mean 1.2 or 1200
* `diameter=*` without a unit and less than 10 (e.g. `diameter=0.5`): without a unit the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:diameter) indicates millimetres, and it would be a trunk under 1 cm. If the value is in metres the unit must be written (`diameter=0.5 m`). These values are not used for the circumference; the check applies even if `circumference` is present. The threshold is `DIAM_MM_MIN` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `circumference=*`, `height=*`, `diameter_crown=*` or `diameter=*` with a unit written in a non-standard form: uppercase or spelled out (`2 M`, `8 metres`, `12 mt`), without the space (`150cm`, `2m`), or feet and inches written with letters (`30 ft`, `5 in`, `7 ft 4 in`, `7 feet 4 inches`) or with other quote marks than `'` and `"` (`7′4″`, `7’4”`, `7'4''`), for which `30'`, `5"` and `7'4"` are proposed. This is aligned with [Osmose](https://osmose.openstreetmap.fr/), which also reports `30 ft` (proposing `30'`) and a unit without the space (`2m`, proposing `2 m`). Spaces alone (`7' 4"`) are a hint, not an error (see below). When there is a single [standard form](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Map_features/Units) the card proposes it (`150 cm`, `30'`); for metres, which can also be written without a unit (`8` or `8 m`; for `diameter`, which without a unit is in millimetres, `2000` or `2 m`), the card does not choose between the two forms, and "Fix with JOSM" writes the one without a unit (`circumference=4.8m` → `4.8`, `diameter=2 M` → `2000`). These values are still read and used to draw the tree (`parse_length` and `unit_fixes` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`)
* Overlapping trunks: the distance between two trees is less than the sum of the radii of their trunks. Only measured trunks count (from `circumference` or `diameter`, not estimated ones) that are not already reported as too large; a tree without a measured trunk counts as a point, and is reported (with the other) if it falls inside the trunk of another tree. Stumps and trees at the same point do not count. If they are the trunks of a single tree, only one node with `multi_stemmed=*` should be mapped. This check and "Trees at the same point" (in [Other tags](#other-tags)) compare nearby trees, so they are computed after the single-tree ones (`neighbour_checks` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`)
* `circumference=*` and `diameter=*` both present but not matching: the circumference differs by more than 20% from π × diameter (with `diameter` without a unit in millimetres). The margin lets measurement inaccuracies through, such as non-round trunks. The threshold is `CIRC_DIAM_TOL` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `circumference=*`, `height=*`, `diameter_crown=*` or `diameter=*` with text, ranges, lists, unknown units or values of 0 (or negative). These values are not used to draw the tree. Values with known units (`150 cm`, `7'4"`) are not reported here and are converted to metres; a number that starts with the point (`.75`) is read as 0.75 and gets a hint (see below)

### Species and genus name

* `species=*` possibly misspelled: it is not in the species list, but is very close to it (e.g. `Olea europeea` → `Olea europaea`). Genus and epithet are compared, without the hybrid sign `×` and without cultivar or author: with a one-letter difference it is always reported, with two only if the written genus is not a known genus (so `Myrtus communis` does not become `Pyrus communis`). A species of the list in the same genus with the same letters in another order is proposed before the closest one (`Prunus serratula` → `Prunus serrulata`, not `Prunus serrula`). True species that are not in the list but look similar are excluded in `scripts/whitelist.json` (`species_typo`, e.g. the hybrid `Prunus × incam`, also with a cultivar: `Prunus × incam 'Okame'`); a name that is closer to one of them than to any species of the list is not reported either (`Quercus myrsinfolia` is one letter from `Quercus myrsinifolia`, in the whitelist, and two from `Quercus myrtifolia`, in the list). A genus followed only by a cultivar (`Magnolia 'Susan'`, `Magnolia x 'Vulcan'`) is not compared: the cultivar is not an epithet. Nor is an uppercase second word of five letters or fewer that differs by two letters from an epithet of the same genus (`Magnolia Susan` does not become `Magnolia yulan`). "Fix with JOSM" writes the proposed name in `species` when `species`, without the author, is only genus and epithet (`Chamaecyparis lawsonianna` → `Chamaecyparis lawsoniana`; not with a cultivar or a rank, which the fix would lose), and adds the missing `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` of that species. With the author too (`Cupréssus sempervìrens L.`) the fix of "Species with the author name" writes the same name (`Cupressus sempervirens`), so the two fixes agree. Only with data from the region update after this change
* Species name in a language (`species:it=*`, `species:pl=*`, `species:en=*`…) without `species=*`, i.e. without the scientific name. 2-3 letter language codes are valid, also with a variant (`species:zh-Hans`): two-letter codes must be ISO 639-1 languages (`species:mp` and `species:at` are not), three-letter ones are all accepted except `ref`, `lat`, `src`, `alt` and `old` (`LANG_2` and `NOT_LANG_3` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`); `species:wikidata` and `species:wikipedia` do not count. If the value is the name of a genus in the same language in the `genus:en` and `genus:fr` columns of the [OSM wiki list of genera](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/List_of_Genus,_Leaf_cycle,_Leaf_type) (case-insensitive; a variant such as `species:en-GB` counts as `en`), the message suggests the genus key instead: `species:fr=Tilleul` → `genus:fr=Tilleul`, plus `genus=Tilia` if `genus` is missing. Only names with no doubt are used: a name-genus pair must also be in `genus_names` in `scripts/whitelist.json`, so a name added to the wiki gives no suggestion until it is added there too. Names that the wiki list does not give to any genus come only from `genus_names`: for English `Plane` (*Platanus*, which has no English name in the wiki list; not in Scotland, where the name is also used for *Acer pseudoplatanus*: `genus_names_except` in `scripts/whitelist.json` lists, per name, the regions or countries where it gives no suggestion) and `Poplar` (*Populus*), for Japanese `ポプラ` (*Populus*), for Polish `Brzoza` (*Betula*), `Dąb` (*Quercus*), `Jesion` (*Fraxinus*), `Kasztanowiec` (*Aesculus*), `Klon` (*Acer*), `Lipa` (*Tilia*), `Modrzew` (*Larix*), `Olcha` (*Alnus*), `Platan` (*Platanus*), `Sosna` (*Pinus*), `Topola` (*Populus*), `Wiąz` (*Ulmus*) and `Wierzba` (*Salix*), for Italian `Acero` (*Acer*), `Betulla` (*Betula*), `Frassino` (*Fraxinus*), `Gelso` (*Morus*), `Noce` (*Juglans*), `Olmo` (*Ulmus*), `Ontano` (*Alnus*), `Pino` (*Pinus*), `Pioppo` (*Populus*), `Platano` (*Platanus*), `Quercia` (*Quercus*), `Salice` (*Salix*), `Tiglio` (*Tilia*), `Cipresso` (*Cupressus*), `Tamerice` (*Tamarix*), `Carpino` (*Carpinus*), `Ginepro` (*Juniperus*), `Castagno` (*Castanea*), `Faggio` (*Fagus*), `Larice` (*Larix*), `Ippocastano` (*Aesculus*) and `Tasso` (*Taxus*), so `species:it=Quercia` → `genus:it=Quercia`, plus `genus=Quercus`, and `species:pl=dąb` → `genus:pl=Dąb` (with a capital initial), plus `genus=Quercus`. A name followed by `sp.` or `spp.` counts as the name alone: `species:pl=klon sp.` → `genus:pl=Klon`, plus `genus=Acer`. If the tree has a different `genus`, the message reports the mismatch instead: `species:pl=dąb` with `genus=Acer`. The same if `genus:xx` in the same language has another name: `species:pl=Dąb` with `genus:pl=Buk`. If `genus:xx` already has that name (ignoring case and `sp.`), the message suggests removing `species:xx`. Names of a species, written by hand in `species_names` in `scripts/whitelist.json` (now English: `American Chestnut` *Castanea dentata*, `Bur Oak` *Quercus macrocarpa*, `Northern Red Oak` *Quercus rubra*, `Southern Red Oak` *Quercus falcata*; Polish: `Dąb czerwony` *Quercus rubra*, `Dąb szypułkowy` *Quercus robur*, `Jesion wyniosły` *Fraxinus excelsior*, `Klon jawor` *Acer pseudoplatanus*, `Klon zwyczajny` *Acer platanoides*, `Platan klonolistny` *Platanus × hispanica*, `Robinia akacjowa` *Robinia pseudoacacia*; Italian: 62 names of common species, among them `Bagolaro` *Celtis australis*, `Farnia` *Quercus robur*, `Ginco`/`Ginko`/`Ginkgo` *Ginkgo biloba*, `Leccio` *Quercus ilex*, `Olivo`/`Ulivo` *Olea europaea*, `Pino d'Aleppo` *Pinus halepensis*, `Platano comune` *Platanus × hispanica*), get the suggestion to add the species: `species:pl=klon zwyczajny` → `species=Acer platanoides`, `species:it=Bagolaro` → `species=Celtis australis`; with a `genus` other than the species' genus, the mismatch is reported. With a single species proposed, "Fix with JOSM" also adds the missing tags of that species: `species:wikidata` from the wiki list (for a species not in the list, the only item of species rank with that scientific name in the Wikidata cache, `data/wikidata_taxa.json`), `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle`, not `genus`, which is implicit in `species` (an existing `genus` is kept). Names that also refer to other genera or to a single species are left out (e.g. "Cedar", "Lime", "Aspen", "Horse chestnut", "Cyprès", "Marronnier", "Sorbier"), as are "Sequoia" and "Séquoia" (also used for *Sequoiadendron giganteum*). For a genus with a single species (`single_species_genera` in `scripts/whitelist.json`, now only *Cydonia*) the message suggests adding that species instead: `species:fr=Cognassier` → `species=Cydonia oblonga`, keeping `species:fr`. There is no suggestion if the name belongs to more than one genus in the wiki list (e.g. French "Palmier" or "Pin")
* Name of the taxon in a language (`taxon:en=*`, `taxon:de=*`…) without `taxon=*`: `taxon:xx` goes alongside `taxon`, not instead of it. Language codes as for `species:xx`; `taxon:cultivar` and similar keys are not languages. With the same name tables as for `species:xx`, the message suggests a key for the name: the name of a genus goes in `genus:xx` (with a capital initial, without `sp.`), plus `genus=*` if both `genus` and `species` are missing (`taxon:it=pino` → `genus:it=Pino` and `genus=Pinus`); the name of a species goes in `species:xx`, plus `species=*` if it is missing; any other name goes in `species:xx` when `species=*` is already present (`species=Citrus × aurantium` with `taxon:en=Bitter orange` → `species:en=Bitter orange`). No suggestion for a name whose `genus:xx` or `species:xx` already has another value, and none at all if the names point to different genera or species, or to a genus or species other than `genus` and `species`. "Fix with JOSM" removes `taxon:xx`, writes the new keys and, when it adds `species`, also the missing `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` of that species. Only with data from the region update after this change
* Plant that is not a tree: a `natural=tree` whose `species=*` (genus and epithet, also with author or cultivar) or `species:wikidata=*` indicates a plant that is not a tree, now only the century plant (`Agave americana`, `species:wikidata=Q161115`); one of the two tags is enough. Plants that are not trees are mapped with `natural=plant`, which "Fix with JOSM" proposes. Stumps are excluded. The plants are in `not_trees` in `scripts/whitelist.json` (species → QID)
* `species=*` with wrong capitalisation: an uppercase epithet (`Catalpa Bignonioides`, `Taxus Baccata`), an uppercase letter inside the epithet (`Ginkgo biloBa`) or inside the genus after the initial (`GINKGO biloba`, `GinKgo biloba`), or a hybrid sign written as an uppercase `X` between genus and epithet (`Crataegus X prunifolia` → `Crataegus × prunifolia`; a lowercase `x` and an `X` at the start of the name are not reported). The name with only the initial of the genus uppercase is suggested. With an uppercase epithet initial it is genus and epithet as in the species list, and what follows the epithet stays as written (`Pyrus Communis 'Beech Hill'` → `Pyrus communis 'Beech Hill'`); for two-word names with a known genus that are not in the list, it is the same words in lowercase with the genus initial capitalised, only when the second word is the epithet of a species in the list, of any genus (`Prunus Chinensis` → `Prunus chinensis`), when the name is one or two letters away from a species in the list (see "Species possibly misspelled"), or when the whole name is uppercase (`PRUNUS COMUNIS` → `Prunus comunis`); the other two-word names (`Malus Zierform`, `Tilia Greenspire`) are reported as "Non-scientific species", without a suggestion. In the other cases only for names in the species list and two-word names with a known genus: the suggestion fixes genus and epithet and keeps the rest (cultivar) as written. Cultivars in quotes do not count. The suggestion also capitalises the genus initial, so with this error "Species starting lowercase" is not repeated (`quercus Robur`). With an author in the name (`Ginkgo biloBa L.`, `quercus robur L.`) only "Species with the author name" is reported, and its suggestion also fixes the capitalisation (`Ginkgo biloba`, `Quercus robur`), so the two suggestions never disagree
* `species=*` that does not have the form of a scientific name (Genus epithet) because the second word is uppercase: common names (`Pino Marittimo`, `Olive Tree`, `Black Matipo`) or cultivars without quotes (`Malus Golden Hornet`, `Tilia Greenspire`). Names for which "Species with wrong capitalisation" has a suggestion are reported there instead. If the scientific name is written in parentheses (`Black Matipo (Pittosporum tenuifolium)`) the card suggests it. If the whole value is the name of a species in `species_names` of `scripts/whitelist.json` (see "species:xx without species", in any case and any language of the list, for one species only), the card suggests that species and moves the common name to `species:xx` of its language if that tag is missing: `species=American Chestnut` → `species=Castanea dentata`, `species:en=American Chestnut`; "Fix with JOSM" writes them, with the missing `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` of the species (no suggestion if `genus` is another genus; only with data from the region update after this change). Otherwise, if the whole value is the common name of a genus (see "Non-scientific genus") or a common name whose genus is certain even though it can also mean a species (`Horse Chestnut`: *Aesculus*, list `genus_of_names` in `scripts/whitelist.json`), the card suggests `genus=*` without `species`, and "Fix with JOSM" removes `species` and adds `genus` if it is missing (no fix if `genus` is another genus); a common name of a genus in one language only also moves to `genus:xx` of that language, if missing (not the names of `genus_of_names`). Cultivars in quotes and authors do not count
* Parentheses in `species=*`: no scientific name contains them. Not repeated if the parentheses are already explained by the checks on the author or on the non-scientific name
* `species=*` indicating an unidentified species (`Pinus sp.`, `Pinus spp`): `genus=Pinus` without `species` should be used
* `species=*` with the author of the scientific name (`Phoenix dactylifera L.`, `Camellia sinensis (L.) Kuntze`, `Picea abies (L.) H. Karst.`): the card suggests the name without the author, keeping infraspecific ranks (`var.`, `subsp.`…) and cultivars in quotes. Author names without a dot count as the author when another author, a joining word (`et`, `ex`, `&`) or a rank follows (`Taxus cuspidata Siebold et Zucc.`, `Betula platyphylla Sukaczev var. japonica`), or after a rank in a name that already has an author (`Salix hultenii Flod. var. angustifolia Kimura`). Parentheses with lowercase words are not an author (`Ulmus laevis (Ulmus pedenculata, Ulmus effusa)`: parentheses in `species=*`). Uppercase words in parentheses are an author when they have an abbreviation with a dot or a joining word (`(L.)`, `(Siebold & Zucc.)`) or when another author follows (`Malus toringo (Siebold) Siebold ex de Vriese`); otherwise, at the end of the name, they are not (`Quercus robur (Farnia)`, `Hechtia sp. 10429 (Lunel)`: parentheses in `species=*`). If, without the author, something other than the scientific name is left (`Insieme omogeneo di Fagus sylvatica L.`), the card reports the author without a suggestion. "Fix with JOSM" writes the name without the author and adds the missing `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` of that species (`species=Quercus petraea (Matt.) Liebl.` → `species=Quercus petraea`, `species:wikidata=Q158608`; only with data from the region update after this change); if the name also has a spelling error, it writes the name proposed for it (see "Species possibly misspelled"). It applies only if the second word is lowercase, as in every scientific name, so as not to mistake common names for authors (`Black Matipo (Pittosporum tenuifolium)`)
* Underscore (`_`) in `species=*` or `genus=*` instead of a space
* Multiple values separated by `;` in `species=*` or `genus=*` (`genus=Crataegus;Quercus`): a `natural=tree` node is a single tree, with a single genus and a single species
* Tag written inside a value: `=` in `species`, `genus`, `taxon`, `name`, `start_date`, `ref:masaf` or `ref:rami` (e.g. `genus=leaf_type=broadleaved`). Links (`image`, `website`, `url`) are not checked, because `=` is normal in addresses. With `=` in `species` or `genus` the checks on the form of the name are not repeated, since they would be triggered by chance
* `species=*` or `genus=*` in a non-Latin script (e.g. `genus=череша`): scientific names are written in the Latin script, the name in the local language goes in `species:xx`. Accented Latin letters are not reported. As with `=`, the checks on the form of the name are not repeated on that tag and `genus` is not compared with `species`
* `species=*` made of a single word. A leading hybrid sign written as a separate word (`×`, `x` or `X`) does not count (`x carnea` is a single word), and a genus followed only by the author counts as a single word (`Tilia L.`). If the word is a genus of the wiki list, in any case (`species=magnolia`), the card says so and proposes `genus=Magnolia` without `species`. If it is the common name of a genus, in any language of the names used for `species:xx` (see above) and for one genus only (`species=oak`), the card proposes that genus: `genus=Quercus` without `species`. When the name belongs to one language only and `genus:xx` for that language is missing, the common name moves there instead of being removed, with an uppercase initial (`species=Brzoza` → `genus=Betula`, `genus:pl=Brzoza`). In both cases "Fix with JOSM" removes `species`, adds `genus` if it is missing and, for a common name, adds that `genus:xx` (no fix with a different `genus`)
* `genus=*` made of more than one word. The leading hybrid sign does not count: `× Sycoparrotia` (intergeneric hybrid genus) is a single word, and it does not count for the capital letter and for the comparison with `species` either
* `species=*` or `genus=*` starting with a lowercase letter (for `species` only with more than one word: with a single word the previous error is enough). A leading hybrid sign written `x` is not a lowercase initial: `x Chitalpa tashkentensis` is not reported
* `genus=*` that is not a known genus but the common name of a genus, in one of the languages of the names used for `species:xx` (see above) and for one genus only, in any case: `genus=wierzba` (Polish for *Salix*), `genus=Oak`. The card names the language when the name belongs to only one, and proposes the scientific name, which "Fix with JOSM" writes (`genus=Salix`). When the name belongs to one language only and `genus:xx` for that language is missing, the common name moves there with an uppercase initial (`genus=świerk` → `genus=Picea`, `genus:pl=Świerk`). With this error "Genus starting lowercase" is not repeated, nor "Genus differs from species" when `species` is of that genus
* `genus=*` different from the first word of `species=*` (the leading hybrid sign, `×` or `x`, is ignored); not checked if `species` is a single lowercase word (e.g. `species=alba`, `species=x carnea`), which is not a genus, if it is a hybrid sign followed by a single uppercase word that is not a known genus (`species=x Sepulcralis`), or if `genus` or `species` has more than one value (`genus=Crataegus;Quercus`). When `genus` is not a known genus, the genus of `species` is, and the two differ by at most two letters (`genus=Styphlonobium` with `species=Styphnolobium japonicum`), the card proposes the genus of `species`, which "Fix with JOSM" writes (`genus=Styphnolobium`)

### Wikidata

* `species:wikidata=*` inconsistent with `species=*` according to the [OSM wiki species list](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/List_of_Species): a QID different from the one expected for the species, or a QID that belongs to another species. It is not reported if on Wikidata the scientific name (P225) or the basionym ([P566](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P566)) of the item is exactly `species` (the list may use the other name: for example `Mespilus germanica` and `Crataegus germanica`). Other accepted QIDs (for example synonyms) are added, per species, in `scripts/whitelist.json`; a synonym listed there (for example `Camphora officinarum` for `Cinnamomum camphora`) accepts the QID of the name in the wiki list. Not reported either when `species` has a spelling error and the QID belongs to the species proposed for it (`Platanus *hispanica` with the QID of `Platanus × hispanica`): only the spelling error is reported
* `species:wikidata=*` or `genus:wikidata=*` that on Wikidata are not a taxon, i.e. do not have a scientific name ([P225](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P225)), for example an illustration, a fruit or a single monumental tree, or nonexistent items. If it triggers on `species:wikidata`, the previous check on the same key is not repeated
* `wikidata=*` that is a taxon: `wikidata` indicates the individual tree, the species goes in `species:wikidata`
* `wikipedia=*` whose article is linked to a Wikidata item that is a taxon: `wikipedia` indicates the article about the individual tree, the species article goes in `species:wikipedia`, the genus article (an item of genus rank, such as `wikipedia=en:Holly`, *Ilex*) in `genus:wikipedia`. The item of the article is read from the Wikipedia API at every update (`data/wikipedia_qids.json`, as for `species:wikipedia`)
* `species:wikidata=*` that on Wikidata has the rank of genus or higher (section, family…), or `genus:wikidata=*` with the rank of species or lower (property [P105](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P105)). With a single `species:wikidata` of genus rank, no `genus:wikidata` and no `genus` of another genus, the card suggests moving the item to `genus:wikidata`, plus `genus=*` with the item's scientific name when both `genus` and `species` are missing; "Fix with JOSM" does it (`species:wikidata=Q45417383` → `genus:wikidata=Q45417383`, `genus=Tilia`)
* Scientific name on Wikidata ([P225](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P225)) of the item of `species:wikidata=*` or `genus:wikidata=*` different from `species=*` or `genus=*`. It applies to all species, not only those in the wiki list; it is not repeated if the same error is already reported by the list. Genus and epithet are compared, without `×`; the item's basionym (P566) and the QID that the wiki list associates with the species are also accepted; the QIDs in `whitelist.json` are accepted
* Species of the tree on Wikidata (property [P10241](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P10241), "individual of taxon", of the item of `wikidata=*`) different from `species:wikidata=*` and from `species=*`; the synonyms in `scripts/whitelist.json` count as the same species (`Platanus × hispanica` on Wikidata with `species=Platanus acerifolia` is not reported)
* `wikidata=*` whose Wikidata item has coordinates (P625) more than 1 km from the tree (if it has more than one, the nearest counts; items without coordinates or that are a taxon are not checked). The threshold is `WD_MAX_DIST` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `wikidata=*` whose item has no coordinates, but has an image (P18) with coordinates on Commons more than 500 m from the tree (the subject's position if the file gives one, otherwise the camera position; same `COMMONS_MAX_DIST` threshold as the check on Commons photos). If the same photo is also linked to the tree (`wikimedia_commons` or `image`), only that check examines it

### Links, sources and monumental trees

* Links with an invalid format: `wikidata` other than `Q…`, `wikimedia_commons` without `File:` or `Category:`, `panoramax` that is not a UUID, `image` that does not start with `http` (or that is not a single Commons file, `File:…`: see the hints) or that has more than one address separated by `;`
* `website=*` or `url=*` (a single value) that is a Wikipedia article (also on the mobile site, `fr.m.wikipedia.org`): articles have their own tag, `wikipedia=*`, which gives the article about the single tree. Without `wikipedia=*`, the card says that an article about this tree is written `wikipedia=<language>:<title>`, while an article about a place or a list of trees does not go in `wikipedia=*`; there is no "Fix with JOSM", because the choice depends on the article. With `wikipedia=*` of the same article (underscores and the case of the first letter do not count) `website`/`url` can be removed, and "Fix with JOSM" removes it; with a different article the card shows both
* Unreachable links in `image`, `website` and `url` (nonexistent page, 404 or 410, or a site that no longer exists) and `wikimedia_commons=File:…` files that do not exist on Commons. 403, 429, server errors and timeouts are not reported, as they are often blocks against automated tools. Links on the same site are checked one at a time, and each link is rechecked at most every 30 days (`URL_RECHECK_DAYS`)
* Linked Commons files (`wikimedia_commons=File:…`, or `image=*` pointing to a file page on Commons or to the file address on upload.wikimedia.org, also of a thumbnail) whose coordinates are more than 500 m from the tree. The subject's position (*object location*) is used if the file gives one, otherwise the camera position (*camera location*); files without coordinates are not checked. Coordinates are read from the Commons API and re-read at most every 30 days, like links. The threshold is `COMMONS_MAX_DIST` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `ref:masaf`, `ref:rami`, `ref:monumentaltrees`, `ref:CRFOP` or `ref:gid` (Poland, [issue #8](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/8)), `ref:usop` (Czechia, code of the ÚSOP register in the [DRUSOP](https://drusop.nature.cz/portal/) database), `heritage:operator=icnf` (Portugal) or `heritage:operator=anf` (Luxembourg), `website`/`url` pointing to monumentaltrees.com, or `wikidata=*` whose item has the monumentaltrees.com ID ([P9704](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P9704)) or is a direct instance (P31) of monumental plant ([Q811534](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q811534)) or has a heritage designation ([P1435](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P1435)), without `denotation=natural_monument` (a monumental tree without a source is not reported). All the sources of a tree are listed in a single message. P9704, P31 and P1435 are read from Wikidata at every update, together with the other data of the item. On stumps (`natural=tree_stump`) `ref:CRFOP` and `ref:gid` are not reported. Being a member of a relation with `ref:masaf` (a [homogeneous group](mappa.md#homogeneous-groups-of-the-masaf-list) of the MASAF list) is not a source: those trees are mapped as the wiki says, with their own `denotation`. A member with a `ref:masaf` of its own (or another source) on its tags gets this check like any other tree. Relations are not checked: removed or unknown codes on a relation give no error
* Tags of a national register of monumental trees on a tree of a region in another country: `ref:masaf`, `masaf:criteria` and `ref:rami` (Italy), `ref:icnf` and `heritage:operator=icnf` (Portugal), `heritage:operator=anf` (Luxembourg), `ref:CRFOP` and `ref:gid` (Poland), `ref:usop` (Czechia). The country is the one of the region (`iso` in `scripts/regions.json`); stumps are checked too. The list is `NATIONAL_TAGS` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`
* `ref:masaf=*` of a tree that the MASAF list of monumental trees of Italy gives as removed (due to natural death, felling, loss of requirements or downgrading): the card gives the reason, and the recognition row becomes "Formerly listed in the MASAF list of monumental trees of Italy…". It does not apply to stumps (`natural=tree_stump`), for which the code of a removed tree is normal. Codes are compared without spaces and ignoring leading zeros (`03/G114/CN/01` = `003/G114/CN/01`). The list is in `scripts/masaf_list.json`, produced with `scripts/update_masaf.py` from the KML file that MASAF publishes on the [list page](https://www.masaf.gov.it/flex/cm/pages/ServeBLOB.php/L/IT/IDPagina/11260); source: Masaf - Direzione generale dell'economia montana e delle foreste - Difor4 - "dataset AMI - Censimento alberi monumentali d'Italia vs. 11_2024", licence CC BY 4.0
* `ref:masaf=*` with a code that is not in the MASAF list, neither among the listed trees nor among the removed ones. Codes are compared without spaces; the first number can have two or three digits (`02/A002/RM/02` = `002/A002/RM/02`), but a single digit is reported (`2/A002/RM/02`). Stumps are checked too; not on trees of regions in other countries, where the previous check on national registers already reports the tag
* `masaf:criteria=*` with invalid codes: only `a`–`g`, `a1`, `a2`, `a2.1`, `a2.2`, `a2.3` are allowed, separated by commas without spaces (see the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/IT:Key:ref:masaf#Criteri_di_monumentalit%C3%A0)); spaces, uppercase, `;` and empty parts are also reported

### Other tags

* `tree=*` or `type=*`, tags not documented for trees: check whether they can be replaced with another tag. `type=palm` is not reported, because it needs to be fixed in bulk
* Misspelled key ([issue #32](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/32)): a key that is not one of those used for trees, but differs from one of them only in capitalisation (`Species`, `HEIGHT`), by one letter (`leaftype`, `leaf type`, `species_wikidata`) or by two adjacent letters swapped (`heigth`); for keys of at least 12 letters two differences are allowed (`circumfence` → `circumference`). Keys shorter than 5 letters (`name`, `sex`) are reported only for capitalisation, because one letter makes another word; for `ref:…` keys the part after `ref:` counts, so `ref:gid`, `ref:rami`, `ref:icnf` and `ref:usop` are short keys too (`ref:uid` is another key, `ref:massaf` is reported). Subkeys of known keys (`species:wikipedia:it`) and numbered variants (`image2`, `panoramax:1`) are not reported. The card suggests the known key with the same value; the value is not used to draw the tree. The known keys are `KNOWN_KEYS` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`; none of the 146 keys most used with `natural=tree` on Taginfo is reported
* `fixme=*` (also `FIXME=*`, [issue #85](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/85)): the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:fixme) uses it to describe something to check or correct, and the tag is removed once that is resolved. The card shows the value. Empty values are not reported; stumps are checked too. Only with data from the region update after this change
* `start_date=*` invalid or in the future. The forms of the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:start_date) are accepted: `1850`, `1850-05`, `1850-05-01`, `~1850`, `1850s`, `early`/`mid`/`late 1850s`, `C19`, `early`/`mid`/`late C19`, `before 1850`, `after 1850`, years BC (`500 BC`) and the ranges `1850..1900` (both ends are checked). Other forms are reported (`05/03/1990`, `c. 1888`, `2020;2021`, years of one or two digits, months or days of one digit such as `1990-5-3`, more than one prefix such as `before after 1850`, the century with a lowercase `c`), as are ranges that end before they start (`1900..1800`; a decade or a century counts up to its last year, so `1890s..C19` is accepted), dates that do not exist (`2020-02-30`, `2025-06-00`) and dates after the day of the data update (the oldest date indicated counts, also with `before`: a mapped tree already exists, so `before 2030` is reported). With `=` in the value only the check on the tag written inside a value is reported
* Trees at the same point: two or more `natural=tree` nodes with exactly the same coordinates (stumps do not count). The card lists the other nodes
* `leaf_type=*` or `leaf_cycle=*` (valid) different from those of the species in the species list or, if the species is not there, of the genus in the [genus list](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:natural%3Dtree/List_of_Genus,_Leaf_cycle,_Leaf_type). `deciduous` and `semi_deciduous` are considered equivalent, as are `evergreen` and `semi_evergreen`; `leafless` is not compared. For palms (recognised as described in [The map](mappa.md#rendering): `taxon:family=Arecaceae`, a palm genus, etc.) both `palm` and `broadleaved` are accepted. `leaf_type=palm` is also accepted for palm-like plants that are not palms (`PALM_LIKE_GENERA` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`: *Yucca*, *Cordyline*, *Dracaena*, *Cycas*, *Musa*, tree ferns…). For *Tamarix* both `needleleaved` and `broadleaved` are accepted, and for *Cycas* `needleleaved`, `broadleaved` and `palm` (`leaf_types` in `scripts/whitelist.json`). On any other tree that the lists give as `broadleaved` (for example *Ginkgo* or *Morus*), `leaf_type=palm` is reported; for this case only, when the tree has neither `species=*` nor `genus=*`, the genus is taken from the first word of `taxon=*` (`taxon=Ginkgo biloba`).
* `leaf_type=*` other than `broadleaved`, `needleleaved`, `leafless`, `palm`
* `leaf_cycle=*` other than `deciduous`, `semi_deciduous`, `evergreen`, `semi_evergreen`
* `denotation=*` rare or invalid. Each part of a multiple value (`natural_monument;landmark`) is checked on its own, against the number of uses on nodes according to [Taginfo](https://taginfo.openstreetmap.org/keys/denotation#values). It is reported if:
  * it has an invalid format: characters other than lowercase letters, digits and `_` (for example `=`, `:`, `/`, spaces, uppercase) or it does not start with a letter;
  * it has spaces before or after it (`urban; natural_monument`): multiple values are separated by `;` without spaces. "Fix with JOSM" removes the spaces;
  * it is a common value (at least 1000 uses) with other text attached without `_`, for example `urbanheidialhoranta` or `single1953`;
  * it is used at most 10 times.

  The thresholds are at the top of `scripts/fetch_trees.py` (`DEN_RARE_MAX`, `DEN_COMMON_MIN`). The format check always applies; the other two require `scripts/denotation_values.json`.

### General notes

The list-based checks require `scripts/species_list.json`, `scripts/genus_list.json` and `scripts/denotation_values.json`, which are generated by the "Update reference lists" Action; if the files are missing, the checks are not run.

If `circumference=*` is missing but `diameter=*` is present, the circumference is derived as π × diameter. As the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:diameter) states, `diameter` without a unit is in millimetres (unlike `circumference` and `diameter_crown`, in metres); written units (`mm`, `cm`, `m`, `ft`, `in`) are respected. Values without a unit below 10 are not used (see the check above). The derived circumference goes through the same checks and estimates; if both tags are present, `diameter` is ignored (but the checks on the format of numeric values still apply to `diameter` too). One exception: when `circumference` is not a readable number (`circumference=big`), the 43 m limit is checked on `diameter` (`diameter=20000`); the tree is still drawn without a measured trunk.

The checks are computed by `scripts/fetch_trees.py` and saved in the `issues` field of `data/regions/<region>.geojson.gz`.

## Hints

Besides errors, the tree card can show hints: information that could improve the data, not errors to fix. They appear after the errors, preceded by ⓘ, in grey and not bold; they do not colour the tree on the map, are not in the errors menu and do not count in the ranking. Only in the QA tool, not in the monumental trees map. This is also where checks that would give too many false positives as errors live.

* No `species` or `genus`, but the tree's Wikidata item (`wikidata=*`) indicates the species ([P10241](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Property:P10241), not of genus rank or above): `species` could be added with that name, after a survey on the ground
* `species=*`, but the tree's Wikidata item (`wikidata=*`) only indicates its genus (P10241, for example `Vitis` with `species=Vitis vinifera`): it is not an error on OSM, but the item could be made more precise on Wikidata, citing a source compatible with the Wikidata licence (OSM data is not, so the OSM value cannot be copied there). In this case "Species differs from Wikidata" is not reported
* `species:wikidata=*` (a taxon, not of genus rank or above) without `species`: `species` could be added with the item's scientific name (P225)
* `species` in the wiki species list, without `species:wikidata`: the QID from the list could be added
* `species:wikipedia=*` without `species:wikidata` (when the list does not already give the QID, see above): `species:wikidata` could be added with the QID of the item linked to the Wikipedia article
* `species:wikipedia=*` without `species` (and without the two hints on `species` above): `species` could be added with the scientific name (P225) of the item linked to the article
* For both, the article must have a Wikidata item that is a species (a taxon, not of genus rank or higher). Not the other way round: `species:wikidata` without `species:wikipedia` is not reported
* The hints that suggest a value for `species` appear only if the scientific name has at least two words: a single word (`Pinus`) is a genus, not a species
* `species:it=*` with a lowercase initial (each value, if there are several separated by `;`): on OSM 98% of the values have a capital (for example `Platano comune`), so a capital is suggested. It is a hint and not an error because it is not a rule written on the wiki
* `name:en=*` with a lowercase initial (`the big oak`): in English a proper name starts with a capital letter, which "Fix with JOSM" writes (`The big oak`)
* `note=*` (also `NOTE=*`, [issue #85](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/85)): the card shows the note for mappers. Language variants (`note:it`) do not count. It also appears for stumps
* `taxon=*`: the tag does not say which rank the name belongs to, and the message suggests a more precise tag if possible (`species`, `genus`, `taxon:cultivar`…). If the value is genus and epithet of a species in the wiki species list (without a cultivar or anything else), or a genus of the wiki genus list, the card says so and "Fix with JOSM" writes it in `species` or `genus` (with the name as in the list) and removes `taxon`; if `species` or `genus` already has that name, it only removes `taxon`. A value that is not in the list but is a known genus (capital initial) followed by a lowercase epithet, possibly with `×` (`taxon=Citrus sinensis`), has the form of a species name: the card suggests `species` with the same value and asks to check the name (synonyms, hybrid sign), and the fix moves it as written. `sp`, `spp`, `var` and similar words are not epithets. No fix if `species` or `genus` indicate something else (another species, another genus)
* Hybrid sign written with the letter `x` (or `X`) as a separate word in `species=*` or `genus=*`, at the start or inside the name (`x Chitalpa tashkentensis`, `Platanus x hispanica`): the sign `×` is suggested, and "Fix with JOSM" replaces it. Not with multiple values or with an error on the form of the name, which already proposes its own fix. The page computes it from the tags, so it appears without waiting for the region update
* `species=*` that includes a variety or a cultivar, or has the genus abbreviated ([issue #68](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/68)); the cases can be combined:
  * a variety, `var.` (also `var` without the point) followed by a lowercase epithet (`Quercus robur var. fastigiata`): it could be written as `species=Quercus robur` with `taxon:variety=fastigiata`. "Fix with JOSM" writes it only if the name is a taxon of variety rank on Wikidata, among those in `data/wikidata_taxa.json` (`Populus alba var. pyramidalis`; property `sp_var_ok`); otherwise the hint has no fix and adds to check that it is a variety and not a cultivar, which goes in `taxon:cultivar`: cultivars are found written with `var.` too (`Pyrus calleryana var. chanticleer`)
  * a cultivar in quotation marks (`Acer palmatum 'Atropurpureum'`, also after `cv.`): `species=Acer palmatum` with `taxon:cultivar=Atropurpureum` (the cultivar without quotation marks)
  * a cultivar without quotation marks after genus and epithet of a species in the wiki species list (`Pyrus calleryana Chanticleer`, `Tilia cordata Green Spire`): only words after the epithet, the first with a capital and at least three letters, none of them an author (with the point, or one of the authors written without it in `AUTHOR_NAMES` of `scripts/fetch_trees.py`: `Betula pendula Roth`), a rank or a hybrid sign. Not when the word after the epithet is lowercase (`Quercus robur fastigiata`)
  * a genus of the wiki genus list with only the cultivar (`Magnolia 'Susan'`): `genus=Magnolia` with `taxon:cultivar=Susan` instead of `species`; the fix removes `species` and adds `genus` if missing. Not with a different `genus`
  * the genus abbreviated to its initial (`T. baccata 'Fastigiata'`, `Z. serrata`): the genus written in full, `species=Taxus baccata`. The genus is that of `genus=*`, if it starts with that letter and is a known genus (or gives a species of the list with the epithet); without `genus`, the only genus of the wiki species list with that initial and a species with that epithet (`Z. serrata` → `Zelkova serrata`; none for `C. japonica`, which has more than one)

  "Fix with JOSM" writes these tags and, for the species left in `species`, the `species:wikidata`, `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle` that are missing. The rest of the name (a subspecies) stays as it is. If `taxon:variety` or `taxon:cultivar` (or `variety`, `cultivar`) already has that value, case and quotation marks aside, the fix only shortens `species`; with a different value there is no hint. Not with more than one variety or cultivar, with multiple values, when the whole value is a name of the wiki species list, with the author in `species`, another error on its form or the hybrid sign written `x`, which already propose another `species`
* `ref:masaf=*` without `masaf:criteria`: the criteria that the MASAF list gives for that code could be added (from `scripts/masaf_trees.json`)
* Only in Italian regions: a tree without `denotation=natural_monument` or `landmark` and without `ref:masaf`, with a circumference (from `circumference`, or from `diameter`) at least equal to the MASAF indicative threshold for its species: it could be a monumental tree. The thresholds are the MASAF "minimum indicative circumferences for the size criterion" (January 2020), transcribed in `scripts/masaf_thresholds.json`, only for the species in the table (for Betula, which has 200 or 300 cm, 300 is used). The hint is not shown if the tree has "tree too slender", "tree too slender for its species" or "tree too stocky", nor for the members of a relation with `ref:masaf` ([homogeneous groups](mappa.md#homogeneous-groups-of-the-masaf-list)), which already count as monumental. These trees also appear on the map of monumental trees, with a note on the card, unless the circumference has an error
* `source=*` of a tree import listed in `scripts/imports.json` ([issue #2](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/2)): "Tree from an undocumented import" for imports without documentation, "Imported tree" with the link to the wiki page for documented ones. The comparison ignores case, surrounding spaces and the trailing slash; with several values separated by `;` one is enough
* `genus=*` of a genus with a single living species (the others are only fossils), without `species`: `species` could be added with that species (for example `genus=Ginkgo` → `species=Ginkgo biloba`). The genera are in a hand-written list (`MONOTYPIC` in `index.html`): Ginkgo, Metasequoia, Sequoiadendron, Sequoia, Cryptomeria, Sciadopitys, Glyptostrobus, Platycladus, Thujopsis, Pseudolarix, Wollemia, Fitzroya, Davidia, Idesia, Eucommia, Tetracentron, Trochodendron, Oxydendrum, Franklinia, Umbellularia. For `genus=Sequoia` in Italy, French-speaking and English-speaking countries (from the region's ISO code: IT, FR, BE, LU, CH, MC, GB, IE, US, CA, AU, NZ) a second hint adds that there the common name "sequoia" is often used for the giant sequoia (*Sequoiadendron giganteum*) too, so the tree should be checked
* A tree without `species=*` at 1800 m of elevation or more (`ELEV_HINT` in `index.html`): above that height there are few tree species, and the species could be identified on site and added. Only in the regions with elevations (see [Elevation](dati.md#elevation); for now Piedmont), and not when another hint already suggests a species (from Wikidata, Wikipedia or a genus with a single living species). The elevation comes from a model of the surface, so it is approximate: the hint says "about"
* `circumference=*`, `height=*`, `diameter_crown=*` or `diameter=*` in feet and inches with spaces (`7' 4"`): the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Map_features/Units) writes them without spaces (`7'4"`). It is a hint and not an error because the value is unambiguous and validators such as Osmose accept it; it also appears for stumps
* `circumference=*`, `height=*`, `diameter_crown=*` or `diameter=*` with a number that starts with the point (`circumference=.75`, `height=.5 m`): the value is read as it is (0.75), and the form with the zero in front is suggested (`0.75`, `0.5 m`), which "Fix with JOSM" writes. It is a hint and not an error because the value is unambiguous and it is not a rule written on the wiki. Not for `diameter` without a unit, where the error on a diameter too small for millimetres already applies, nor with a unit in a non-standard form, where that error proposes its own fix. The page computes it from the tags, so it appears without waiting for the region update; it also appears for stumps
* `leaf_type` or `leaf_cycle` missing, with the species or genus in the wiki lists: the values from the lists could be added (wrong ones are already errors). If the species is not in the species list, the values come from the genus and the hint names the genus (e.g. «For Ulmus, …»). `leaf_type=palm` is never proposed: for palms (drawn as palms all the same) the hint and "Fix with JOSM" propose the value of the lists, usually `broadleaved`, and nothing when the palm is in neither list; an existing `leaf_type=palm` on a palm is not reported nor replaced ([issue #55](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/55); only with data from the region update after this change)
* `image=*` pointing to a Wikimedia Commons file (the file page on commons.wikimedia.org, the file address on upload.wikimedia.org, also of a thumbnail, or the file name alone, `image=File:…`), without `wikimedia_commons=*`: `image` could be replaced with `wikimedia_commons=File:…`; "Fix with JOSM" adds `wikimedia_commons` and removes `image` (only if `image` has a single value). It also appears for stumps
* `image=*` (a single value) that is a web page and not an image file: a page of Google Drive (not `drive.google.com/uc`, which gives the file), Google Maps (`maps.app.goo.gl`), `goo.gl`, Mapillary, Flickr, Geograph, Imgur, Wikiloc, Twitter/X, Facebook, Instagram, OneDrive or Wikipedia. Addresses that give the file are not reported (`i.imgur.com`, `live.staticflickr.com`, `images.mapillary.com`), nor Google Photos links (`photos.app.goo.gl`, `photos.google.com`), which are accepted. The card says that a photo can also be uploaded to Wikimedia Commons or Panoramax. For Mapillary the card says that it has its own tag, `mapillary=*`; for the page of a Mapillary photo (`mapillary.com/map/im/<key>` or `mapillary.com/app/?pKey=<id>`), without `mapillary=*`, it suggests `mapillary=<id>` and "Fix with JOSM" writes it and removes `image`. The sites are `IMAGE_PAGE_SITES` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`. It also appears for stumps
* `wikidata=*` without `denotation=natural_monument` or `landmark`: a tree with a Wikidata item is notable, and could have one of the two (it does not appear if an error already says so, for example protection on Wikidata)
* `species=Ginkgo biloba` (or `genus=Ginkgo` without `species`) without `sex=*`, only from April to November (October to May in the southern hemisphere, from the tree's latitude): `sex=female` could be added if the tree is female, with a note on when and how to check ([issue #25](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/25)). The words "foul-smelling seeds" are underlined with a dashed line: a click or tap opens a photo of the seeds ([File:Gingko biloba seeds.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gingko_biloba_seeds.jpg)) with the Commons credits, as for the card photos (loaded only when opened); a second click, a click elsewhere or Esc closes it
* A tree of another dioecious species or genus with known traits, without `sex=*` ([issue #27](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/27)): `sex=female` or `sex=male` could be added if the sex can be recognised; the hint gives the traits of females and males, the same as in the ♂ / ♀ box of the [tree card](scheda.md). It appears only in the months when at least one of the two sexes can be recognised (shifted by six months in the southern hemisphere, from the tree's latitude), and names that period; for the species that can be recognised all year it always appears, without a period. The species is that of `species=*` (also followed by a variety or cultivar), otherwise the genus of `genus=*` or of `species=*`; trees with more than one species (`;`) have no hint. The months, for the northern hemisphere (`SEX_SEASON` in `index.html`): Taxus baccata February–October, Ilex aquifolium May–February, Laurus nobilis March–November, Acer negundo March–December, Ceratonia siliqua August–November, Populus (and Populus nigra, with its own traits) and Salix March–June, Broussonetia April–September, Cephalotaxus March–October, Cercidiphyllum March–December, Chamaerops April–November, Gymnocladus May–March, Hippophae August–April, Maclura May–November, Phellodendron June–January, Pistacia March–November, Torreya April–October, Trachycarpus April–December, Ailanthus altissima June–February, Diospyros lotus and Diospyros virginiana May–December, Juniperus virginiana September–April, Phytolacca dioica May–November; all year for Juniperus communis, Juniperus oxycedrus, Phoenix, Podocarpus, Araucaria araucana and Schinus molle. The page computes it from the tags, so it appears without waiting for the region update

The hints on Wikidata, Wikipedia, `species:it`, `name:en`, `note`, `taxon`, variety and cultivar, MASAF and imports are computed in `hint_props` of `scripts/fetch_trees.py` (properties `hint_sp`, `hint_wg`, `hint_sn`, `hint_sw`, `hint_wp`, `hint_wn`, `hint_it`, `hint_ne`, `hint_nt`, `hint_tx`, `sp_base` / `sp_gen` / `sp_var` / `sp_cv` / `sp_abbr`, `hint_mc`, `hint_ms`, `hint_imp_u` / `hint_imp_d`) and arrive with the next update of the region; the others in the page (`hints` in `index.html`) from the tree's data.
