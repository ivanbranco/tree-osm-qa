# The map

How trees are drawn, what the filters contain, the card and the panel tabs (legend, completeness, record trees, ranking, trend), and how the language works.

## Rendering

The maximum zoom is 19, the last level of the openstreetmap.org tiles: beyond that, the background would only be enlarged and blurred.

* **Crown**: a circle with the real size from `diameter_crown=*` (like every circle on the map, trunks included, it is scaled at the tree's latitude, so it matches buildings and roads of the base map); if missing, a fallback radius (3 m; 5 m for `denotation=natural_monument` and `landmark`, 2.5 m for `agricultural` and `orchard`, 2 m for `parking` and `decoration`), varied by ±15% at random but always the same for the same tree, so that the map is not uniform (except for monumental trees and landmarks, which stay at 5 m), and a more transparent fill. The crown is not estimated from circumference or height: the estimates tried (median crown/circumference ratio, UrbanEye3D method) turned out too large. The shape depends on `leaf_type=*` (if missing or invalid, it is estimated from the OSM wiki lists: from the species or, if that is not there, from the genus):
  * round if `leaf_type` is missing (or has another value, such as `leafless`)
  * rounded, lobed, if `broadleaved`
  * 8, 9 or 10 lance-shaped fronds with deep notches between them, if the tree is a palm ([issue #55](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/55)); the number of fronds, the rotation and the length of each frond (±8%) are fixed for each tree, and the crown area stays the same as the circle. The whole circle of the crown stays clickable, including the notches. A tree is a palm with `leaf_type=palm`, with `taxon:family=Arecaceae`, `family=Arecaceae`, `taxon=Arecaceae` or `genus=Arecaceae`, with `taxon:en=Palm`, with `crown_type=I`, or when its genus (from `genus=*`, or the first word of `species=*` or `taxon=*`) is in the list of palm genera (`PALM_GENERA` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`: *Phoenix*, *Washingtonia*, *Trachycarpus*, *Chamaerops*…). These take precedence over `leaf_type=broadleaved` or `needleleaved`: a tree with `taxon:family=Arecaceae` and `leaf_type=broadleaved` is drawn as a palm (`leaf_type=leafless` is left as it is). Palms have no seasons, whatever their `leaf_cycle`. The legend has an entry of its own for palms, separate from the broadleaved one
  * a dense star (14 short points, rotated differently for each tree) if `needleleaved`; the crown area stays the same as the circle. The shape for needleleaved trees can be voted on in [issue #5](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/5), whether the issue is open or closed: the most voted rendering is the one applied. If more than one rendering gets many votes, they can be applied together, each to a different share of the trees. Proposals to use a specific rendering for a specific species are also welcome
* **Trunk**: a brown circle with a radius derived from `circumference=*` (or from `diameter=*`). If the tag is missing, the circumference is estimated (trunk drawn lighter) from `height=*` or from `diameter_crown=*`, using the species ratios in `scripts/species_limits.json` (`height_circ_ratio`, `crown_circ_ratio`) or, for species without a ratio, the general medians of `_default`: for the height, the height/circumference ratio of the tree's height band (15.4 below 6 m, 13.2 from 6 to 8 m, 11.1 from 8 to 15 m, 11.3 from 15 to 20 m, 12.0 from 20 to 30 m, 11.4 from 30 m; the lower bound belongs to the band), then the single value 12; for the crown, crown/circumference 7.03. The bands are explained in [Data and updates](dati.md#height-bands-for-the-estimated-trunk). The estimates are used only for rendering and do not enter the QA checks. The trunk is always drawn at the size indicated by the tags, even if it exceeds the crown. In that case, if the tree has errors (usually a wrong measurement, such as a circumference in centimetres), the red outline goes on the trunk, which would otherwise cover the crown and its outline. The trunk is also clickable and opens the card. The trunk of the Himalayan birch (`species=Betula utilis`, also with a variety, such as `var. jacquemontii`) is white with a thin grey border, like its bark; that of the paperbark maple (`species=Acer griseum`) is red-orange, like its glossy bark. There is also a surprise for the rainbow eucalyptus (`species=Eucalyptus deglupta`).
* **Multiple trunks** (`multi_stemmed=*`, from zoom 16): instead of the single trunk, several trunks arranged in a circle around the centre. With `yes` (or a non-numeric value) there are 2, with an integer (`multi_stemmed=3`) that number, and they divide the trunk area; with several circumferences (`multi_stemmed=0.75;0.57;0.50`) each trunk has its own measurement. Without a circumference (and without trunk measurements) nothing is drawn, as with the single trunk.
* **Ivy** (`ivyclad=yes`, from zoom 16): a dotted green ring around the trunk, which stays brown; with multiple trunks, a ring around each one.
* **Christmas trees** (`xmas:feature=tree`, from zoom 16, only from 8 December to 6 January in the viewer's date): 8, 11 or 14 coloured lights (by crown size) and a yellow star in the centre; fruits are not drawn. The crown follows `leaf_type=*` (or its estimate); without it, it is drawn as a needleleaved one. A Christmas tree tagged as deciduous follows the seasons like any other tree (bare in December); lights and star stay. Christmas trees that are not `natural=tree` (or `natural=tree_stump`) are also shown, with the crown from `diameter_crown=*` or the fallback radius: they get no checks, are not counted and do not appear in filters or downloads; without a name, their info panel is titled "Christmas tree". The lights switch on and off at random every 0.6 s; when they are off they stay dimmed. With "reduce motion" enabled in the operating system or browser (`prefers-reduced-motion`) they stay on. The legend entry appears only in that period. Adding `xmas=1` to the address (`#xmas=1&region=…`) shows them outside the period too.
* **Fruit trees** (from zoom 16): 5, 6 or 7 coloured dots on the crown according to its size, in fixed positions for each tree (from the id). They apply to `species=Malus domestica` (apples, red), `Juglans regia` (walnuts, light green like the husk, with a dark border to set them apart from the crown), `Citrus limon` (lemons, yellow) and `Citrus sinensis` and `Citrus × aurantium` (oranges and bitter oranges, orange), `Olea europaea` (olives, dark purple) and `Diospyros kaki` (persimmons, reddish orange), also with author, cultivar or the form with `×` (`Citrus × limon`); not whole genera, because many *Malus* and *Prunus* are ornamental. Fruits are visible only in their season, according to the date of whoever is looking at the map: apples August–October, walnuts July–October, oranges November–April, bitter oranges November–March, olives and persimmons October–December, lemons all year (northern hemisphere; south of the equator the months shift by 6). The species, colour and months of the fruits are in `FRUIT_SPECIES` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`. A click on the text of the legend entry (not on the icon, which shows the tags) switches from "Fruits (seasonal)" to "Fruits (always)", which shows them all year, then to "Fruits (hidden)" and back to "Fruits (seasonal)"; the choice is saved in the browser (the entry is struck through when hidden).
* **Stumps** (`natural=tree_stump`): a brown X, as large as the circumference (`circumference=*` or `diameter=*`); if missing, a radius of 0.25 m and a lighter X. They appear from zoom 10, with a red X if the stump has errors; they follow the same checks as trees.
* **Crown colour**: from `leaf_cycle=*` (or, if missing, estimated from the wiki lists like `leaf_type`): light yellow-green for deciduous, dark green for evergreen, medium green if unknown.
  * **Seasons** ([issue #51](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/51)): deciduous trees (`leaf_cycle=deciduous` or `semi_deciduous`, written or estimated; the estimate counts like the tag) change with the date of whoever is looking at the map. There are three phases:
    * **leaves**, April to September: the usual rendering
    * **autumn**, October and November: the crown takes one of four autumn tones (yellow, orange, red-brown, brown), fixed for each tree (from the id). Species with an autumn colour of their own (see Species colours) use it instead
    * **bare**, December to March: from zoom 16 only the outline of the crown is drawn, in a grey-brown twig colour; the whole crown stays clickable. Below zoom 16 the circle is sand-coloured and more transparent. Trunk, fruits in season (persimmons in December), Christmas lights and the outlines for errors, monumental, sacred and landmark trees stay as they are

    Broadleaved trees (`leaf_type=broadleaved`, or estimated) go through all three phases. Deciduous needleleaved trees are bare in the bare phase, with their star shape, but in autumn only the species with a colour of their own change (`Larix decidua`, `Taxodium distichum`, `Metasequoia glyptostroboides`). Evergreen trees (`semi_evergreen` included), trees with an unknown leaf cycle, palms and stumps do not change. Trees with several species (`;`) or without a species follow the default calendar.

    The change of phase is gradual: each tree is early or late by a fixed number of days, up to 10 (from the id), so around the first day of a phase some trees are already in it and others not yet. South of the equator the months shift by 6. Species with a colour of their own follow their own months, with no early or late days: `Ginkgo biloba` is green in October, yellow in November and bare from 1 December; `Elaeagnus angustifolia` stays silver until the end of October. A region can have its own calendar (`seasons` in `scripts/regions.json`, see [Data and updates](dati.md)); none has one for now, and the monumental trees map always uses the default one, also for the trees of the MASAF list.

    The "Deciduous" legend entry is the switch: a click on its text turns the seasons off or on, and the choice is saved in the browser. With the seasons on the entry reads "Deciduous (seasons)" and its icon is split in three (leaves top left, autumn top right, bare at the bottom); with the seasons off the word "seasons" is struck through and greyed out, the icon is the plain one and deciduous trees keep their leaves all year. Adding `season=leaves`, `season=autumn` or `season=bare` to the address (`#season=bare&region=…`) forces that phase for all the trees that can have it, whatever the date; it stays in the address while moving the map. The calendar, the tones and the number of days are in `SEASONS_DEFAULT`, `AUTUMN_TONES` and `SEASON_SHIFT` in `index.html`; the seasons do not depend on the data, so they need no region update.
  * **Species colours**: some species with a characteristic, documented foliage colour have that colour instead of the `leaf_cycle` one, in the months when it shows (according to the date of whoever is looking at the map; south of the equator the months shift by 6):
    * autumn, October and November: red for `species=Quercus rubra` (rust red), `Liquidambar styraciflua` (crimson), `Nyssa sylvatica` (scarlet) and `Acer rubrum`; golden yellow for `Larix decidua`; russet for `Taxodium distichum` and `Metasequoia glyptostroboides`; golden yellow for `Ginkgo biloba`, in November only. The autumn colours are part of the seasons: they need both the seasons and the species colours on. With the seasons on and the species colours off these trees take a default autumn tone (the needleleaved ones the `leaf_cycle` colour); with the seasons off they are green
    * silver for `Elaeagnus angustifolia`, from April to October (when it has leaves)
    * blue-grey for `Picea pungens` and silver-blue for `Eucalyptus cinerea` and `Eucalyptus pulverulenta`, all year

    The species also match with author or cultivar; species whose colour belongs only to some cultivars (such as purple beech) are not included. These crowns are a little less transparent than the others, so that the colour stays recognisable over the green of parks. A click on the text of the legend entry switches from "Species colours (on)" to "Species colours (off)", where the crowns no longer take the colour of the species (the seasons stay as they are), and back; the choice is saved in the browser. Adding `colors=1` to the address (`#colors=1&region=…`) shows the seasonal colours outside their months too. The species, colours and months are in `SPECIES_COLORS` in `index.html`; the colour does not depend on the data, so it needs no region update.
* **Crown outline**, in order of precedence: red for trees with errors, yellow for `denotation=natural_monument` (monumental trees are drawn above the others), light blue for sacred trees (`ref:zapis=*`, the Serbian zapis, or `place_of_worship=sacred_tree` or `sacred_tree=*`, like the rag trees of Ireland and Scotland, or `religion=shinto`, the Japanese goshinboku according to the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:religion%3Dshinto)), purple for `denotation=landmark`. A tree that is both monumental and sacred, without errors, has from zoom 16 an outline split in half: yellow on the left and light blue on the right (below zoom 16 it stays yellow). With multiple values (`natural_monument;landmark`) each counts, as in the script: that tree is monumental. On the card of sacred trees "Sacred tree" appears, with the type and the link to Wikipedia: "zapis" if there is `ref:zapis` (the number is not shown: it comes from an unofficial list), "rag tree" with `sacred_tree=rag_tree` (link to the article on clootie wells, because rag trees do not have an article of their own), "goshinboku" with `religion=shinto` (article "Shinboku").
* **Zoom**: trees with errors are visible at every zoom; from zoom 9 monumental trees are added; from zoom 10 all trees (as circles); from zoom 16 the shapes and the trunk appear.

## Filters and card

* Filters by genus/species and by error type: both lists are built from the trees in view, with the count. With no region loaded there are no trees to filter, so both menus are greyed out and cannot be opened ([issue #20](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/20)). If the loaded region has no trees with errors (in the whole region, not only in view), the error menu shows "No errors" and is greyed out too, and the ⤓ download button is hidden; an error chosen in another region is cleared. In the genus/species filter, "Unknown" shows trees with neither `genus` nor `species`, "With species" those with `species=*` (any value). Errors are grouped by theme (measurements, species and genus name, Wikidata, links and references, other tags); the first entry of each group ("All "…" errors") shows trees with at least one error of that group (omitted if the group has only one error type in view). From zoom 10, when trees without errors appear too, the error menu has "Errors only" below "All errors": it hides every tree without errors (monumental trees and MASAF trees not on OSM included) and leaves the trees and stumps with at least one error. With a region loaded, the ⤓ button next to the menu downloads a GeoJSON file with all the trees of the region (not only those in view) with the chosen error (or, with "All errors", with at least one error) and, if set, of the chosen genus/species: for each tree the link to the OSM node, the codes and description of the errors and the main tags. The file opens in JOSM, QGIS, uMap and similar. To see only monumental trees, just stay between zoom 9 and 10.
* The collapsible tabs of the panel (Legend, Error ranking, Error trend, Completeness, Record trees) open one at a time. The closed tabs sit in two columns of equal width, two per row; when their number is odd, the last one is alone on its row and takes the whole row, and the open tab is below them, full width. Opening a closed tab sends the open one back to its place in the grid; tapping the open tab closes it, and all go back to the grid. The same applies to the monumental trees map, which has Legend, Completeness and Record trees. The legend list, the ranking list, the Completeness tab and the Record trees tab are as tall as the space left in the window (recalculated when a tab opens, the window is resized or the box on top changes): if the entries do not fit, only the list scrolls. If the panel is still taller than the window, the panel scrolls and not the page
* "Completeness" tab ([issue #31](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/31)): how many trees have the main data. The checks report wrong values, this tab the missing ones. It counts the trees in view, the same set as the "Trees in view" count: the trees inside the map on screen, at the current zoom (below zoom 10 only trees with errors and, from zoom 9, monumental trees: a note says so) and with the genus/species and error filters applied; stumps are not counted. It is recalculated when the map moves or a filter changes, and only while the tab is open. One bar from 0 to 100% per entry, with the percentage and the absolute numbers ("38% · 1,204 of 3,170"), in two groups: "Basics" (Species `species=*`, Leaf type `leaf_type=*`, Leaf cycle `leaf_cycle=*`) and "Measurements and photo", marked as extra (Height `height=*`, Circumference `circumference=*`, Crown diameter `diameter_crown=*`, Photo). Below them, the "Dioecious trees" group ([issue #58](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/58)) has the Sex entry (`sex=*`), which counts only the trees in view of dioecious taxa, with male and female flowers on separate trees: those whose `genus=*`, or the first word of `species=*`, is one of the genera Broussonetia, Cephalotaxus, Cercidiphyllum, Chamaerops, Ginkgo, Gymnocladus, Hippophae, Ilex, Laurus, Maclura, Phellodendron, Phoenix, Pistacia, Podocarpus, Populus, Salix, Taxus, Torreya, Trachycarpus, and those whose `species=*` is (also followed by an author or a cultivar) Acer negundo, Ailanthus altissima, Araucaria araucana, Ceratonia siliqua, Diospyros lotus, Diospyros virginiana, Juniperus communis, Juniperus oxycedrus, Juniperus virginiana, Phytolacca dioica or Schinus molle (`DIO_GENERA` and `DIO_SPECIES` in `index.html`). The group appears only when there is at least one of these trees in view, and its highlight marks only them ("Highlighted: dioecious, no sex=* ×"); it is in the monumental trees map too, below "Optional". A tree counts when the tag is present, whatever its value; for Circumference a tree with `diameter=*` counts too, because the trunk is drawn from it. Photo counts with at least one of `wikimedia_commons` (file or category), `panoramax`, `image:fotoshi`, `image` (an http address) or the photo (P18) of the linked Wikidata item that the card shows. The ⓘ next to each entry (mouse over or tap) says which tag to use, with the link to the OSM wiki, and how the value is surveyed (for example the circumference is measured 1.30 m above the ground), and adds that values should be surveyed on the ground or taken from a source, not estimated. Tapping a row highlights on the map in magenta (following the crown shape from zoom 16) the trees that lack that value; the row turns magenta and a magenta notice appears in the box on top ("Highlighted: no height ×"), also when the tab is closed: tapping the notice, or the row again, removes the highlight. One highlight at a time. With no region chosen, while the trees are loading or with no trees in view, the tab says so instead of showing the bars. The texts are in `cp` in the `I18N` object of `index.html`, the entries in `COMP`
* "Record trees" tab ([issue #15](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/15)): for six records, the tree with the best value and, on request, the top ten ([issue #48](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/48)). With a tie all the tied trees are shown (the first 10, then "and N more tied").
  * **Tallest**: the highest value of `height=*`
  * **Thickest trunk**: the largest trunk circumference, from `circumference=*`; if that tag is missing, from `diameter=*` multiplied by π (`diameter` without a unit is in millimetres, whatever its value). When the value comes from `diameter`, the row shows the diameter (e.g. "⌀ 1,200 mm"). The values of `multi_stemmed=*` are not used
  * **Most slender** and **Stockiest**: the highest and the lowest ratio between the height and the trunk diameter (`diameter=*`, or `circumference=*` divided by π), only for trees with both measurements and at least 2 m tall. The row shows the ratio (e.g. "85×") and, below, the two measurements
  * **Widest crown**: the highest value of `diameter_crown=*`
  * **Oldest**: the oldest date of `start_date=*`. The forms read are a year of three or four digits, `YYYY-MM`, `YYYY-MM-DD`, a decade (`1850s`), a century (`C16`, also `early`, `mid` or `late C16`), years BC (`500 BC`, `500 BCE`), each also preceded by `~`, `before` or `after`, and ranges (`1500..1600`). Approximate dates count with the oldest year they indicate: `~1500` and `before 1500` count as 1500, `1850s` as 1850, `C16` as 1500 (`mid C16` 1533, `late C16` 1566), `1500..1600` as 1500. Dates of the same year are tied (`1850-05-01`, `1850`, `~1850` and `1850s` are one position). The row shows the date as written on OSM. Other forms (`c. 1850`, `1850;1900`, `12.05.1900`) are not read

  **Top ten.** To the right of the name of each record a small triangle points right; pressed, it points down and the top ten list opens below the record. Opening one closes the one opened before, like the tabs; when the region or the filters change, the open record stays open and its list is recalculated. The list shows whole positions until it reaches ten trees; if a tied position does not fit in the room left, as many trees as fit are shown, followed by "and N more tied" (1 tree + 9 tied = 10, and the list ends there; 1 + 15 tied = 1 + 9 and "and 6 more tied"). From the second position on, each row starts with its position; tied trees have the same one. Order inside a tie: first the values reported by a check (⚠); for Oldest, then the most precise date (day, month, year, then `~`/`before`/`after`/range, decade, century) and, with the same precision, the oldest; finally the node id, so the order is always the same. Values are tied when they are equal in the first 9 significant digits (`recTop` in `index.html`, `record_order` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`: the two must stay the same).

  **Top three compared (Tallest, Thickest trunk, Widest crown).** For Tallest, between the name and the triangle, the same icon that opens "Trunk comparison" on the card opens that window with the first three trees of the list, in list order, to scale and side by side, each numbered, with height and trunk (two trees if only two have a height; with one the icon is not there). A tree without a mapped circumference has the trunk estimated for the map (see [Height bands for the estimated trunk](dati.md#height-bands-for-the-estimated-trunk)), drawn lighter with a dotted outline and marked "⌀ ~"; if there is no estimate either (for example a height above the limit), only the height is drawn, as a dashed line marked "⌀ ?". A note below the drawing explains both. Values reported by a check have a ⚠, also when they dwarf the others (`height=300`). The references (person, house, Tower of Pisa, and the width reference behind the thickest trunk) follow the rules of the comparison on the card, and the trunks have the colour of the species. For Thickest trunk and Widest crown the icon has two concentric circles ([issue #60](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/60)) and the first three trees are drawn seen from above, to scale, one inside the other on the same centre, each numbered on its edge and with its own outline (solid, dashed, dotted): trunks as circles with the colour of the species, crowns with the outline used on the map (lobed for broadleaved, star-shaped for needleleaved, with fronds for palms, from `leaf_type` or estimated from the species; a circle if the type is not known), with the same area as the circle of `diameter_crown`. Next to them, the same reference seen from above as in "Trunk from above" on the card, chosen on the first tree, with a sentence comparing its diameter with the length of the reference. Values reported by a check have a ⚠.

  **What happened that year (Oldest only).** Each row of Oldest has a calendar button that shows, below the row, a sentence about the year ("In 1969, humans land on the Moon"), with a serif "W" that links to its source on Wikipedia (for a year without its own sentence, the sentence of its decade); the button does not open the card. The sentence is about the year, not about the tree: `start_date` does not necessarily say when the tree was planted. The sentences are in `scripts/year_events.json`, one entry per year (not per tree), in Italian and English; approximate dates use the entry of the year they name (`~1850`, `before 1850`, `1850..1900` → `1850`), of the decade (`1850s`) or of the century (`C16`, also for `early`/`mid`/`late`), years BC their own (`480 BC`). If the year has no entry the button is not there. The events are known worldwide and the same for every country: discoveries, inventions, feats, culture, wars and other historical events. The summary of "Update tree data" lists the years in the top ten of Oldest of some region that have no entry yet, see [Data and updates](dati.md).

  Values with a unit (`150 cm`, `30 ft`, `7'4"`) are converted and shown in the site's unit (metres; millimetres for `diameter`). Values that are not readable, zero or negative do not count. Values reported by a check count too: the row then has a ⚠, and hovering over it with the mouse (or tapping it) names the check. The checks considered are those on the value used: "Height > 116.22 m" and the species height limit for the height; "Circumference > 43 m", the species circumference limit, "circumference and diameter do not match" (or, when the value comes from `diameter`, "diameter without unit too small for millimetres") for the trunk; the three checks on the height/circumference ratio for height, trunk and ratio; "Crown diameter > 60.4 m" for the crown; and, for the tags used, "Decimal comma", "Unit written in a non-standard form" and "Tag written in the value (=)". Hovering over the name of a record (or tapping it) explains how it is calculated: the tags the value comes from and, for the two ratios, the 2 m threshold.

  Each row has the name of the tree (as in the card title: `name`, or the common name of the species, or the species), the value and, when the records cover more than one region, the region and the country. A click on a row opens the card of the tree and centres the map on it at zoom 19, like the Z key; if the tree is in another region, that region is loaded first.

  In the QA tool the tab follows the open level, and the subtitle says which one, with the date of the latest data update (two dates when the regions were updated on different days). With no country chosen: all the regions loaded on the site. With a country open: its regions. With a region loaded: all the trees of the region, not only those in view. The records cover only the regions loaded on the site, and the subtitle says so. Without a loaded region the records come from `data/records.json`, see [Data and updates](dati.md), requested the first time the tab is opened; with a region loaded they are calculated from the trees of the region, and follow the genus/species and error filters: choosing a species shows the records of that species in the region. Without a loaded region the two filter menus are greyed out, so there the records are always those of all the trees. The top ten of a country or of all the regions is built from the top ten of each region: a region whose data was written before the top ten existed has only its first position, and a note says for how many regions the list is not complete yet (it arrives with their next update). On the monumental trees map the records and the lists cover the trees in view.

  In the monumental trees map the records cover the trees in view, like the Completeness tab, with the genus/species filter, and are recalculated when the map moves, only while the tab is open. Below zoom 7, where the map shows only some of the trees, the tab shows a notice that invites to zoom in instead of the list. The trees of the MASAF list not yet on OSM are not counted.

  When a filter is active, a yellow notice in the tab names it ("Active filter: Quercus robur"). If no tree has the measurement of a record, or the data is still loading, the tab says so. The texts are in `rc` in the `I18N` object of `index.html`, the rules in `recValues` (`index.html`) and `record_values` (`scripts/fetch_trees.py`), which must stay the same
* "Error ranking" tab: countries in order of share of trees with at least one error and, inside each country, the regions; tapping a region opens it. The counts are in the `errors` field of `data/index.json` and are filled in when the data is updated. The `%` and `#` buttons next to the title, visible only when the tab is open, sort by percentage (the default) or by number of trees with errors; the choice is saved in the browser. Numbers are coloured according to position, with the same scale as the map (from green, last place, to orange up to red, first place): countries among themselves and the regions of each country among themselves; rows without counts are grey. Next to each row, the position compared with 7 days before the latest date in `data/history.json` (for each region the last value recorded up to that day; if the history is shorter, the oldest date; `RANK_DAYS` in `index.html`), so a movement stays visible for a week even if in the meantime the data is updated again without changing: red ▲ if it goes up towards first place (more errors than the others), green ▼ if it goes down, grey = if it stays the same; nothing if the region has no previous value. Positions are compared only among rows that have both the current and the previous value: a newly added country or region does not push down those below it. The position can also change without the region's data changing, when the others are updated
* "Error trend" tab: the share of trees with at least one error over time, for the loaded region, for the open country or for all regions (the subtitle says which). The values come from `data/history.json`; for a country or for all regions, at each date the last known value of each region is summed. The vertical axis narrows around the values (for example from 0.1% to 0.3% if the share is 0.2%). The box appears only with at least two dates. When a check is added the share goes up even if the data has not changed
* With a card open, the **Z** key centres the map on the tree at zoom 19 (useful after clicking it from far away)
* With a card open, the **C** key ("Compare") opens the trunk comparison window, if the tree has it. For trees without errors on the height/circumference ratio that have a mapped height and circumference (also from `diameter`), it is the same window as the small icon (two trunks and a person) next to the height: with the typical trunk if the species has a known ratio, otherwise only the tree to scale with the references; for trees with an error on the ratio, the icon and the C key open the window of that error; see [Checks](check.md). Like Z, it does nothing while typing in a field or with a window open; without a comparison nothing happens
* Clicking a tree opens the card with the main data (a tree with `diameter=*` but no `circumference=*` shows a "Circumference" row with the diameter multiplied by π, marked "(from diameter)"; `diameter` without a unit is in millimetres; the "Planting date" row, from `start_date=*`, is followed by the age in brackets, [issue #61](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/61): today minus the date, in completed years with a full date, the difference of the years with the year only, preceded by "~" for `~`, decades and centuries, ">" for `before`, "<" for `after`, two ages for a range such as `1500..1600`, nothing for the forms not read by Oldest in Record trees or for dates in the future), the problems found and the links to Wikidata, Commons, Panoramax and Fotoshi (Commons and Fotoshi only if the photo shown is not already that one: in that case the photo credit links there). The "Edit ▾" entry opens a menu with the node's page on OpenStreetMap and the editors to fix it: iD (the OSM web editor), JOSM (loads the area around the tree into the already open editor and selects it, if remote control is on) and MapComplete (trees theme). With iD and JOSM the changeset gets `hashtags=#dendrosm` and as `source` the site address with the open region (e.g. `https://ivanbranco.github.io/tree-osm-qa/#region=slovenia`).
* "Fix with JOSM" ([issue #19](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/19)): when the card has errors or hints with only one possible fix, the "Edit ▾" menu shows "Fix with JOSM" instead of "Open in JOSM", preceded by ⚠ if it fixes errors, ⓘ if it applies hints, or both. The link works like "Open in JOSM" (same changeset hashtag and source) and also passes the tags to change (`addtags`) and a changeset comment in English (e.g. "Remove the author name from species; add leaf_type"). JOSM downloads the tree as it is now and shows the tags before applying them, so the user checks them and can cancel. Hovering the entry lists the changes (`species: Thuya occidentalis L. → Thuya occidentalis`, or `leaf_type=broadleaved` for an added tag); the old values are those of the last update of the region. Errors with a fix: "Species with the author name" (the name without the author), "Species with uppercase epithet" (the name from the reference list), "Species starting lowercase" and "Genus starting lowercase" (capital first letter; not for a genus of more than one word), "Decimal comma" (only for a number with one comma between the digits, e.g. `2,9 m` → `2.9 m`) and "Unit written in a non-standard form" (only when there is one standard form). Hints with a fix: missing `leaf_type` and `leaf_cycle`, `species` (from `species:wikidata`, from `species:wikipedia` or for a genus with one living species), `species:wikidata`, `masaf:criteria`, `species:it` with a capital letter, feet and inches without spaces. No fix for checks and hints that need a choice (a typo, a common name, an underscore, `denotation` for a tree with Wikidata) or a survey (the species from the tree's Wikidata item). If two fixes give different values for the same tag, that tag is left out; values containing `|` (the tag separator in the JOSM link), or `;` in `species` and `genus`, are left out too. Stumps get only the feet-and-inches fix among the hints, as on the card. Nothing on the monumental trees map, and nothing in iD, which cannot receive tag changes through its address.
* Requesting new regions: the "+ Request a new region…" entry in the menu opens a [GitHub issue](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/new?template=region.yml); in the overview (below zoom 10) a click outside the covered regions opens a popup with two links, a GitHub issue and a message on OpenStreetMap, with the name of the country asked from Nominatim (nothing appears on the sea). In a country covered only in part (the United States) the popup shows the name of the state, and does not appear in the states already covered. The same invitation, with the name of the region, appears when clicking a region drawn on the map but still without data (in grey). The same GitHub issue can also be used to ask for the update of an existing region
* Old data ([issue #59](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/59)): below the count, "Data updated on …" gives the date of the latest update of the loaded region. When that date is 7 or more days old (`OLD_DAYS` in `index.html`), it is followed by an orange "⚠ N days ago" and a small icon that opens the GitHub issue for requesting an update, already filled in with "Update of an existing region" and the region name. Not on the monumental trees map
* The address contains the region and the map position (`#region=lombardia&map=17.00/45.46420/9.18990`): copying it shares the exact point. With a tree's card open it also contains its OSM node (`#region=molise&node=6311161280&map=…`): the link reopens the card, and without `map` it centres the tree. If the node no longer exists the region is shown. Changing the address by hand in the bar (another `node`, `region` or `map`) and pressing Enter, the page follows it without reloading: it loads the region if needed and opens the card; changing `lang` instead reloads.
* The image comes, in order, from `wikimedia_commons=File:…`, `panoramax`, `image:fotoshi` ([Fotoshi](https://www.fotoshi.com/), with the link to the photo and, before the author's name in the credit, the Fotoshi symbol, as on osm.fotoshi.com), `image` and from the image (P18) of the Wikidata item of `wikidata=*`, if it is not a taxon. When the photo comes from Wikidata and the item also has a winter view (P5252), the card shows the winter view from December to March (June to September south of the equator), or with `season=bare` in the address. If there is only `wikimedia_commons=Category:…`, the card shows the first file of the category, requested from Commons when it opens. Images that fail to load are hidden. At the bottom right of the photo, author and licence appear, with the link to the source, once the photo is loaded; if they do not fit on one line, only the author is shortened (with "…", full text on hover) and the licence stays visible: for Commons files they are requested from the Commons API when the card opens, for Panoramax from the Panoramax API, for Fotoshi the script reads them from the photo page. For Commons the author is the attribution required in the licence template (`|attribution=`), if present; otherwise the author field, without the parts hidden by templates, and if this contains links (for example a box with name and contacts) only the text of the first link. For Panoramax the author is the one indicated in the photo, if any (among the "providers", the one without an account, from the EXIF data), otherwise the account that uploaded it (for example `mapcomplete`). The "Panoramax" link at the bottom of the card does not appear if the photo shown is already that one.
* The card title is the tree's name (`name`); if missing, the common name of the species in the site's language (e.g. "Judas tree" for *Cercis siliquastrum*); if that is missing too, the species in italics. The value used as the title is not repeated in the data below. The common name comes from Wikidata: the item is that of `species:wikidata` if correct, otherwise that of the species in the wiki list; the label is used if it is not the scientific name, otherwise the first alias that is not (in Italian the labels of plants are almost always the scientific name). A wrong common name is therefore fixed on Wikidata, by changing the label or the order of the aliases. With `wikidata=*`, the card also has the link to the tree's Wikipedia article, if it exists.
* The mouse wheel over a card zooms the map, as it does outside the card
* With `sex=male` or `sex=female` (dioecious plants, such as ginkgo or yew) ♂ or ♀ appears next to the card title, in grey; other values are not shown. Clicking it opens a box with "Male specimen" or "Female specimen" and, for some dioecious species and genera (Ginkgo biloba, Taxus baccata, Ilex aquifolium, Laurus nobilis, Juniperus communis, Acer negundo, Ceratonia siliqua, Populus, Salix, Phoenix), the traits that tell males and females apart; for Ginkgo biloba also a photo from Wikimedia Commons (seeds or male cones)
* After the errors (⚠, in red) the card can show hints (ⓘ, in grey): information that could improve the data, not errors; see [Hints](check.md#hints)
* On the card each error is preceded by ⚠: hovering over it with the mouse (or tapping it on a phone) shows the explanation of the check, for example where the threshold comes from. The texts are in `why` in the `I18N` object of `index.html`, one per error code
* The errors on the height/circumference ratio ("tree too slender", "tree too slender for its species", "tree too stocky") have the same icon next to the height ([issue #62](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/62)): it opens a window with a drawing of the trunk next to a typical trunk, a person and a house (a house and the Leaning Tower of Pisa for trees taller than 40 m; a sauropod, a Boeing 747 or the Colosseum behind the trunk when the trunk diameter is above 5 m), see [Checks](check.md). The same window opens with the C key. The window is closed with Esc, with the × button or by clicking outside it
* Next to the circumference (also when it comes from `diameter`), a small icon with two concentric circles opens "Trunk from above" ([issue #60](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/60)): a scale drawing of the trunk seen from above, as a circle with the mapped circumference. If the tree has a height and its species has a known height/circumference ratio, the typical trunk of the species at the same height is drawn as a dashed circle on the same centre, and the text gives how many times wider the trunk is. Next to the circles, at the same scale and upright (its length from top to bottom, so that it can be compared with the diameter), a reference seen from above: the shoulders of a person, a car (4.5 × 1.8 m), a city bus (12 × 2.55 m), a tennis court (23.77 × 10.97 m), a football pitch (105 × 68 m) or the Colosseum (189 × 156 m), whichever has the length closest to the trunk diameter; a sentence below compares the diameter with that length (for example "about half the length of a car"). Not shown for stumps
* If present, the card shows `ref:masaf` (MASAF list of monumental trees of Italy) with the monumentality criteria from `masaf:criteria` (only the names, without the codes: for example `a2.1,a2.2,g` becomes "circumference, height, historical-cultural or religious value", see the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/IT:Key:ref:masaf#Criteri_di_monumentalit%C3%A0)), `ref:rami` (RAMI register), `heritage:operator=icnf` (tree of public interest protected by the ICNF in Portugal, with the code from `ref:icnf` if present), `heritage:operator=anf` (tree protected by the ANF in Luxembourg) and `website`/`url` pointing to monumentaltrees.com. The lines of the official lists and registers (MASAF, RAMI, ICNF, ANF) start with a ★; the MASAF criteria are indented under the MASAF line.

## Language

The site is in Italian if the browser language is Italian, otherwise in English. It is changed with IT · EN at the top of the panel: the choice is saved in the browser. It can also be forced from the address, for example `#lang=en` or `#region=lombardia&lang=en`.

All texts are in the `I18N` object at the start of the script in `index.html`: to add a language, copy one of the two blocks, translate it and add the code to `LANGS`. Region names, OSM tags and values are not translated.
