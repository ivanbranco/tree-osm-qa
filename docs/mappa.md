# The map

How trees are drawn, what the filters contain, the card and the panel boxes, and how the language works.

## Rendering

The maximum zoom is 19, the last level of the openstreetmap.org tiles: beyond that, the background would only be enlarged and blurred.

* **Crown**: a circle with the real size from `diameter_crown=*` (like every circle on the map, trunks included, it is scaled at the tree's latitude, so it matches buildings and roads of the base map); if missing, a fallback radius (3 m; 5 m for `denotation=natural_monument` and `landmark`, 2.5 m for `agricultural` and `orchard`, 2 m for `parking` and `decoration`), varied by ±15% at random but always the same for the same tree, so that the map is not uniform (except for monumental trees and landmarks, which stay at 5 m), and a more transparent fill. The crown is not estimated from circumference or height: the estimates tried (median crown/circumference ratio, UrbanEye3D method) turned out too large. The shape depends on `leaf_type=*` (if missing or invalid, it is estimated from the OSM wiki lists: from the species or, if that is not there, from the genus):
  * round if `leaf_type` is missing (or has another value, such as `leafless`)
  * rounded, lobed, if `broadleaved` or `palm`
  * a dense star (14 short points, rotated differently for each tree) if `needleleaved`; the crown area stays the same as the circle. The shape for needleleaved trees can be voted on in [issue #5](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/5), whether the issue is open or closed: the most voted rendering is the one applied. If more than one rendering gets many votes, they can be applied together, each to a different share of the trees. Proposals to use a specific rendering for a specific species are also welcome
* **Trunk**: a brown circle with a radius derived from `circumference=*` (or from `diameter=*`). If the tag is missing, the circumference is estimated (trunk drawn lighter) from `height=*` or from `diameter_crown=*`, using the species ratios in `scripts/species_limits.json` (`height_circ_ratio`, `crown_circ_ratio`) or, for species without a ratio, the general medians of `_default` (height/circumference 12, crown/circumference 7.03). The estimates are used only for rendering and do not enter the QA checks. The trunk is always drawn at the size indicated by the tags, even if it exceeds the crown. In that case, if the tree has errors (usually a wrong measurement, such as a circumference in centimetres), the red outline goes on the trunk, which would otherwise cover the crown and its outline. The trunk is also clickable and opens the card. The trunk of the Himalayan birch (`species=Betula utilis`, also with a variety, such as `var. jacquemontii`) is white with a thin grey border, like its bark; that of the paperbark maple (`species=Acer griseum`) is red-orange, like its glossy bark. There is also a surprise for the rainbow eucalyptus (`species=Eucalyptus deglupta`).
* **Multiple trunks** (`multi_stemmed=*`, from zoom 16): instead of the single trunk, several trunks arranged in a circle around the centre. With `yes` (or a non-numeric value) there are 2, with an integer (`multi_stemmed=3`) that number, and they divide the trunk area; with several circumferences (`multi_stemmed=0.75;0.57;0.50`) each trunk has its own measurement. Without a circumference (and without trunk measurements) nothing is drawn, as with the single trunk.
* **Ivy** (`ivyclad=yes`, from zoom 16): a dotted green ring around the trunk, which stays brown; with multiple trunks, a ring around each one.
* **Christmas trees** (`xmas:feature=tree`, from zoom 16, only from 8 December to 6 January in the viewer's date): 8, 11 or 14 coloured lights (by crown size) and a yellow star in the centre; fruits are not drawn. The crown follows `leaf_type=*` (or its estimate); without it, it is drawn as a needleleaved one. Christmas trees that are not `natural=tree` (or `natural=tree_stump`) are also shown, with the crown from `diameter_crown=*` or the fallback radius: they get no checks, are not counted and do not appear in filters or downloads; without a name, their info panel is titled "Christmas tree". The lights switch on and off at random every 0.6 s; when they are off they stay dimmed. With "reduce motion" enabled in the operating system or browser (`prefers-reduced-motion`) they stay on. The legend entry appears only in that period. Adding `xmas=1` to the address (`#xmas=1&region=…`) shows them outside the period too.
* **Fruit trees** (from zoom 16): 5, 6 or 7 coloured dots on the crown according to its size, in fixed positions for each tree (from the id). They apply to `species=Malus domestica` (apples, red), `Juglans regia` (walnuts, light green like the husk, with a dark border to set them apart from the crown), `Citrus limon` (lemons, yellow) and `Citrus sinensis` and `Citrus × aurantium` (oranges and bitter oranges, orange), `Olea europaea` (olives, dark purple) and `Diospyros kaki` (persimmons, reddish orange), also with author, cultivar or the form with `×` (`Citrus × limon`); not whole genera, because many *Malus* and *Prunus* are ornamental. Fruits are visible only in their season, according to the date of whoever is looking at the map: apples August–October, walnuts July–October, oranges November–April, bitter oranges November–March, olives and persimmons October–December, lemons all year (northern hemisphere; south of the equator the months shift by 6). The species, colour and months of the fruits are in `FRUIT_SPECIES` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`. A click on the text of the legend entry (not on the icon, which shows the tags) switches from "Fruits (seasonal)" to "Fruits (always)", which shows them all year, then to "Fruits (hidden)" and back to "Fruits (seasonal)"; the choice is saved in the browser (the entry is struck through when hidden).
* **Stumps** (`natural=tree_stump`): a brown X, as large as the circumference (`circumference=*` or `diameter=*`); if missing, a radius of 0.25 m and a lighter X. They appear from zoom 10, with a red X if the stump has errors; they follow the same checks as trees.
* **Crown colour**: from `leaf_cycle=*` (or, if missing, estimated from the wiki lists like `leaf_type`): light yellow-green for deciduous, dark green for evergreen, medium green if unknown.
* **Crown outline**, in order of precedence: red for trees with errors, yellow for `denotation=natural_monument` (monumental trees are drawn above the others), light blue for sacred trees (`ref:zapis=*`, the Serbian zapis, or `place_of_worship=sacred_tree` or `sacred_tree=*`, like the rag trees of Ireland and Scotland, or `religion=shinto`, the Japanese goshinboku according to the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:religion%3Dshinto)), purple for `denotation=landmark`. A tree that is both monumental and sacred, without errors, has from zoom 16 an outline split in half: yellow on the left and light blue on the right (below zoom 16 it stays yellow). With multiple values (`natural_monument;landmark`) each counts, as in the script: that tree is monumental. On the card of sacred trees "Sacred tree" appears, with the type and the link to Wikipedia: "zapis" if there is `ref:zapis` (the number is not shown: it comes from an unofficial list), "rag tree" with `sacred_tree=rag_tree` (link to the article on clootie wells, because rag trees do not have an article of their own), "goshinboku" with `religion=shinto` (article "Shinboku").
* **Zoom**: trees with errors are visible at every zoom; from zoom 9 monumental trees are added; from zoom 10 all trees (as circles); from zoom 16 the shapes and the trunk appear.

## Filters and card

* Filters by genus/species and by error type: both lists are built from the trees in view, with the count. In the genus/species filter, "Unknown" shows trees with neither `genus` nor `species`, "With species" those with `species=*` (any value). Errors are grouped by theme (measurements, species and genus name, Wikidata, links and references, other tags); the first entry of each group ("All "…" errors") shows trees with at least one error of that group (omitted if the group has only one error type in view). From zoom 10, when trees without errors appear too, the error menu has "Errors only" below "All errors": it hides every tree without errors (monumental trees and MASAF trees not on OSM included) and leaves the trees and stumps with at least one error. With a region loaded, the ⤓ button next to the menu downloads a GeoJSON file with all the trees of the region (not only those in view) with the chosen error (or, with "All errors", with at least one error) and, if set, of the chosen genus/species: for each tree the link to the OSM node, the codes and description of the errors and the main tags. The file opens in JOSM, QGIS, uMap and similar. To see only monumental trees, just stay between zoom 9 and 10.
* The collapsible boxes of the panel (Legend, Error ranking, Error trend) open one at a time: opening one closes the others. The legend list and the ranking list are as tall as the space left in the window (recalculated when a box opens, the window is resized or the box on top changes): if the entries do not fit, only the list scrolls. If the panel is still taller than the window, the panel scrolls and not the page
* "Error ranking" box (collapsible): countries in order of share of trees with at least one error and, inside each country, the regions; tapping a region opens it. The counts are in the `errors` field of `data/index.json` and are filled in when the data is updated. The `%` and `#` buttons next to the title sort by percentage (the default) or by number of trees with errors; the choice is saved in the browser. Numbers are coloured according to position, with the same scale as the map (from green, last place, to orange up to red, first place): countries among themselves and the regions of each country among themselves; rows without counts are grey. Next to each row, the position compared with 7 days before the latest date in `data/history.json` (for each region the last value recorded up to that day; if the history is shorter, the oldest date; `RANK_DAYS` in `index.html`), so a movement stays visible for a week even if in the meantime the data is updated again without changing: red ▲ if it goes up towards first place (more errors than the others), green ▼ if it goes down, grey = if it stays the same; nothing if the region has no previous value. Positions are compared only among rows that have both the current and the previous value: a newly added country or region does not push down those below it. The position can also change without the region's data changing, when the others are updated
* "Error trend" box (collapsible): the share of trees with at least one error over time, for the loaded region, for the open country or for all regions (the subtitle says which). The values come from `data/history.json`; for a country or for all regions, at each date the last known value of each region is summed. The vertical axis narrows around the values (for example from 0.1% to 0.3% if the share is 0.2%). The box appears only with at least two dates. When a check is added the share goes up even if the data has not changed
* With a card open, the **Z** key centres the map on the tree at zoom 19 (useful after clicking it from far away)
* With a card open, the **C** key ("Compare") opens the trunk comparison window, if the tree has it. For trees without errors on the height/circumference ratio that have a mapped height and circumference (also from `diameter`) and a species with a known ratio, it is the same window as the small icon (two trunks and a person) next to the height; for trees with an error on the ratio, which have no icon, it is the window of the "Compare visually" link; see [Checks](check.md). Like Z, it does nothing while typing in a field or with a window open; without a comparison nothing happens
* Clicking a tree opens the card with the main data, the problems found and the links to Wikidata, Commons, Panoramax and Fotoshi (Commons and Fotoshi only if the photo shown is not already that one: in that case the photo credit links there). The "Edit ▾" entry opens a menu with the node's page on OpenStreetMap and the editors to fix it: iD (the OSM web editor), JOSM (loads the area around the tree into the already open editor and selects it, if remote control is on) and MapComplete (trees theme). With iD and JOSM the changeset gets `hashtags=#dendrosm` and as `source` the site address with the open region (e.g. `https://ivanbranco.github.io/tree-osm-qa/#region=slovenia`).
* Requesting new regions: the "+ Request a new region…" entry in the menu opens a message on OpenStreetMap to the author; in the overview (below zoom 10) a click outside the covered regions offers the same, with the name of the country asked from Nominatim (nothing appears on the sea). The same invitation, with the name of the region, appears when clicking a region drawn on the map but still without data (in grey). A new region, or the update of an existing one, can also be requested by opening a [GitHub issue](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/new?template=region.yml)
* The address contains the region and the map position (`#region=lombardia&map=17.00/45.46420/9.18990`): copying it shares the exact point. With a tree's card open it also contains its OSM node (`#region=molise&node=6311161280&map=…`): the link reopens the card, and without `map` it centres the tree. If the node no longer exists the region is shown. Changing the address by hand in the bar (another `node`, `region` or `map`) and pressing Enter, the page follows it without reloading: it loads the region if needed and opens the card; changing `lang` instead reloads.
* The image comes, in order, from `wikimedia_commons=File:…`, `panoramax`, `image:fotoshi` ([Fotoshi](https://www.fotoshi.com/), with the link to the photo and, before the author's name in the credit, the Fotoshi symbol, as on osm.fotoshi.com), `image` and from the image (P18) of the Wikidata item of `wikidata=*`, if it is not a taxon. If there is only `wikimedia_commons=Category:…`, the card shows the first file of the category, requested from Commons when it opens. Images that fail to load are hidden. At the bottom right of the photo, author and licence appear, with the link to the source, once the photo is loaded; if they do not fit on one line, only the author is shortened (with "…", full text on hover) and the licence stays visible: for Commons files they are requested from the Commons API when the card opens, for Panoramax from the Panoramax API, for Fotoshi the script reads them from the photo page. For Commons the author is the attribution required in the licence template (`|attribution=`), if present; otherwise the author field, without the parts hidden by templates, and if this contains links (for example a box with name and contacts) only the text of the first link. For Panoramax the author is the one indicated in the photo, if any (among the "providers", the one without an account, from the EXIF data), otherwise the account that uploaded it (for example `mapcomplete`). The "Panoramax" link at the bottom of the card does not appear if the photo shown is already that one.
* The card title is the tree's name (`name`); if missing, the common name of the species in the site's language (e.g. "Judas tree" for *Cercis siliquastrum*); if that is missing too, the species in italics. The value used as the title is not repeated in the data below. The common name comes from Wikidata: the item is that of `species:wikidata` if correct, otherwise that of the species in the wiki list; the label is used if it is not the scientific name, otherwise the first alias that is not (in Italian the labels of plants are almost always the scientific name). A wrong common name is therefore fixed on Wikidata, by changing the label or the order of the aliases. With `wikidata=*`, the card also has the link to the tree's Wikipedia article, if it exists.
* The mouse wheel over a card zooms the map, as it does outside the card
* With `sex=male` or `sex=female` (dioecious plants, such as ginkgo or yew) ♂ or ♀ appears next to the card title, in grey, with "Male specimen" or "Female specimen" on hover; other values are not shown
* After the errors (⚠, in red) the card can show hints (ⓘ, in grey): information that could improve the data, not errors; see [Hints](check.md#hints)
* On the card each error is preceded by ⚠: hovering over it with the mouse (or tapping it on a phone) shows the explanation of the check, for example where the threshold comes from. The texts are in `why` in the `I18N` object of `index.html`, one per error code
* The errors on the height/circumference ratio ("tree too slender", "tree too slender for its species", "tree too stocky") are followed by a "Compare visually" link: it opens a window with a drawing of the trunk next to a typical trunk, a person and a house, see [Checks](check.md). The same window opens with the C key; these trees do not have the icon next to the height. The window is closed with Esc, with the × button or by clicking outside it
* If present, the card shows `ref:masaf` (MASAF list of monumental trees of Italy) with the monumentality criteria from `masaf:criteria` (only the names, without the codes: for example `a2.1,a2.2,g` becomes "circumference, height, historical-cultural or religious value", see the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/IT:Key:ref:masaf#Criteri_di_monumentalit%C3%A0)), `ref:rami` (RAMI register), `heritage:operator=icnf` (tree of public interest protected by the ICNF in Portugal, with the code from `ref:icnf` if present), `heritage:operator=anf` (tree protected by the ANF in Luxembourg) and `website`/`url` pointing to monumentaltrees.com.

## Language

The site is in Italian if the browser language is Italian, otherwise in English. It is changed with IT · EN at the top of the panel: the choice is saved in the browser. It can also be forced from the address, for example `#lang=en` or `#region=lombardia&lang=en`.

All texts are in the `I18N` object at the start of the script in `index.html`: to add a language, copy one of the two blocks, translate it and add the code to `LANGS`. Region names, OSM tags and values are not translated.
