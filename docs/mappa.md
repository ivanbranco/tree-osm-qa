# The map

How trees are drawn, the filters, the keyboard shortcuts, the address parameters and how the language works. The card of a tree and the tabs of the panel have pages of their own: [The tree card](scheda.md) and [The panel tabs](pannello.md).

## Rendering

The maximum zoom is 19, the last level of the openstreetmap.org tiles: beyond that, the background would only be enlarged and blurred.

* **Crown**: a circle with the real size from `diameter_crown=*` (like every circle on the map, trunks included, it is scaled at the tree's latitude, so it matches buildings and roads of the base map); if missing, a fallback radius (3 m; 5 m for `denotation=natural_monument` and `landmark` and for the members of a [homogeneous group](#homogeneous-groups-of-the-masaf-list) of the MASAF list, 2.5 m for `agricultural` and `orchard`, 2 m for `parking` and `decoration`), varied by ±15% at random but always the same for the same tree, so that the map is not uniform (except for monumental trees and landmarks, which stay at 5 m), and a more transparent fill. The crown is not estimated from circumference or height: the estimates tried (median crown/circumference ratio, UrbanEye3D method) turned out too large. The shape depends on `leaf_type=*` (if missing or invalid, it is estimated from the OSM wiki lists: from the species or, if that is not there, from the genus):
  * round if `leaf_type` is missing (or has another value, such as `leafless`)
  * rounded, lobed, if `broadleaved`
  * 8, 9 or 10 lance-shaped fronds with deep notches between them, if the tree is a palm ([issue #55](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/55)); the number of fronds, the rotation and the length of each frond (±8%) are fixed for each tree, and the crown area stays the same as the circle. The whole circle of the crown stays clickable, including the notches. A tree is a palm with `leaf_type=palm`, with `taxon:family=Arecaceae`, `family=Arecaceae`, `taxon=Arecaceae` or `genus=Arecaceae`, with `taxon:en=Palm`, with `crown_type=I`, or when its genus (from `genus=*`, or the first word of `species=*` or `taxon=*`) is in the list of palm genera (`PALM_GENERA` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`: *Phoenix*, *Washingtonia*, *Trachycarpus*, *Chamaerops*…). These take precedence over `leaf_type=broadleaved` or `needleleaved`: a tree with `taxon:family=Arecaceae` and `leaf_type=broadleaved` is drawn as a palm (`leaf_type=leafless` is left as it is). Palms have no seasons, whatever their `leaf_cycle`. The legend has an entry of its own for palms, separate from the broadleaved one
  * a dense star (14 short points, rotated differently for each tree) if `needleleaved`; the crown area stays the same as the circle. The shape for needleleaved trees can be voted on in [issue #5](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/5), whether the issue is open or closed: the most voted rendering is the one applied. If more than one rendering gets many votes, they can be applied together, each to a different share of the trees. Proposals to use a specific rendering for a specific species are also welcome
* **Trunk**: a brown circle with a radius derived from `circumference=*` (or from `diameter=*`). If the tag is missing, the circumference is estimated (trunk drawn lighter) from `height=*` or from `diameter_crown=*`, using the species ratios in `scripts/species_limits.json` (`height_circ_ratio`, `crown_circ_ratio`) or, for species without a ratio, the general medians of `_default`: for the height, the height/circumference ratio of the tree's height band (15.4 below 6 m, 13.2 from 6 to 8 m, 11.1 from 8 to 15 m, 11.3 from 15 to 20 m, 12.0 from 20 to 30 m, 11.4 from 30 m; the lower bound belongs to the band), then the single value 12; for the crown, crown/circumference 7.03. Four species (Aesculus hippocastanum, Carpinus betulus, Gleditsia triacanthos, Liriodendron tulipifera) have height bands of their own, used before their single ratio, and five (Acer platanoides, Acer pseudoplatanus, Celtis australis, Salix alba, Tilia cordata) use the general bands before their single ratio. The bands are explained in [Data and updates](dati.md#height-bands-for-the-estimated-trunk). The estimates are used only for rendering and do not enter the QA checks. The trunk is always drawn at the size indicated by the tags, even if it exceeds the crown. In that case, if the tree has errors (usually a wrong measurement, such as a circumference in centimetres), the red outline goes on the trunk, which would otherwise cover the crown and its outline. The trunk is also clickable and opens the card. The trunk of the Himalayan birch (`species=Betula utilis`, also with a variety, such as `var. jacquemontii`) is white with a thin grey border, like its bark; that of the paperbark maple (`species=Acer griseum`) is red-orange, like its glossy bark. There is also a surprise for the rainbow eucalyptus (`species=Eucalyptus deglupta`).
* **Multiple trunks** (`multi_stemmed=*`, from zoom 16): instead of the single trunk, several trunks arranged in a circle around the centre. With `yes` (or a non-numeric value) there are 2, with an integer (`multi_stemmed=3`) that number, and they divide the trunk area; with several circumferences (`multi_stemmed=0.75;0.57;0.50`) each trunk has its own measurement. Without a circumference (and without trunk measurements) nothing is drawn, as with the single trunk.
* **Ivy** (`ivyclad=yes`, from zoom 16): a dotted green ring around the trunk, which stays brown; with multiple trunks, a ring around each one.
* **Christmas trees** (`xmas:feature=tree`, from zoom 16, only from 8 December to 6 January in the viewer's date): 8, 11 or 14 coloured lights (by crown size) and a yellow star in the centre; fruits are not drawn. The crown follows `leaf_type=*` (or its estimate); without it, it is drawn as a needleleaved one. A Christmas tree tagged as deciduous follows the seasons like any other tree (bare in December); lights and star stay. Christmas trees that are not `natural=tree` (or `natural=tree_stump`) are also shown, with the crown from `diameter_crown=*` or the fallback radius: they get no checks, are not counted and do not appear in filters or downloads; without a name, their info panel is titled "Christmas tree". The lights switch on and off at random every 0.6 s; when they are off they stay dimmed. With "reduce motion" enabled in the operating system or browser (`prefers-reduced-motion`) they stay on. The legend entry appears only in that period. Adding `xmas=1` to the address (`#xmas=1&region=…`) shows them outside the period too.
* **Fruit trees** (from zoom 16): 5, 6 or 7 coloured dots on the crown according to its size, in fixed positions for each tree (from the id). They apply to `species=Malus domestica` (apples, red), `Juglans regia` (walnuts, light green like the husk, with a dark border to set them apart from the crown), `Citrus limon` (lemons, yellow) and `Citrus sinensis` and `Citrus × aurantium` (oranges and bitter oranges, orange), `Olea europaea` (olives, dark purple) and `Diospyros kaki` (persimmons, reddish orange), also with author, cultivar or the form with `×` (`Citrus × limon`); not whole genera, because many *Malus* and *Prunus* are ornamental. Fruits are visible only in their season, according to the date of whoever is looking at the map: apples August–October, walnuts July–October, oranges November–April, bitter oranges November–March, olives and persimmons October–December, lemons all year (northern hemisphere; south of the equator the months shift by 6). The species, colour and months of the fruits are in `FRUIT_SPECIES` in `scripts/fetch_trees.py`. A click on the text of the legend entry (not on the icon, which shows the tags) switches from "Fruits (seasonal)" to "Fruits (always)", which shows them all year, then to "Fruits (hidden)" and back to "Fruits (seasonal)"; the choice is saved in the browser (the entry is struck through when hidden).
* **Stumps** (`natural=tree_stump`): a brown X, as large as the circumference (`circumference=*` or `diameter=*`); if missing, a radius of 0.25 m and a lighter X. They appear from zoom 10, with a red X if the stump has errors; they follow the same checks as trees.
* **Crown colour**: from `leaf_cycle=*` (or, if missing, estimated from the wiki lists like `leaf_type`): light yellow-green for deciduous, dark green for evergreen, medium green if unknown.
  * **Seasons** ([issue #51](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/51)): deciduous trees (`leaf_cycle=deciduous` or `semi_deciduous`, written or estimated; the estimate counts like the tag) change with the date of whoever is looking at the map. There are three phases:
    * **leaves**, April to September: the usual rendering
    * **autumn**, October and November: the crown takes one of four autumn tones (yellow, orange, red-brown, brown), fixed for each tree (from the id). Species with an autumn colour of their own (see Species colours) use it instead
    * **bare**, December to March: from zoom 16 only the outline of the crown is drawn, in a grey-brown twig colour; the whole crown stays clickable. Below zoom 16 the circle is sand-coloured and more transparent. Trunk, fruits in season (persimmons in December), Christmas lights and the outlines for errors, monumental, sacred and landmark trees stay as they are

    Broadleaved trees (`leaf_type=broadleaved`, or estimated) go through all three phases. Deciduous needleleaved trees are bare in the bare phase, with their star shape, but in autumn only the species with a colour of their own change (`Larix decidua`, `Taxodium distichum`, `Metasequoia glyptostroboides`). Evergreen trees (`semi_evergreen` included), trees with an unknown leaf cycle, palms and stumps do not change. Trees with several species (`;`) or without a species follow the default calendar.

    The change of phase is gradual: each tree is early or late by a fixed number of days, up to 10 (from the id), so around the first day of a phase some trees are already in it and others not yet. South of the equator the months shift by 6. Species with a colour of their own follow their own months, with no early or late days: `Ginkgo biloba` is green in October, yellow in November and bare from 1 December; `Elaeagnus angustifolia` stays silver until the end of October. A region can have its own calendar (`seasons` in `scripts/regions.json`, see [Data and updates](dati.md)); none has one for now, and the monumental trees map always uses the default one, also for the trees of the MASAF list.

    The "Deciduous" legend entry is the switch: a click on its text turns the seasons off or on, and the choice is saved in the browser. With the seasons on the entry reads "Deciduous (seasons)" and its icon is split in three (leaves top left, autumn top right, bare at the bottom); with the seasons off the word "seasons" is struck through and greyed out, the icon is the plain one and deciduous trees keep their leaves all year. Adding `season=leaves`, `season=autumn` or `season=bare` to the address (`#season=bare&region=…`) forces that phase for all the trees that can have it, whatever the date; it stays in the address while moving the map. The calendar, the tones and the number of days are in `SEASONS_DEFAULT`, `AUTUMN_TONES` and `SEASON_SHIFT` in `index.html`; the seasons do not depend on the data, so they need no region update.
  * **Species colours**: some species with a characteristic, documented foliage colour have that colour instead of the `leaf_cycle` one, in the months when it shows (according to the date of whoever is looking at the map; south of the equator the months shift by 6):
    * autumn, October and November: red for `species=Quercus rubra` (rust red), `Liquidambar styraciflua` (crimson), `Nyssa sylvatica` (scarlet) and `Acer rubrum`; golden yellow for `Larix decidua`; russet for `Taxodium distichum` and `Metasequoia glyptostroboides`; golden yellow for `Ginkgo biloba`, in November only. The autumn colours are part of the seasons: they need both the seasons and the species colours on. With the seasons on and the species colours off these trees take a default autumn tone (the needleleaved ones the `leaf_cycle` colour); with the seasons off they are green
    * silver for `Elaeagnus angustifolia`, from April to October (when it has leaves)
    * blue-grey for `Picea pungens` and silver-blue for `Eucalyptus cinerea` and `Eucalyptus pulverulenta`, all year

    The species also match with author or cultivar; species whose colour belongs only to some cultivars (such as purple beech) are not included. These crowns are a little less transparent than the others, so that the colour stays recognisable over the green of parks. A click on the text of the legend entry switches from "Species colours (on)" to "Species colours (off)", where the crowns no longer take the colour of the species (the seasons stay as they are), and back; the choice is saved in the browser. Adding `colors=1` to the address (`#colors=1&region=…`) shows the seasonal colours outside their months too. The species, colours and months are in `SPECIES_COLORS` in `index.html`; the colour does not depend on the data, so it needs no region update.
* **Crown outline**, in order of precedence: red for trees with errors, yellow for `denotation=natural_monument` (monumental trees are drawn above the others), light blue for sacred trees (`ref:zapis=*`, the Serbian zapis, or `place_of_worship=sacred_tree` or `sacred_tree=*` other than `no`, like the rag trees of Ireland and Scotland, or `religion=shinto`, the Japanese goshinboku according to the [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:religion%3Dshinto)), purple for `denotation=landmark`. A tree that is both monumental and sacred, without errors, has from zoom 16 an outline split in half: yellow on the left and light blue on the right (below zoom 16 it stays yellow). With multiple values (`natural_monument;landmark`) each counts, as in the script: that tree is monumental. The members of a [homogeneous group](#homogeneous-groups-of-the-masaf-list) of the MASAF list count as `denotation=natural_monument`. On the card of sacred trees "Sacred tree" appears, with the type and the link to Wikipedia: "zapis" if there is `ref:zapis` (the number is not shown: it comes from an unofficial list), "rag tree" with `sacred_tree=rag_tree` (link to the article on clootie wells, because rag trees do not have an article of their own), "goshinboku" with `religion=shinto` (article "Shinboku").
* **Zoom**: trees with errors are visible at every zoom; from zoom 9 monumental trees are added (members of homogeneous groups included); from zoom 10 all trees (as circles); from zoom 16 the shapes and the trunk appear.

### Homogeneous groups of the MASAF list

The MASAF list of monumental trees of Italy sometimes lists a homogeneous group ("insieme omogeneo"): two or more trees with a single code. The [wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/IT:Key:ref:masaf) maps it with a relation `type=site` + `ref:masaf=*` + `natural=tree_group` + `denotation=natural_monument` whose members are the trees ([issue #9](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/9)); species, circumference and height are on the single trees, which have another `denotation` (`park`, `urban`…), because the group is what is monumental. In the Italian regions, a `natural=tree` node that is a member of at least one relation with `ref:masaf` counts as a tree with `denotation=natural_monument`, in both maps: yellow outline (after the red of errors), drawn above the other trees, visible from zoom 9, the 5 m fallback crown, hidden by "Only errors" like the other monumental trees; with `denotation=landmark` too it is monumental, and if it is also sacred it gets the "monumental and sacred" outline. `ref:masaf` on the relation is enough: `type=site`, `natural=tree_group` and `denotation=natural_monument` may be missing. Only relations with `ref:masaf` count, not those with `denotation=natural_monument` alone. Members that are not tree nodes (ways, other relations, nodes without `natural=tree`) are ignored, and a member stump stays a stump. When the members of a relation fall in two regions, each tree is handled in its own region. The card has a line about the group (see [The tree card](scheda.md)). The checks are described in [QA checks](check.md); the membership is not an error and adds no check.

The relations are read at every update of a region (see [Data and updates](dati.md)), so the changes arrive with the next update of the region and, for the monumental trees map, of that map.

## Filters and regions

* Filters by genus/species and by error type: both lists are built from the trees in view, with the count. With no region loaded there are no trees to filter, so both menus are greyed out and cannot be opened ([issue #20](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/20)). If the loaded region has no trees with errors (in the whole region, not only in view), the error menu shows "No errors" and is greyed out too, and the ⤓ download button is hidden; an error chosen in another region is cleared. In the genus/species filter, "Unknown" shows trees with neither `genus` nor `species`, "With species" those with `species=*` (any value). The leaf button next to the genus/species menu opens, in a new tab, the list of the species pages (`species/index.html`: search by scientific or common name, order by name or by number of trees) or, when the species chosen in the menu has a page, that page; it also works with no region loaded and in the monumental trees map. Errors are grouped by theme, as in [QA checks](check.md#qa-checks) (measurements, species and genus name, Wikidata, links, sources and monumental trees, other tags); the first entry of each group ("All "…" errors") shows trees with at least one error of that group (omitted if the group has only one error type in view). From zoom 10, when trees without errors appear too, the error menu has "Errors only" below "All errors": it hides every tree without errors (monumental trees and MASAF trees not on OSM included) and leaves the trees and stumps with at least one error. "Edited in the last 30 days", at every zoom when at least one such tree is in view, shows the trees and stumps with at least one error whose node was last edited on OSM in the 30 days before today (the date of the last edit is the one in the data of the region, so edits made after its latest update are not counted); it appears in each region after its first update with this date. With a region loaded, the ⤓ button next to the menu downloads a GeoJSON file with all the trees of the region (not only those in view) with the chosen error (or, with "All errors", with at least one error) and, if set, of the chosen genus/species: for each tree the link to the OSM node, the codes and description of the errors, the main tags and the day of the last edit (`last_edit`). The file opens in JOSM, QGIS, uMap and similar. To see only monumental trees, just stay between zoom 9 and 10.
* Requesting new regions: the "+ Request a new region…" entry in the menu opens a [GitHub issue](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/new?template=region.yml); in the overview (below zoom 10) a click outside the covered regions opens a popup with two links, a GitHub issue and a message on OpenStreetMap, with the name of the country asked from Nominatim (nothing appears on the sea). In a country covered only in part (the United States) the popup shows the name of the state, and does not appear in the states already covered. The same invitation, with the name of the region, appears when clicking a region drawn on the map but still without data (in grey). The same GitHub issue can also be used to ask for the update of an existing region
* Old data ([issue #59](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/59)): below the count, "Data updated on …" gives the date of the latest update of the loaded region. When that date is 7 or more days old (`OLD_DAYS` in `index.html`), it is followed by an orange "⚠ N days ago" and a small icon that opens the GitHub issue for requesting an update, already filled in with "Update of an existing region" and the region name. Not on the monumental trees map

The tabs of the panel (Legend, Error ranking, Trends, Completeness, Record trees) are described in [The panel tabs](pannello.md), the card of a tree in [The tree card](scheda.md).

## Keyboard shortcuts

| Key | When | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Z** | with a card open | centres the map on the tree at zoom 19 (useful after clicking it from far away) |
| **C** | with a card open | opens the [trunk comparison](scheda.md#trunk-comparison) window, if the tree has it |
| **B** | always | switches between the OpenStreetMap background and the grayscale one (same as the button under + and −) |
| **J** | with a region open, from zoom 17 | passes to JOSM the fixes of the trees in view, up to 25 (see [All the trees in view](scheda.md#all-the-trees-in-view)); if no tree in view has a fix, it offers to download the area in view in JOSM anyway |
| **E** (expert) | with a region open, from zoom 10 | shows or hides a dashed red ring around the trees in view that have at least one hint (ⓘ in the card) and no errors, up to 5,000 (the nearest to the centre); it follows the genus/species filter. Not shown in the page |

They do nothing while typing in a field; C, B and J also do nothing with a window open.

The **B** key ("Background", [issue #72](https://github.com/ivanbranco/tree-osm-qa/issues/72)), or the small button with two stacked sheets under + and − (its top sheet is filled with grey while the grayscale background is on), switches the background between the standard OpenStreetMap tiles (the default) and the grayscale [Positron](https://openfreemap.org/quick_start/) style of OpenFreeMap (vector tiles), on which the colours of the trees stand out. The Positron style is downloaded only the first time the key is pressed; its layers are placed under those of the trees, and each background shows its own credit only while it is visible. The choice is not saved: on reload the map starts with the OpenStreetMap tiles. It works on both maps (regions and monumental trees).

Other keys:

* **Enter** or **Space** on a legend row highlights it (or removes the highlight), on a row of Record trees opens it, on ♂ / ♀ and on the photo pop-ups opens them; **Esc** closes a pop-up or the comparison window
* From MapLibre: the arrow keys move the map, **+** and **−** zoom in and out. Rotation is disabled

## Address parameters

Parameters go after `#` in the address, joined by `&` (for example `#region=lombardia&season=autumn&lang=en`). Details are in the sections linked in the table.

| Parameter | What it does | Kept when the map moves |
|---|---|---|
| `region=<slug>` | opens that region (the slugs are in `scripts/regions.json`), see [Data and updates](dati.md) | yes |
| `node=<id>` | opens the card of that OSM node; without `map`, centres it ([The tree card](scheda.md)). Also works without `region` | while the card is open |
| `map=<zoom>/<lat>/<lon>` | map position, e.g. `map=17.00/45.46420/9.18990` | yes (updated) |
| `lang=it` / `lang=en` | forces the language ([Language](#language)) | yes |
| `season=leaves` / `autumn` / `bare` | forces that seasonal phase for all the trees that can have it, whatever the date; `bare` also shows the winter view of the Wikidata photo on the card ([Rendering](#rendering)) | yes |
| `colors=1` | shows the seasonal species colours outside their months ([Rendering](#rendering)) | no |
| `xmas=1` | shows the Christmas trees outside 8 December – 6 January ([Rendering](#rendering)) | no |

The address contains the region and the map position (`#region=lombardia&map=17.00/45.46420/9.18990`): copying it shares the exact point. With a tree's card open it also contains its OSM node (`#region=molise&node=6311161280&map=…`): the link reopens the card, and without `map` it centres the tree. If the node is not in the region's data, the region is shown with a note under the count.

`#node=<id>` alone (e.g. `#node=13798129808`) also works: the page asks the OpenStreetMap API for the node's current position, opens the region that contains it and adds `region` to the address. Near a border the neighbouring regions are tried too. If the position is not available, or it is outside the regions on the map, a note under the count says so; if the node is not in the region's data, the map is centred on its position.

`colors=1` and `xmas=1` are read when the page opens: they disappear from the address as soon as the map moves, but their effect stays until the page is reloaded. Changing `region`, `node` or `map` by hand in the address bar takes effect without reloading; changing `lang` reloads the page. On the monumental trees map `region` is not used.

## Language

The site is in Italian if the browser language is Italian, otherwise in English. It is changed with IT · EN at the top of the panel: the choice is saved in the browser. It can also be forced from the address, for example `#lang=en` or `#region=lombardia&lang=en`.

All texts are in the `I18N` object at the start of the script in `index.html`: to add a language, copy one of the two blocks, translate it and add the code to `LANGS`. Region names, OSM tags and values are not translated.
